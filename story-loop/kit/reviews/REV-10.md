## Load-bearing claim and steelman
On the spine I named Friday at the bowl as the claim. On the page Friday holds (see What survived), and the weight has moved to what Friday stands on.

**The sentence the story cannot survive losing** is the one it holds back until Thursday: "The lights in Mytholmroyd, the heater roaring, the head torch still round his neck: You wanted me to have to come and get you."

**The claim** the reader must accept is that nothing can check that sentence:
- Not eleven runs. Her telling of that day was shaped by the sentence itself, so the runs give "a different man in the van" every night, and she "could not tell which of the four was hers".
- Not memory.
- Not asking. Any answer would be one more father, and the house's language is silence.

Everything grows round it: "someone nobody need fetch", the test at four, "You did it because—", the shape that rises at six, the question not asked at the bowl.

**The steelman.** The audit fails not because the machine is weak but because its only witness is the girl the sentence made. Then the week gives her the one audit no run can:
- At four on Thursday she leaves two places empty and lies with her palm on the wall.
- She hears him begin the sentence on Kit and stop: "No. I don't. Why did you?"
- At six the same shape rises in her for Kit, and she hears whose wish is in it. She has audited her father the only way open to her, by doing it to her brother.
- On Friday she makes her first decision for a child. She puts his counsel in her own ear, hears nothing, and carries it up.
- That night he whispers through the wall in her whisper, and she cannot hear the answers. The question goes one generation down, unasked.

The page does all of this. Two things on the page threaten the claim at its root:
1. **The world's rules give the machine the witness the claim says it lacks.** Outside the house everyone wears a bud, and a bud hears what is near it, even from the bowl. So at thirteen, in the van, it was in her ear (REV-10 F1).
2. **The draft finishes the one working audit in narration.** It states the bowl's purpose, "so that his children would have to come to him", and then gives "the reason" at the bowl as a pointer back to it. The protagonist answers "cannot be checked" on the page, with a "perhaps", and the turn then cites that answer (REV-10 F2).

Neither breaks the claim. Each makes it smaller than it is.

## Findings
F1: MINOR — The audit cannot hear the lights, and the draft never says why. This is a missing mechanism.

The story's whole undecidable rests on the machine knowing that day only from her ("She had told it everything she knew about that day"; three general reasons, "different ones each night"). But the draft's own rules put a listening bud in her ear in the van:
- "outside the house you wore it, like everyone";
- a bud hears what is near it even from the bowl ("because it had heard the keys from the bowl"; "with nothing to do but hear the door and the keys");
- it comments on what it overhears ("He's found it").

The moor and the van at Mytholmroyd are outside the house. A reader who knows the record-and-replay stories (Chiang's "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling"; Black Mirror's "The Entire History of You") will ask at the Thursday reveal why she does not simply replay it. The page has no answer, so the reader must supply one: either she had taken it out, or buds keep nothing. The story's distance from both works, and the one thing only it does, rest on that sentence never having been heard by a machine.

One clause on the moor or in the van fixes it. Two ways, with different costs:
- She had taken it out herself. This leans on one of tonight's three reasons ("she had wanted to be on her own") and so partly settles her motive.
- His hand took it, as on the first night. This keeps the motive open and makes the lights the second time he took it.

F2: MINOR — At six on Thursday the draft says its discovery aloud, and the turn then leans on the saying.
- **The best moment.** "The sentence rose in her, in her father's shape... she heard whose wish was in it: hers... and so perhaps his, at the lights" is the best moment in the story.
- **The purpose clause.** The clause after it is "the wish of a man who had put every bud in the house in a bowl so that his children would have to come to him". It states the bowl's purpose as a fact about the man. The "perhaps" hedges whose wish it was, not the purpose clause. It is the one thing about Rob the story otherwise lets the reader earn, from the week the mother went and "He had not come to her door".
- **The pointer at the bowl.** The withheld question is explained by "for the reason she had given Kit no sentence at six". That sends the reader back for a reason the six o'clock paragraph implies but never states.
- **Friday night.** The decision there closes on "Going down would make Friday hers", which the reader has to decode at the moment the turn's meaning is fixed.

Steelman: the insight rises "in her father's shape", so it is one more father, and it is hedged. That holds for "perhaps his". It does not hold for the purpose clause, or for the two pointers, which treat the insight as settled. The cost is that the reader is handed, inside Tess's head, a reading of Rob that the story's method says no one can reach. Friday then drifts toward correction: she will not make Kit have to come to anyone.

The fix:
- Cut the purpose clause and let "and so perhaps his, at the lights" stand.
- Give the reader something to see at the bowl and at the stairs in place of a cross-reference.

F3: MINOR — The curve's scope contradicts the audit's. The audit is "Forty-one entries, each an occasion on which her father had decided something about her". But the curve puts "her years down in the bottom left and Kit's up in the top right", and the bud explains it by "a second child being easier than the first". If every entry is about her, Kit has no points on the curve. The curve is the story's one picture of "the child her father learned on", so a reader who tries to see it hits the contradiction two paragraphs after the definition. Either the audit takes in decisions about Kit, or the corners are named by her own ages.

F4: MINOR — "He would not have." The second-to-last sentence is the gloss REV-07's sweep asked to keep off the page. It turns the last image into a verdict on Rob's character: he is the man who does not check, set against the daughter who audited him. That leans the close toward the noble refuser at the very moment the ear goes red. "Her father did not check it. He was looking at the rim of Kit's ear, which was going red" does all the work without it.

F5: MINOR — The narration walks in the audit's own gait.
- Every remembered entry closes on its grade and a short sentence of her going along with it:
  - the party: "Every run graded it worse than the bud, and the bud said why in the same words every time, and she had never once argued with it";
  - Halifax: "Every run graded that drive comparable to typical advice. She had not asked it twice";
  - the moor: run four, run nine, run ten, tonight's.
- Every habit arrives dated to its wound: "since she was" small, nine, fourteen, fifteen, eight and thirteen; "since August" three times; "since he was one".

By the Halifax drive a reader hears the cadence before the content.

The party is weakened by its own grading, which is the residue of REV-07 F3. It is still scored twice. And in the same breath as "Any scoring of a learner draws it", the bud's "the earliest entries follow a change in the household" excuses it before it is shown.

Steelman: the narration is her ledger, and the form is the point. Keep the form, but break the cadence at least once. The party's cost already carries its case, so the party can lose its score.

F6: MINOR — "She could do that sum." Tess was eight the week the bowl began and her mother went to Manchester. She has always held both halves, so the "sum" reads as a reveal staged for the reader, not a thought of hers. The narration then lays out the terms ("He had been one, and she had been eight, and the bowl had started the same week") one paragraph after the purpose clause has already given the answer. The trace works without the announcement: Kit gives the Manchester times "as if she had asked", and she "did not ask him what the times the other way were for".

F7: NIT — "He did not talk in the van; he never had." But the lights happened in the van ("the van afterwards with the heater roaring... where he had turned in his seat and told her"). If the absolute is meant to mark the lights as the one exception, it has to say so. A reader who counts will stop here.

F8: NIT — "Rob went up." This is the only place the narration calls him Rob. Everywhere else he is "her father" or "their father".

F9: NIT — At four on Thursday: "went back up with hers still in... She went back up to see what her father would do." That is two "went back up"s in consecutive sentences, and the second carries the motive.

F10: NIT — "Not for a twelfth run; she knew what that would give her" rebuts a reading the reader has not formed. The clause after it ("it was another man in the van with the heater on") is the good half.
