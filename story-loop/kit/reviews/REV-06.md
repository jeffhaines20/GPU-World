# REV-06 — depth-critic, M2, the spine of story A, "Eleven Fathers", second round (2026-09-26)

## Reading

1. **What the story is about.** It is about whether you can know the person who made you without spending someone. The machine gives Tess eleven reasonable fathers and never the real one. The only real one she gets, heard through a wall, she gets by doing to her brother what she came to bill her father for. That is not the plot. The plot is a week of nightly audit, a scene heard through a wall and a bud carried upstairs; this is what the plot is for, and the beats carry it:
   - "The only thing no rerun can vary is a live case, and the only live case in the house is Kit" (Obstacle).
   - "to see what her father does" (beat 3).
   - The father she hears at five ("No," her father said. "I don't. Why did you?") is not one any run produced.

   Under it runs a second idea, about being told what you are: "the one time he told her what she was" (beat 1), "Six years she had lived inside the rest of that sentence" (passage), "Ask it. It'll give you three" (beat 5).

2. **Is the question real?** Yes. A thoughtful person can hold either answer, and the spine gives each side its evidence.
   - **The gift:** the limb's line Tess sees "because she learned to see it from him and the thing in the client's ear did not", and the silence on the drive from Halifax.
   - **The charge:** the party, which "every run scores ... worse than the bud would have done", and the curve with her years "bottom left".
   - **The lights carry both at once.** "You wanted me to have to come and get you" can be a true reading of a thirteen-year-old. It can also be the wish of a man who put every bud in the house in a bowl so that his children would have to come to him. The spine closes neither reading.

   The story also turns an assumption over instead of repeating one. The hedge, usually the model's failing, is what Tess has wanted since thirteen. The committed judgment that the world's question calls "their own" is what made her. A reader can hold the reverse: the true sentence was the gift, and three reasons is never being known.

   **Where the text decides:** once, by an act and not a verdict. On Friday Tess chooses the machine's three over the father's one for Kit, and its price is on Kit's ear by Saturday. That is where a story should decide, and it does not turn the question into a slogan.

   **Where it leans rather than decides:** Rob acting well is always a scene in the present, while Rob acting badly is a clause of memory (F6). And the refusal of a total rests, as written, on the runs disagreeing as a whole (F1).

   None of the findings below disagrees with the idea, and none rises to MAJOR. Each is about whether the text earns what the spine already has.

3. **Earned or announced.** Mostly earned. The strangeness changes what people do in four places:
   - **The live case.** One model for everyone means Tess's only second opinion is a rerun. So the only fresh evidence is a live case, and she leaves Kit to be found (beat 3). The one voice that is not the bud in another ear is the man being audited, and she does not ask him (beat 5).
   - **The test at the bowl.** A bud is silent in any ear but its owner's. So she can find Kit's only by putting her own in her ear in front of her father, which makes the test and the disclosure one act (the turn).
   - **The red rim.** It is how a man without a bud knows who has been using one ("Your sister's goes red").
   - **The client.** He relays his bud's felling plan, and Tess sees the line from Rob instead.

   The spine's thesis, that her judgment "was made in the house she is auditing", is also used rather than said. At six the sentence from the lights rises in her "in her father's form", an hour after she has heard him stop it. Her restraint is his, an hour old.

   The idea is explained, rather than used, in two places:
   - The bud's caveat in beat 1 ("any scoring of a learner draws that shape, and it cannot tell learning from luck or an easier second child"). It is in character for a thing that hedges, and it can stay if it takes one breath.
   - "It was the silence." at the end of beat 2, which is the narration's own (F7).

4. **Do the people behave like people?** Rob and Kit do.
   - **Rob.** His "Right." on Friday gives up an eleven-year rule the evening after "Give it here". It is believable because the spine has built him out of withheld words: the drive from Halifax, the door not visited since August, the sentence stopped at five. Anything more would also end a pretence both of them are keeping.
   - **Kit.** "Did you see mine was gone?" is a twelve-year-old's question, not the story's.
   - **Tess, Thursday at four.** This is where the plot shows through. The story needs a live case, and Tess supplies one by leaving her twelve-year-old brother to be found. Nothing earlier in the spine shows what Kit is to her. She was eight, in a house the mother had just left, with a father up at five, when he was one. The reader has nothing to weigh the act against, so "to see what her father does" reads as the plot's reason in her mouth.

   The spine also leaves unused something its own passage already has. Her bud is in her ear at five, so her place in the bowl is empty too. He goes to Kit's door when he had grounds, that morning, to come to hers (F3).

5. **What the ending costs.** Real things, to all three.
   - **Rob:** his boy's nights now have a listener he cannot hear. On Saturday he is reading it on Kit's ear, as he read hers.
   - **Kit:** a red ear already, and a voice in his nights whose effect nobody can price, on the child the curve says got the best of his father. That is Tess's own departure at a risk she cannot price: the world's question turned on her. She has also put in his hand the instrument that can audit her ("she cannot hear what it says back").
   - **Tess:** the one question only Rob could answer, carried out of the house unasked, and her leaving unmended, "fifty minutes early for a walk of ten".

   What costs nothing is the item the spine names first: "the bill she came for, since the last night she could have run it went to Kit". From Saturday her bud is in her ear for good (F4). And "will never be told what he was" is listed as a cost but reads as Kit's gain (F8).

6. **Would a reader be different afterwards?** Yes, in one small way, if the draft keeps what the spine has. A reader could carry out this thought: when every second opinion is the same machine in another ear, the only new thing you can learn about a person is what they do when something happens to someone, and that someone is always near and never asked.

   That thought is the story's own. The others it carries are older, and true: the child made by the parent cannot judge the parent from outside, and the harm and the gift are one thing.

   For the new thought to be what the reader takes away, two things must hold. It has to be staged before Thursday (F2). And the eleven fathers have to read as the fathers her entries permit, not as a machine that cannot make up its mind (F1). Otherwise the reader leaves with a complaint about an unreliable tool.

## Findings

F1: MINOR — The spine rests the audit's refusal on the runs disagreeing as a whole ("kinder than last night's", beat 1; "the gap between runs wider than any entry", Obstacle; "the form of the refusal", the Question), which a reader who knows these tools will take for noise to be averaged, a faulty instrument rather than an undecidable man. The spine already has the better design (the party worse in every run, the curve in all eleven): let the swing between nights come only from the lights and the silence, so that the eleven fathers are the ones her entries permit.
F2: MINOR — "Every second opinion is the same bud in another ear", the spine's freshest idea and the reason Thursday is method rather than curiosity, lives only in the Obstacle, and no beat stages it. The beech (beat 2) can stage it without a new scene: the client's bud misses the line and the wire as hers would have, and Tess can know it is hers in another ear.
F3: MINOR — Thursday at four (beat 3) is where Tess does what the plot needs, leaving her brother to be found, with nothing earlier in the spine to show what Kit is to her. Give her one ordinary act of care for him before it, and state what the passage already implies (her bud is in her ear at five, so her place in the bowl is empty too), so that she lets them both be found and he goes to Kit's door with grounds to come to hers.
F4: MINOR — Friday's price as the spine names it, "the last night she could have run it" (Ending image; "the kindness reading"), will not be credited: from Saturday her bud is in her ear for good, and eleven runs have shown a twelfth would settle nothing. The price the story actually has is the night before Leeds spent without the bud she has used every night since August, by the girl who was sick in the van on the way to a party that had started without her, and the question only Rob can answer; name those, or one of Friday's two guards against the kindness reading is soft.
F5: MINOR — The curve's rival explanations in beat 1 (learning, luck, an easier second child) leave out the one a reader supplies first from the Who: Tess was eight and Kit one the week the buds went into the bowl and their mother went. A bud described as competent that does not say so looks steered toward a father-audit; let it say so, obliquely if the mother must stay one clause, and let Tess pass over it, so that the omission is hers.
F6: MINOR — Rob acting well is always a scene in the present (the beech, the stopped sentence, "Your sister's goes red", the door, the look at Kit's ear), and Rob acting badly is only a clause of memory (the party, inside beat 2) or a graph (beat 1), so a reader will total the eleven years as a gift, the one total the Question says the story refuses. Give the party a scene's weight, or let the lights' other reading show: the sentence as the wish of a man who put every bud in a bowl so that his children would have to come to him.
F7: MINOR — "It was the silence." (end of beat 2) glosses the Halifax drive, and the gloss states the story's most familiar idea, that the machine cannot score what mattered. The passage's register leaves such verdicts to the reader, and "comparable to typical advice" set against the drive says it without one.
F8: NIT — "Kit has the three and will never be told what he was" is listed among the ending's costs (Ending image) but is Kit's gain; the cost is Tess's, in giving away what she wanted and cannot have, and "never" promises what an ending cannot show.
