# REV-05 — adversarial-critic, M2, the spine of story A, "Eleven Fathers" (2026-09-25)

**Verdict:** NEEDS CHANGES (covers the spine as read today)

## The load-bearing claim

**The claim.** The story's turn is Friday at the bowl. In front of Rob, Tess takes Kit's bud out and carries it upstairs, and Rob lets her. The spine says this one act both collects the bill and spends it. It is her own first act on a child, at a risk she cannot price, and it mirrors Rob's eleven years. It must read as payment, not kindness. The story cannot survive losing this. Every cost in the ending is a cost of Friday. The spine itself says that without it Friday is "a sister being nice." Read that way, the reddening rim and Rob's look become a sad-sweet coda to an audit that failed.

**The steelman.** All week Tess asks for a bill from an instrument that can only regrade what she tells it. Thursday gives her the one thing no rerun can vary. Through the wall she hears her father begin, on Kit, the sentence he once finished on her, and stop. That is the father her years bought. In the same minute she learns he has known about her red ear since August and let her be. On Friday she spends what she bought. Taking Kit's bud while Rob watches ends the arrangement that made him the only one who knows his boy, and that arrangement is what she paid for. She does it without a word, as he let her go without one. From then on she is someone who has decided for a child. The one who paid leaves as one who charged someone else, and nobody says so.

**The attack.** The spine has used Friday up before Friday comes. What is left of it is not Tess's.

1. *What the turn changes has already happened.* The turn claims two consequences.
   - "Tess can never again be only the one it was done to." By the spine's own plain sentence, that stopped being true at four on Thursday. She set her bud's plan aside "to see what her father does, and the one it is done on is asleep on the far side of the wall."
   - "No child in that house can again be raised by a man whose words he cannot check." This was already broken. Kit took his own bud on Wednesday night and asked it "Stuff." Tess has been checking her father since August, and he knows.

   Friday makes public what Thursday already made true. A turn that only confirms is not a turn.
2. *What Friday adds belongs to Rob, and it happens where the point of view has sworn not to go.* At five on Thursday Rob says "Give it here" and clicks Kit's bud into the bowl with his own hand. On Friday he watches Tess undo it and lets her. The real hinge is whatever changed in him between those two scenes, and it is off the page. Once Tess is on the 9:14, "never again" is his to keep or reverse.

   There is also a contradiction. "Words he cannot check" names a power that three beats of the story show the bud does not have. It cannot check a father's sentence; it can only multiply it ("It'll give you three"). What Kit actually gets is not having to grow round one sentence. That is the rescue half of the spine's "rescue or spending."
3. *The guard against the kindness reading is on the wrong day.* The spine admits Friday needs a sentence two beats earlier to be read correctly. But that sentence confesses Thursday's motive, not Friday's. Once Thursday is confessed as using Kit, the most available reading of Friday is that she pays him back. A debt settled within a day makes her Rob's opposite, not his mirror.

   And Kit asked for it, by taking the bud himself. So Friday has no payer who could not be asked, and that is the one feature of Rob's eleven years the mirror needs. The only loser who did not choose is Rob, and he agrees by watching.
4. *Tess's cost is invisible, so "rescue or spending" is not left open; the evidence decides it.* On Saturday, Rob's cost and Kit's cost are things you can see. Tess's cost is only a figure of speech: she "leaves without the bill she came for, having spent it." Meanwhile the story has taught the reader to count growing round something as harm: the wire that sparks the saw, the sentence she cannot verify. So Kit's loss reads as a rescue, and Rob's reads as overdue. The spine's own evidence settles the question as rescue. The ending then asks for the weight of a bill it has not earned.

**The nearest real cost is already in the spine, unclaimed.** The passage's last line, "He had not come to her door once since August," turns her nights with the bud into a summons he never answered. When Rob lets her take Kit's bud on Friday, in daylight, it is the same not-coming. That is a cost Tess pays and the reader can see. The spine books it to Rob as consent.

## The sweep

- **An ending that explains itself:** cleared at spine level. The last beat is an image: Kit reading out the 9:14, Rob not checking, the rim of Kit's ear reddening. The gloss "because he cannot and would not," and the paragraph listing what each person lost, must stay in the spine and off the page.
- **A character who exists to be told things:** partly found. Every beat Kit has does a job for Tess's case (F5). The client at the beech exists to relay a plan and be ignored, which is fine for a part that size.
- **A middle that repeats the first act at a higher volume:** found. Beat 2's night is beat 1 again (F6).
- **Strangeness that is decoration:** cleared. The bud's strangeness does structural work. It goes on grading in her palm, the red ear is the evidence Rob had all along, and every second opinion is the same bud in another ear.
- **A theme stated in dialogue:** mostly cleared. Two lines come close.
  - The bud's "asking rather than telling ... good practice" survives. The machine states the lesson and is cut off mid-word, and the cut is the point.
  - Tess's "Ask it. It'll give you three" puts the story's preference (three answers over one sentence) in the protagonist's mouth at the turn. It is a verdict on the form of what Rob said at the lights, if not on whether it was true, and it feeds F1.
- **A first page that is throat-clearing:** cleared. It opens on an act at three in the morning: the boards that do not speak, the bowl as an object. The numbers are held back to page two.
- **A last line reaching for profundity:** cleared, just. The last line is an ear. It is also the neatest planted callback in the spine ("Your sister's goes red"), and neatness is its only risk.
- **A plot that would not change if the science-fictional element were removed:** partly found (F7).

## Findings

F1: MAJOR — The turn is not a turn, and the mechanism that would make it one is missing. One of Friday's two claimed consequences has already happened on Thursday, by the spine's own sentence ("the one it is done on is asleep on the far side of the wall"). The other belongs to Rob. He grants it off the page, somewhere between "Give it here" and Friday evening. And it is described as a power the bud does not have: "words he cannot check," when the story shows the bud can only give three answers. The guard against the kindness reading covers Thursday's motive, not Friday's. Kit asked for the bud by taking it, so Friday has no payer who could not be asked. Tess's only cost is a figure of speech. As the evidence stands, it settles "rescue or spending" as rescue, and the ending image is unearned.

F2: MAJOR — Everything shown on the page argues for Rob, and the spine's named counterweight appears in no beat. Tally the scenes:
- **The beech:** he lays the limb whole where the bud said three pieces.
- **The wire:** neither of them saw it, and only the bud's miss is said aloud.
- **The Halifax drive:** it scores "comparable to typical advice," and "It was the silence."
- **The curve:** it is the one place he sits below the bud, and the bud discredits the finding itself.
- **Thursday:** he stops himself, and the bud's gloss is cut off.
- **Friday and Saturday:** he is all restraint.

The bud is never once right against him in a scene. The spine knows this is the noble-refuser story and names the guard: Rob "was once plainly wrong (the party at nine)." But the party at nine is in no beat. The one concrete charge on the other side, the lights, is built so that nobody can decide it. The story's form is refusing a verdict, so it cannot let the reader keep score. Here the reader's score gives the verdict anyway: Rob was right.

F3: MINOR — The obstacle turns on "to collect the bill is to spend it," and the noun "bill" changes meaning from clause to clause:
- first the account of what the years cost her ("entries she can read");
- then what she bought ("the father who stops mid-sentence for Kit is what she bought");
- then something that can be spent.

You can collect a bill or pay one; you cannot spend one. The rule against lecturing keeps this figure off the page. So the draft must act out a transaction the spine has not defined. The writer needs the plain-words version first, whichever way F1 is fixed.

F4: MINOR — The ending stands at the bowl and does not say whether Tess takes her bud. The last we see of it, it is in the bowl ("leaving her own"), and on Saturday she stands at that bowl with one bag. Taking it rejoins the world's default. Leaving it makes her a departer whose first-year curve her patients will pay for. Either choice answers the story's question for her. If the draft leaves it unsaid, it becomes the ending the spine itself forbids: a pen held over a line. The spine should decide it, even if the prose never states it.

F5: MINOR — Kit is a device. Every beat he has does a job for Tess's case: the empty place in the bowl, being caught, asking to be told, being handed the bud, reading out the train. Even his transgression is learned from her. "Stuff," "Bit" and "Dunno" are a way of talking, not a person. "Something of his own the story never gets" is a promise with nothing behind it. The spine should know what that something is, so the withholding has a shape the reader can feel.

F6: MINOR — Beat 2's night is beat 1 again: the same room, the same whisper, a rerun, a remembered entry, the bud falling short. The escalation, from "the runs disagree" to "their one agreement is an artefact," is an idea, not an event. That makes two nights of the audit failing in the first two beats, with the beech as the only thing that happens between them.

F7: MINOR — The contest's premise does its work only by implication. Swap in a 2025 phone and chatbot and the events all survive: a bowl of devices, a sneaked audit, a confiscation, a handover. Two things survive only in 2040:
- "every second opinion is the same bud in another ear";
- a curve that means something only because the bud has not changed in eleven years.

A father can overtake a fixed target, and only the stop in progress fixes the target. That is the premise earning its place, and nothing on the page points at it.

F8: MINOR — Eleven years ago Kit was one. The reader will do that sum on page two, where "eleven" appears, and look for what happened then: a mother gone, or the buds arriving. The spine gives Rob a motive, but no beginning and no mother. Each possible beginning moves the question. A lost mother leans the reader toward Rob; the buds arriving makes the bowl exactly as old as the buds. The spine should decide which, even if the story gives it one clause.

F9: NIT — Rob's keys go into the bowl beside the buds, presumably every evening for eleven years, so "the scratched one" does not tell Kit's bud from the others. A narrator who notices things the way a night worker does will make the reader notice the keys.

## What survived

- **The audit's failure.** I attacked it the way a technical judge will: variation between reruns is noise, not eleven fathers, so just average the runs. It holds. The curve is that average, the bud discredits it itself, and the deepest cause belongs to the story alone: the payer's testimony was told in the father's words. No number of runs fixes that.
- **The passage.** I attacked "Your sister's goes red" as a convenient reveal. It holds. It is planted by a physical fact about wearing an earpiece. It turns the auditor into the one who was known all along. And it is said to Kit, not to her.
- **Thursday's staging.** Tess goes back up with her bud still in. So at five Rob finds two empty places in the bowl and goes to one door. His choice is legible without a word, and it is better than the spine seems to know.
- **The refusal to settle the lights.** I attacked it twice, and it held both times.
  - With the passage's last line: a girl still trying, at nineteen, to make him come to her door looks like proof that "You wanted me to have to come and get you" was true. It holds, because a girl who grew to fit the sentence would do exactly the same.
  - With "why doesn't she just ask him?" It holds, because his answer would be a twelfth father, and the family's language is silence (the drive from Halifax).
- **"Grew round it."** It is said once, about wire, by the man who says least. The mapping is left unstated: the audit is the saw that sparks on what the tree closed over.
- **Derivation.** I attacked it as Le Guin's "The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas" (a child's cost buys others' good, and someone walks away). I also attacked it as Chiang's "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling" (a father, a daughter, and a machine re-examining what was said between them). It holds against both. Here the harm and the gift are one thing. And the machine cannot settle what was meant, because it sat in the bowl and has only her telling.
- **The bud as not-a-character.** It holds. The bud hedges, offers three answers, grades, and goes on in her palm.

## The sharpest question for the writer

What does handing Kit a shield against the father her years paid for cost Tess that Thursday had not already cost her — a thing a reader can see, not "the bill"?

## The one thing only this story does

It makes the child her father learned on the one person who cannot audit him: not because the machine is weak, but because the only evidence it can grade is her own life, told in his words.
