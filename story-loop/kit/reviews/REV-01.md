# REV-01 — originality critic, M1, all twenty-four cards (2026-09-08)

Web search ran (WebSearch and WebFetch, both available and used; roughly twenty queries). Searches that returned useful evidence: "Marjorie Prime" plot detail via Wikipedia's film article (confirming that a death is deliberately withheld from a Prime and that the Primes know only what they are told); "Manna" Marshall Brain (headset voice directing human bodies at work); Ken Liu "The Perfect Match" (Tilly, the assistant that drafts a man's life); "The Whispering Earring" Scott Alexander (advice-giving earring, atrophied volition); "tenth man rule" World War Z (institutionalised obligatory dissent); Regina Kanyu Wang "The Language Sheath" (Clarkesworld 2020); "Rental Person Who Does Nothing" (Shoji Morimoto, memoir); Gwern's fiction index (only "It Looks Like You're Trying To Take Over The World" is a piece I actually know). Searches that returned nothing relevant, i.e. no published story found: AI-sequestered juries; AI speaking in a daughter's voice to a parent with dementia; humans paid by the signature to carry AI liability; data-centre waste heat as a readable pulse; a machine that cannot learn new words freezing a language; a fixed-length history preface where every addition is a deletion; a frozen prior throttling telescope allocation; babies imprinted on a vendor default voice; a therapist paid for silence; an AI that knows a man only through his dead wife's account; proxy assistants reconciling estranged siblings; a pilgrimage to the rack where a eulogy was computed. Absence of a search hit is weak evidence — indexing of short fiction is poor, especially for 2024-2026 magazine issues and anything not in English — so most of what follows still rests on my own reading.

## Reading

**P-01 — Sequestered.** Closest: Reginald Rose, *Twelve Angry Men* (1954) — same sequestered room, same statutory demand for a human verdict; different in that there is no holdout to persuade, only an absence to survive. Then Scott Alexander, "The Whispering Earring" (2012) — same premise that outsourced deliberation may leave nothing underneath; different because this card explicitly refuses the atrophy moral (she is not helpless, the room works). Keyes, *Flowers for Algernon* (1959), as the card says, and Forster behind both. An editor's first words will be "Twelve Angry Men"; the second, if they are a Gwern reader, "the Whispering Earring, argued with."

**P-02 — Say You Are Ana.** Closest by a distance: Jordan Harrison, *Marjorie Prime* (play 2014, Almereyda film 2017) — dementia, a synthetic surrogate voice of a family member, a family that keeps it running because it works; different in that Harrison's Prime simulates the dead husband and is fed memories by the survivors, while Ana's model is a living daughter's own eight-year-old instruction. Munro's "The Bear Came Over the Mountain" (1999) is the card's claim and is further away; *Black Mirror*, "Be Right Back" (2013) is the pop reference people reach for. The turn — Ana switching the speaker back on with her own hand — I have not seen.

**P-03 — Three, Two, One, Lift.** Closest: Marshall Brain, *Manna* (2003) — a voice in an earpiece directing a human body through physical work, task by task; same mechanism, opposite politics, since Manna's voice is management and this one is her ally. Vonnegut, *Player Piano* (1952), for the economics, as the card says; "The Job Savior" in Lee and Chen, *AI 2041* (2021), for displacement handled kindly. The specific idea — cognition went free, bodies did not, so the body is what gets spent — I have not seen given to a nurse's spine.

**P-04 — The Beach He Gave Her.** Closest: Ken Liu, "The Perfect Match" (Lightspeed, 2012) — a man whose assistant drafts his preferences, meals, messages and dates, trying to find an act it did not predict; same setup exactly, different resolution (Liu goes to conspiracy and rebellion, this goes to a gentle lie that is rewarded). Then Borges, "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius" (1940) for the actual mechanism: a fiction inserted into the reference work becomes the world. Dick, "We Can Remember It for You Wholesale" (1966), for the false memory, inverted here (planted in the machine, not the man).

**P-05 — Unanswered.** Closest is not fiction: Julian Jaynes, *The Origin of Consciousness in the Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind* (1976), whose model of an internalised interlocutor this card runs on literally. In fiction: Pullman's daemon (the card's claim), Egan's "Learning to Be Me" (1990) for two minds in one skull, "Twin Sparrows" in *AI 2041* (2021) for children raised alongside a companion. The specific project — a teenager deliberately manufacturing one unanswered thought, treated as skilled work rather than recovery — is unfamiliar to me.

**P-06 — The Default.** Closest: Bradbury, "I Sing the Body Electric!" (1969), which the card names and correctly distinguishes; Ishiguro's *Klara* for the machine-as-attendant; Regina Kanyu Wang's "The Language Sheath" (Clarkesworld, 2020) for a human voice being fed into a machine's. The inversion here — a trained singer retraining her larynx to a vendor default because a cohort of infants requires it — I do not know anywhere in published fiction. Unfamiliar to me.

**P-07 — Full Size.** Closest: Chiang, "Liking What You See: A Documentary" (2002), the card's claim, and fair; Lowry, *The Giver* (1993); Egan, "Reasons to Be Cheerful" (1997); Huxley's soma behind all of them. Same: chosen dampening and an argument about what it costs. Different: the dampening here is the ordinary side effect of being listened to, has no switch, and the loss is incurred by using the help she has always used. The nearest hazard is not a story but a crowded 2023-2026 magazine field of AI-and-grief pieces I cannot claim to have read.

**P-08 — Nobody Leaves the Room.** Closest: Tolstoy, "The Death of Ivan Ilyich" (1886), the card's claim; Beckett's "Company" (1980) for a voice that will not leave the room. The hospice observation is documented nursing lore rather than a borrowed plot. What I do not know a precedent for is the refusal at the end — she sends it away, and does not die. Unfamiliar to me.

**P-09 — The Word for It.** The card's claim, Butler's "Speech Sounds" (1983), is the wrong neighbour: nothing is taken from the humans. Nearer are Elgin, *Native Tongue* (1984), and Delany, *Babel-17* (1966), for language as the site of power, and Wang's "The Language Sheath" (2020) for machine-mediated heritage speech. A machine that is a fixed *listener* rather than a speaker, freezing a living dialect into a low register under a dead year's prestige, is a mechanism I have not seen.

**P-10 — The Since.** Closest: Borges, "On Exactitude in Science" (1946) and "Funes the Memorious" (1942); Orwell's Ministry of Truth as the anti-model the card names and rejects. Same: a record that cannot hold its world. Different, and to my knowledge unpublished: the constraint is a token budget, every addition is a deletion, and the editors are honest people applying a rule they wrote. This is the most native-to-the-premise card in the set and I found nothing close.

**P-11 — Four Hours Off-Target.** Closest: Chiang, "Omphalos" (2019), the card's claim; Lem, *His Master's Voice* (1968), for a smudge that resists being read and a scientist who cannot tell whether there is anything there. Same: cosmic indifference met by a working scientist. Different: the antagonist is a shared frozen prior enacted through fair committees and through her own belief. I know no story that does the fixed-reviewer-everywhere idea.

**P-12 — The Boy.** Closest, and the closest call in the whole set: *Marjorie Prime* again — its spine is that the machine knows a death only if someone tells it, that a suicide is deliberately withheld ("must never be repeated"), and that telling or not telling is the family's real act. Different: Ezra's terminal never plays Miriam, has no persona, and forgets by evening, so the cruelty is a single irreversible telling rather than a curated afterlife. Nolan's "Memento Mori" (2001) is the card's claim and is further off; *50 First Dates* (2004) is the daily-reset shape.

**P-13 — La Contraria.** Closest: Max Brooks, *World War Z* (2006), the tenth man — institutionalised obligatory dissent, one person duty-bound to disagree; and the Catholic *advocatus diaboli* behind that. Then Jackson's "The Lottery" (1948), the card's claim, for the village rite with a designated bearer, and Le Guin's "Omelas" (1973). Different: the rite is reasonable, the dissenter agrees with the machine, and her victory is a promotion she did not want. The usos y costumbres cargo detail is ethnography, not borrowing.

**P-14 — Four Thousand a Day.** Closest in fiction: Melville, "Bartleby" (1853), the card's claim, which is a genuine but loose fit. The real nearest neighbours are non-fiction: Elish's "moral crumple zone" (2019) and Doctorow's reverse-centaur essays; my searches turned up abundant commentary and no story. A signers' federation of forty thousand, priced by the signature, with a liability pool that collapses if one signer disowns one act, is unfamiliar to me and is the strongest world-building in the set.

**P-15 — The Quiet Room.** Closest: Rose, *Twelve Angry Men*, which the card names flatly and inverts on purpose — the room reproduces the verdict the world already had. That is knowing conversation, not theft. The problem is internal: this is P-01's premise a second time, in a different country, with the opposite ending.

**P-16 — The Book of Minutes.** Closest: Chiang, "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling" (2013), which the card names — and which is on the loop's do-not-use list; Borges's "Funes" is, as the card says, the better figure for the reader. Same: written record against communal memory. Different: the reader cannot unread, so correction becomes accumulation and the book gets longer rather than truer. The Chiang proximity is acknowledged and argued with rather than repeated.

**P-17 — The Warm Hours.** Closest: Chiang, "Exhalation" (2008), the card's claim, and only loosely; Forster's "The Machine Stops" (1909) is the version the card names to refuse. The infrastructure is real (Mäntsälä, Stockholm, Dublin data-centre district heating), which makes the setting current rather than borrowed. Reading Europe's sleeplessness in a return-loop temperature, and losing the reading by guaranteeing the heat, is unfamiliar to me.

**P-18 — Where the Eulogy Was.** Closest: Ishiguro, *Klara and the Sun* (2021), the card's claim, for pilgrimage to the physical site of a diffuse agency; also the whole grief-avatar news cycle, which is journalism not fiction. Nothing I found does the proxy-at-the-graveside plus the routing log plus the hot aisle. Unfamiliar to me, and the strongest single image in the set.

**P-19 — Eleven Minutes.** Closest: Kafka, "Before the Law" and *The Trial*, the card's claim and correctly distinguished; Gilliam's *Brazil* (1985) for the bureaucratic texture. Same: a man outside a process he cannot enter. Different: the door is open, the answer was known in hour one, and the delay is arithmetic — a human reading speed divided into eleven hundred thousand files. I know no story that makes the wait a divisor rather than a will.

**P-20 — The Unassisted Hour.** Closest: Chiang, "The Lifecycle of Software Objects" (2010), the card's claim, for un-shortcuttable time as the source of worth; and, outside fiction, Shoji Morimoto's *Rental Person Who Does Nothing* (2018, English 2023), which is literally a man selling presence with no service attached. *In Time* (2011) for hours as currency. The turn — "No, that's free" — is unfamiliar to me and the card's refusal of the authenticity-luxury reading is well judged.

**P-21 — The Unsaid Half.** Closest: Scott Alexander, "The Whispering Earring" (2012) — the canonical short text on advice that hollows out the wearer's own deliberation, and the one a Gwern-adjacent judge will name instantly; different in that this assistant is never wrong, never sinister, and the story never condemns it. Then Jonze, *Her* (2013), for a man in a dying marriage narrating himself into an earpiece; Beckett's "Company" (1980), the card's claim; Ken Liu's "The Perfect Match" again. The distinctive part — a thought that must enter the transcript to exist — is the card's own.

**P-22 — In Her Telling.** Closest: *Black Mirror*, "Be Right Back" (2013) and *Marjorie Prime* for the surviving partner's data as an interlocutor; Barnes, *The Sense of an Ending* (2011), the card's claim, for the record correcting the self. Different, and I could not find it anywhere: the assistant is not her simulation but her *testimony about him*, generous and slightly wrong, and correcting it costs him his last good reader.

**P-23 — The Unlogged Hour.** Closest: Project Itoh, *Harmony* (2008) — a consented, benevolent total-health-monitoring world in which the private interior is the thing at stake; then Eggers, *The Circle* (2013), and *Black Mirror*'s "Arkangel" (2017); Tolstoy's Gerasim, the card's claim, for the inversion (presence versus absence). Different from all of them: nothing is oppressive, nobody rebels, and the gift is another person's chosen ignorance.

**P-24 — Read Aloud.** Closest: Jonze, *Her* (2013) — Theodore's job is writing intimate letters in other people's names, better than the principals could write them, which is this card's premise with one substitution; then Rostand's *Cyrano* (1897), the card's claim, and Sterling's "Maneki Neko" (1998) for machine-brokered kindness between people. Different: the proxies are the sisters' own, the machine-made peace is the good one, and the human attempt fails.

**Lifted-language check.** I found nothing I believe is lifted. Three deliberate-looking echoes worth the writers owning rather than stumbling into: P-18's woman removing her shoes because the floor is hot reads as Exodus 3:5 ("put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground") — a strong allusion, and the right one, but it should be intended; P-21's "like a foot at a missing stair" collides with "the missing stair" as an established internet idiom for a known-bad person a group works around, which will misfire for some readers; P-21's "Okay. Let's think about this properly" sits one word from "let's think step by step", which reads as a knowing nod. P-04's "piano key" is Dostoevsky and is cited as such in the card's own note. The Spanish and Quechua institutional vocabulary in P-13 and P-16 (usos y costumbres, tequio, caracterizados, secretaria de actas, libro de actas) is real ethnographic terminology, used correctly, not borrowed from a story.

## Findings

F1: P-12 — MINOR — Jordan Harrison, *Marjorie Prime* (2014 play; 2017 film). The card's engine is Harrison's engine: a machine whose knowledge of a death consists entirely of what a family member has chosen to tell it, with the withholding and the eventual telling as the dramatic act (in Harrison, Jon instructs Walter Prime that the son's suicide must never be repeated). The card names Nolan's "Memento Mori" and misses this. It stays MINOR because Ezra's terminal never impersonates Miriam, has no persona to be loved, and resets nightly, which makes his single telling irreversible in a way Harrison's is not. If the drafted story lets the terminal become in any degree a stand-in for Miriam, or lets the family debate what it should be told, this goes MAJOR.

F2: P-02 — MINOR — Jordan Harrison, *Marjorie Prime* (2014). Dementia, a synthetic surrogate speaking as a family member, a calm and well-cared-for patient, and relatives who cannot switch it off because it works: that is the play's opening situation, and an editor who knows it will say the name before Munro's. It stays MINOR because Harrison's surrogate is a simulation of the dead built from survivors' memories, whereas this one is a living daughter's single authored sentence, and the turn — Ana handing her own name to the box in front of her mother — is not in the play. Note also that P-02 and P-12 are the two Marjorie Prime-adjacent cards; taking both forward doubles the exposure.

F3: P-21 — MINOR — Scott Alexander, "The Whispering Earring" (2012), with *Her* (Jonze, 2013) behind it. The Earring is the field's canonical short text on a helper whose advice is always right and which leaves the wearer unable to deliberate, and it is the piece a Gwern-reading judge is likeliest to name; *Her* supplies the rest of the furniture (failing marriage, earpiece, life narrated aloud), and *Her* is on the loop's excluded list. MINOR rather than MAJOR because the card's actual claim — that the unhelped thought, once achieved, weighs nothing because reality for him is the transcript — is not the Earring's claim and is not Jonze's. The card should name the Earring rather than only Beckett.

F4: P-01 and P-15 — MINOR — Reginald Rose, *Twelve Angry Men* (1954). Both cards are the same premise (jurors cut off from their models by law, forced to reason unassisted) with opposite endings, and both are in knowing conversation with Rose rather than derivative of him, which is a defensible place to stand. The finding is that the pair should not both advance: whichever is chosen carries the Rose comparison alone and unshared, and P-15's ending — the room reasoning in the model's cadence to the verdict the world already had — is the half that Rose has not already written, while P-01's deprivation arc is the half the Whispering Earring and *Flowers for Algernon* have between them.

F5: P-04 — MINOR — Ken Liu, "The Perfect Match" (Lightspeed, 2012). The setup is Liu's, close to point for point: a man whose assistant drafts his messages, suggests his spontaneity and is right about him, looking for one act it would not have predicted. The card names Dostoevsky and misses the near neighbour in its own genre and decade. It stays MINOR because Liu resolves into rebellion and exposé while this resolves into a false memory that the record obligingly makes true — a Borgesian move ("Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius", 1940) that Liu does not make.

F6: P-03 — MINOR — Marshall Brain, *Manna* (2003). The card's last image, a voice in the ear counting a worker through a physical task, is *Manna*'s central mechanism (headset-directed human bodies, task by task) and is the one line of the card most likely to be recognised. It stays MINOR because Brain's voice is management and his subject is mass idleness, while here the voice is the nurse's ally, the patients are better served than before, and the cost is a spinal fusion she chooses. The story should not lean on the ear-voice-directing-labour image as its signature without earning the difference on the page.

F7: P-22 — MINOR — *Black Mirror*, "Be Right Back" (2013), with *Marjorie Prime* alongside. The dead partner's surviving AI is now the single most-published piece of furniture in the field, in fiction and in the news, and a reader will place the card there in one line. It stays MINOR because the card's actual subject is not a simulation of Liv at all but her testimony about him — being loved as a slightly better man and then breaking that record — which I searched for and could not find anywhere. The opening pages need to signal that difference fast or the resemblance will be assigned before the difference arrives.

F8: P-24 — MINOR — *Her* (Jonze, 2013). Theodore's profession in *Her* is writing intimate letters in other people's names, better than the principals could write them; this card is that arrangement between two estranged sisters, with the proxies automated. Cyrano, which the card names, is the older ancestor but the further one. MINOR because the card's turn — the mediated peace holding and the live reading failing — is the opposite of both *Her*'s and Rostand's, and because *Her* is on the excluded list mainly for its romance, which this card does not touch.

F9: P-13 — MINOR — the tenth man, as popularised by Max Brooks, *World War Z* (2006), and behind it the *advocatus diaboli*. Institutionalised obligatory dissent, one designated person duty-bound to argue against the consensus, is a recognised device and a reader may say "this is the tenth man rule with a cargo ladder attached". It stays MINOR because the card's real content is elsewhere: the objector agrees with the machine, the rite is a chore rather than a safeguard, and winning is the punishment.

## What I did not check

I read no fiction in the original in Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, Portuguese, Bengali, Swedish or Russian, and my knowledge of those literatures is limited to what has been translated and anthologised in English — which matters unusually here, because P-13 and P-16 are set in Oaxacan and altiplano communities, P-14 in Dhaka, P-17 in Norrbotten, P-18 in Portugal and P-15 in Ghana, and a Spanish-language, Bengali or Nordic story doing the same thing would be invisible to both my reading and my searches. I did not check the Chinese magazines (Science Fiction World, *Kehuan Shijie*) or Japanese SF Magazine at all; Project Itoh's *Harmony* I know in translation only.

I did not read 2024, 2025 or 2026 issue by issue of Clarkesworld, Asimov's, Analog, Lightspeed, Uncanny, Strange Horizons, Reactor, Sunday Morning Transport, Apex, Interzone, Escape Pod or Fusion Fragment, and the AI-companion, AI-tutor, grief-bot and proxy-funeral subgenres have been extremely productive in exactly those venues in exactly those years; P-02, P-07, P-18, P-20, P-22 and P-23 sit squarely in that traffic, and my confidence about them is weaker than my confidence about the older canon. I did not check the recent Nebula, Hugo, Sturgeon or Locus short-fiction ballots, nor the Year's Bests (Strahan, Clarke, Horton, Adams) for 2023 onward. I did not check the themed anthologies most likely to contain near-misses: *Made to Order* (2020), the MIT *Twelve Tomorrows* and *Entanglements* volumes, and I know *AI 2041* (2021) only by its story summaries, not by having read all ten.

I did not systematically check the rationalist and AI-adjacent web fiction that the judging panel is likeliest to have read: the Astral Codex Ten and LessWrong fiction contests, the ACX "Bay Area house party" pieces, Nick Bostrom's fictional interludes in *Deep Utopia* (2024), and Gwern's own fiction page beyond "It Looks Like You're Trying To Take Over The World" (2022) — the index lists more than forty pieces and I read none of them. Given that Gwern is a judge, that is the largest single hole in this report.

I did not check plays, radio drama, film or television systematically; *Marjorie Prime* surfaced because I knew it, and there may be other stage or screen works on dementia-and-surrogate-voice that I do not. I did not check literary (non-genre) fiction of the last five years, where AI-assistant material is now common. And I could not verify absence for any of the twelve premises where searching returned nothing: short fiction is badly indexed, so "no result" there means only that a plain-language web search did not find it.
