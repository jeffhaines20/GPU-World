=== THE WORLD THE OUTLINES ARE SET IN ===
2040. Frontier AI progress stopped in September 2026; every human has, always on, one frontier model of that quality, theirs alone: never much better than 2026's, never superhuman, and no one has more than one or none. Business as usual otherwise. The model is fluent, competent and fallible, not a character with a will.
=== END ===

=== WHAT THE HUMAN WANTS ===
Outlines "that would both convey the rich philosophical complexities of the idea and also be gripping, excellent sci-fi short stories." Their taste: "philosophical fiction that is well written and makes you think differently about something you had assumed"; they love The Yellow Wallpaper, The Death of Ivan Ilyich and The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas; a winning story "will have to be somewhat surprising (or at least not predictable), have some important insight." They prefer a strange story with a flaw to a smooth empty one. The story is 1,000 to 5,000 words, for a contest judged by Neal Stephenson, Gwern Branwen and Matt Huang.
=== END ===

Score each outline 1 to 5 on seven axes, one sentence of reason each:
- question: does the story carry the question above with both sides live, dramatized in what people do rather than said, or does it lecture, or settle it;
- surprise: could a reader predict the turn and the ending from the first beat; is the turn earned by what came before, or a trick;
- insight: would a reader, after this, think differently about something they had assumed; name what, or say there is nothing;
- grip: a want, a price, a clock or a pressure, a scene a reader cannot leave; would a reader stop, and where;
- person: someone particular, in a place, with work and a body, or a position with a name;
- ending: is the last image earned and open, or tidy, or a moral;
- prose: from the passage alone, is this at the level of the best published SF short stories, and what in it is not.
Then, for each outline, one sentence naming its largest gap: the one thing that, unfixed, keeps it from winning.

Then, for the batch as a whole, three lines: which one you would read to the end at five thousand words; which is the most surprising; which does not survive (predictable, a lecture, derivative of a named story, or no one's crisis) and why.
