# Revision packet for O-1

## The outline as it stands

### O-1 — Right, Late
Conceit: A father who never asked the model about his son kept instead, from the year the boy turned two, a notebook a year of predictions: what he expected, how sure he was, what happened. When the son brings his audit at nineteen, it meets this second ledger, which is both harsher than the machine's and kinder. The entry that decides it can be scored only by the son, and he cannot score it either.
Who: Wade Hautala, 48, night dispatcher at the county 911 center in Duluth, Minnesota, March 2040. All shift he reads his model's protocols to strangers; it has never heard his son's name. His son Owen, 19, ships out at dawn as a deckhand on the season's first laker. Wade wants him to leave knowing he was known and not relayed to. He pays with the notebooks, which prove he was also practicing.
Obstacle: The only instrument that can score him is his son. The model knows what a reasonable father would have done, and the notebooks know what Wade expected. Neither knows what happened inside Owen. No apology buys a verdict the son does not have, and the ship leaves at six.
Turn: On the night Owen, seventeen, stayed out, Wade wrote "Skyline lookout. Home by two if nobody comes after him. He has it with him. Conf 80," and scored it "Right, late." Owen's list says: north pier, nearly three hours, the model asking him six times to call his father. For seventeen years Wade has written the next thing down before it happened. Now he asks instead, and he cannot score the answer. At dawn that year's notebook leaves in Owen's sea bag, and the father no longer holds the record of his own best call.
Last image: The kitchen table after the door shuts. The call-log books stand in a row, spines taped OWEN 2023 to OWEN 2040, with a gap where 2038 stood. In the gap, folded in thirds and standing on edge, is Owen's handwritten list, the model's sentences and his son's in the same ink.
Question: Wade put a number on every risk he ran on his son before he could know it, and the numbers got better. The practice also charged a boy who wasn't his, and no one can add up its largest bet. Was he entitled to learn his son this way, or was his education paid from someone else's account?
What the reader assumed and no longer does: That whether a parent knew their child is a fact about the parent. Here it is a verdict only the child can give, and the child, made by what he is judging, cannot quite give it.
Why it cannot be predicted, and why it is earned: A reader of the card expects the son's list to be the bill; instead the father answers with a ledger that convicts him where the list only faulted him (a stranger's boy paid for the theft), vindicates him where the list only conceded, and turns the audit on a night neither record got right. It is earned because the logging habit is planted at the console and the hand-copied list in scene two, and "He has it with him" shows Wade's refusal leaning on the model in his son's pocket.
Not the obvious version: The nearest cliché: the machine lists a father's failures, he defends himself or breaks, and they reconcile at the dock or don't. Here the father is not a romantic trusting his gut but an empiricist who priced every bet. The machine's audit is the gentler document. The ending changes not a heart but who holds the evidence.
Closest published: "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling," Ted Chiang. A father checks his memory of a quarrel with his daughter against a lifelog and finds the record against him. There the record is neutral and the subject is memory. Here both records take sides, the question is entitlement, and the deciding fact lies inside the son, where no record reaches.
Beats:
1. County dispatch, 22:40, March 24. Wade reads a choking protocol word for word, a toddler breathes, and he writes the times on paper as he always does. He traded away his Christmas shift to get off at eleven. Change: we meet the best relay in the building.
2. Home on the East Hillside at midnight; Owen reports aboard at 05:00. He sets out twelve cases, copied by hand from his model's audit because Wade won't read its words on a screen. He asks which ones Wade knew in advance: was he raised, or practiced on? Change: the clock starts.
3. Wade brings down the call-log books. They hold every decision he made for Owen without asking, written before the outcome: his guess at the script, what he did, what he expected, how sure he was, and later the outcome. Change: Owen learns he was practiced on deliberately and on paper, and Wade has handed over the only proof of what he knew.
4. The joint audit. Owen reads the model's line, Wade reads his prediction, and the early books are full of "Wrong." The theft, 2031: "He'll put it back by Sunday. Conf 80." The Tolliver boy was blamed and cut from the team: "Cost: not mine." Change: the bill spreads past the family and into Owen's own silence.
5. The later books. Wade's confidence numbers come down and his "Right"s go up. The heartbreak, 2037: "Say nothing about her. He'll come to me in April. Conf 65." Owen came on April 9. Change: the case for the father becomes real, and Owen sees his childhood as a learning curve.
6. March 2038: the two records disagree about where Owen was. Change: Wade's best call, made when he was most skilled, was right that Owen would come home and wrong about where he was, and it leaned on the model in Owen's pocket.
7. The turn (the passage). Change: Wade asks instead of predicting, and his pen stays over the line.
8. 04:40. Owen asks him not to come to the dock. Wade goes upstairs for his own father's watch cap, and they part in their own words, badly. Change: Wade does not follow, making the old bet again and knowing that he is making it.
9. The laker's horn sounds from the canal. Change: Wade turns to the table and sees what Owen took and what he left.
The passage:
Owen turned the page and stopped, and Wade knew which page it was by how long he stayed there.

"Read yours," Owen said.

Wade did not need the book. "'Eleven March, twenty-three-oh-five. Not home. Script: call him, police at one. Not calling. He's at the Skyline lookout. Home by two if nobody comes after him. He has it with him. Conf eighty.'" He had written it at this table, with the porch light on. "'Outcome, oh-two-forty. Right, late.'"

Owen smoothed the list and found the line with his thumb. He read it in the voice he had used all night for everything on that paper, his and its, so that Wade still could not have said where his son stopped and the other began.

"'North pier, twenty-three-ten to oh-one-fifty. It asked me to call you six times.'"

The furnace came on. Below the hill a truck geared down toward the harbor, and Wade saw the north pier the way he had seen it on the canal cameras a hundred Marches: the ice glazed over the concrete, the light at the end, the black water lifting against the wall.

"Why didn't you call?"

It was, he would think later, the first time in seventeen years that he had asked before he wrote.

"Because you'd have come."

Wade waited for the rest. That was all of it.

"And if I had?"

Owen looked at the window, where there was nothing to see but the kitchen and the two of them in it.

"I don't know."

The pen was uncapped in his hand; he did not remember uncapping it. After Right, late there was room on the line for a word. He held the pen over it the way he held a finger over the transfer key when a caller went quiet, and the clock on the stove went past four, and he did not write anything at all.

## The two reviewers' critiques of this outline

### reviewer-a — question 4, surprise 3, insight 4, grip 4, person 5, ending 3, prose 4 (total 27 of 35)

question: The ledger puts both sides on paper (early "Wrong"s, the Tolliver boy logged as "Cost: not mine," later calibrated "Right"s), but pricing every call at "Conf 80" turns the judgment the question is about, seeing what cannot be put into words, into forecasting, and the father learning to ask leans toward a lesson.
surprise: The notebooks are a real surprise, but after them the arc runs as expected (early errors, later competence, one ambiguous night, the controlling man finally asks, a parting at dawn), inside what is nearly the card's own father, son and list scene.
insight: The stated insight (only the child can say whether he was known, and he is made by what he judges) is familiar, while two sharper ones stay buried: a stranger's boy paid for the father's education, and the father could refuse to ask only because the model in his son's pocket was his insurance.
grip: The 911 opener, the ship at six and the pier the two records disagree about make a real clock, but beats 4 to 6 are two men reading entries aloud in turn, and a recitation of cases is where a reader would flag.
person: Wade, the best relay in the dispatch center, holding a pen over a line the way he holds a finger over the transfer key, in a kitchen on the East Hillside with the canal cameras in his head, is fully particular.
ending: The taped spines with Owen's list folded in thirds in the 2038 gap are earned but tidy, a symmetrical exchange of records that closes the image while claiming the verdict is open.
prose: The log diction ("Conf eighty," "Right, late") and "Because you'd have come" are excellent, but "It was, he would think later, the first time in seventeen years that he had asked before he wrote" tells the turn, and the furnace-came-on pause is stock.
largest gap: It is the card's own scenario (father, son at nineteen, a list, "raised or practiced on") with a ledger added, so its shape is the expected one and it ends in a neat swap of records, while its strongest material, the Tolliver boy and "He has it with him," sits mid-story as a side detail.

## The originality critic on this outline

**O-1 Right, Late.** (1) Ted Chiang, "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling" (2013). Same: a father's account of raising his child is checked against a record, and the record goes against him. Different: here two records take sides (the model's audit and Wade's own ledger), the question is his right to learn this way rather than his memory, and the deciding fact lies inside the son. (2) Atul Gawande, "The Learning Curve" (*The New Yorker*, 2002; nonfiction). Same: skill is bought by practising on people who never agreed to it, and the early cases pay for the later ones; that is exactly the "learning curve" Owen sees. Different: Gawande's subjects are strangers in a teaching hospital; Wade's is his own son. (3) Andre Dubus, "A Father's Story" (1983). Same: a father's bet on his own child is paid for by another family's son, as in the Tolliver beat ("Cost: not mine"). Different: Dubus's father hides a death and argues it out with God; here the stranger's cost is one beat, not the story. (4) *The Guilty* (*Den skyldige*, Gustav Möller, 2018), setting only. Same: an emergency dispatcher trusts a hunch over the script. Different: Wade keeps the script at work, bets only at home, and his bets mostly come in. The ledger habit (bets with a stated confidence, scored later) is forecasting practice, known from Tetlock and Gardner's *Superforecasting* (2015, nonfiction). I know no fiction built on it, and it is the element in this outline I can place least. Verdict: in conversation with Chiang, knowingly, since the outline names him; otherwise familiar furniture, new house. Nothing I know makes it derivative of a published work. Its problem is O-3.

**Between the outlines.** O-1 and O-3 are one story twice. In both, a father keeps a paper record of his judgments about his child, one book a year, written at night. The grown child (19 in O-1, 23 in O-3) spends the last night before leaving by boat for a working life (deckhand; junior doctor) scoring the books at the kitchen table, as "Right"/"Wrong" or as ticks and crosses. The deciding entry is a teenage episode where the father guessed what was happening and stayed away. The child's answer is that the father's coming was the thing dreaded ("Because you'd have come"; "if Dad comes up those stairs into the gallery I will die"). Both close on the kitchen table after the child has gone, with a line left blank and a pen held over it (O-1) or lying across it (O-3). The only real difference is the father's sign: Wade always departed from the model, Magnus never did. That mirror is interesting, but it does not make two stories. O-4 tells the same premise a third time. Its plot departs furthest from O-1, but it keeps O-1's main elements: the father who never asked the counsel, the 19-year-old leaving home, the learning curve with the child's early years at the bottom, a theft as a key entry, and the question "raised, or practised on?" word for word. O-2 and O-5 are one skeleton twice. In both, a practitioner sits at one end of a concordance scale (Noor's 97.1 is the province's lowest; Aalin's 2,914 judgments all agreed with Counsel). A retiring elder who knows from the inside what a departure costs lets one happen (Marga's five women moved for nothing; Juan's son and grandchildren, spent on another Deemster's education). The one departure falls on a stranger and rests on a perception that cannot be put into words. Then a working woman who reads instruments for a living ("the way she would read a gauge"; "the look she must give a monitor at four in the morning") runs her own model's fair review and asks whether the practitioner knows ("Do you know now?"; "Do you know you're right?"). The two passages are that same scene. All five end on a verdict withheld: a pen held over the line; "Again"; a tick crossed through and a last line left blank; "could not tell"; "No". The passages also share one writer's reflexes. Four cut from the hard line to an ambient sound ("The furnace came on"; "The Rayburn ticked"; "The radiator ticked. Downstairs the fridge came on."; "Rain ticked on the roofs of the cars"). Three have a hard line said flatly "the way" a job reads things (a gauge, a potassium, the wind getting up). Two have someone who "did not remember" a small act of the hand (O-1, O-4). Two have someone look "at the window, where" (O-1, O-2). Three of the five name the same Chiang story as their closest neighbour. That choice is wrong for O-2, and O-3 and O-5 miss nearer neighbours (F5–F7).

Findings naming this outline:
- F1: MAJOR — O-1 and O-3 (each other) are one story twice: the father's yearly books scored overnight by a grown child leaving by boat, the teenage night he stayed away, and the blank line under a pen at the kitchen table; the loop should put them to the human as one option rather than two of five, or rebuild one so it shares neither the scoring night nor the ending.
- F2: MINOR — O-4 (with O-1 and O-3) is a third telling of the premise in which a grown child audits the father, sharing O-1's refusing father, learning curve, theft entry and "raised, or practised on?" word for word; the loop should tell the human that the five outlines are two situations and, counting O-1 and O-3 once, four stories.
- F4: MINOR — O-1 to O-5 (one another) all end on a withheld verdict and share the same prose reflexes (the cut to an ambient sound, "said it the way", "did not remember", "looked at the window, where"); the loop should tell the human that choosing an outline does not choose an ending, and should have the draft's critics look for these reflexes by name.
