## The card as it stands

### Q-21 — The Competence Marriage

Implication: When every adult holds the same always-on clerical and advisory competence, the domestic trade in cognitive labour — "I do the money, you do the words" — stops binding couples, and only relationships that ran on the body keep their texture of need.

Follows from: (1) A 2026-frontier model already does the domestic clerical range — taxes, forms, the letter to the school, the landlord negotiated by text. (2) Each partner has one, so neither needs the other's cognitive competence; help becomes a gesture, not a need. (3) Long partnerships were held partly by these trades — not the whole glue, but the daily texture of small debts and thanks that made two people a team. (4) The premise grants a GPU, not a body: cooking, lifting, driving, nursing remain things only a person does for a person, so bodily complementarity keeps binding. (5) So the cognitive-trade marriage, the professional couple's pride, loses its need while the bodily-trade marriage keeps it: a quiet class inversion. Weakest: (2)-(3), that people stop asking once they need not, and that the asking was load-bearing, not decorative.

For an ordinary person: Margaret, 58, married thirty years, wrote every letter the family sent while he did the bank, the car and the arguing. Now his share writes a better letter than she ever did and hers explains his pension better than he could. The currency is gone: nobody says "thanks for handling that," because nothing was handled. What they say across the table is only what they want to say, and it is either not much or, she is startled to find, enough.

The strongest objection: Complementary competence was never the glue; it was the alibi. Couples stayed for habit, property, children, fear and love, and told a story about teamwork; remove the story and the glue is untouched. People had tax software for decades and still asked their spouses, because asking is a bid for attention, not for information, and the share cannot be bid to. Equality even helps: neither partner can now say "you couldn't manage without me," usually a controlling lie.

Either side: Is a bond that rested on being needed for something the other could not do a lesser bond this world rightly exposes, or the only kind most people can sustain, which this world quietly removes?

Not the obvious version: The cliché is the spouse who leaves for the AI. Nobody leaves; the share is deferential and wants nothing. It competes for the spouse's usefulness, not affection, wins without trying, and the resulting loneliness is two people in one room with nothing left to do for each other.

Who it lands on: Margaret, who has worked out that the last time her husband asked her for anything that was not physical was 2031. She was the words of the marriage, the words are now free, and she does not know what she is.

## The two reviewers' critiques of this card (scores 1 to 5 on importance, rigour, novelty, arguability, story; then one sentence per axis and the weakest step)

### reviewer-a — importance 4, rigour 4, novelty 5, arguability 5, story 5

importance: The daily texture of long partnerships, and which marriages keep their need, is a whole-life matter and the card's class inversion makes it a whole-society one.
rigour: This is the cleanest derivation in the batch — it needs only the granted competence and the granted absence of a body, and it names its real weak joint honestly — though step (3)'s claim that the small-debt trades were load-bearing rather than decorative is asserted from intuition, not argued.
novelty: The inversion — that the professional couple loses its glue while the bodily-trade couple keeps it — is a genuinely unfamiliar thought, and "it competes for the spouse's usefulness, not affection, and wins without trying" is a formulation a reader of this subject has not seen.
arguability: The alibi objection (competence was never the glue, only the story told about it) is strong enough that a thoughtful reader could land on either side and change their mind twice.
story: Margaret, thirty years married, dating the last non-physical request to 2031, not knowing what she is now that the words are free, is the most complete person in the batch with the most precise price.
weakest step: Step (2)'s "help becomes a gesture, not a need" — the card's own objection observes that asking was always a bid for attention rather than information, and the card never answers why a bid that never depended on need would stop being made.

### reviewer-b — importance 4, rigour 3, novelty 3, arguability 4, story 4

importance: Most adults live inside a domestic division of labour, and a claimed class inversion in which bodily-trade marriages keep their need while professional ones lose it is a broad social claim.
rigour: The card's own objection is close to fatal — decades of tax software, search and accountants did not dissolve these marriages because asking is a bid for attention, and the card concedes this without answering it.
novelty: The erosion of what partners do for each other is near the common commentary on domestic automation, and only the class inversion in step (5) is fresh.
arguability: A reader can hold that being needed was an alibi all along or that it was the only glue most people can sustain, and the card does not stack the deck.
story: Margaret dating the last non-physical request to 2031 is a precise, quiet devastation with a real place and a real loss.
weakest step: (3), that the small daily trade was load-bearing rather than decorative, which the pre-2026 availability of the same substitutes already argues against.
