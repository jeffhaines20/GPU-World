# Revision packet for Q-27

## The card as it stands

### Q-27 — Nothing said is evidence
Implication: Because no institution can obtain a share-free person, speech stops being evidence of who someone is, and the institutions that judged by a single encounter can no longer recognize a change of heart, which can only be spoken.
Follows from: (1) A 2026 model writes and coaches a perfect apology, allocution or interview answer; everyone has one. (2) Universal and inalienable, it cannot be separated from the person: a room can be emptied of shares, but the words were rehearsed with one for months. (3) Parole boards, judges, asylum officers, admissions committees and confessors all read articulacy, coherence, detail and affect in one encounter as evidence of the person. (4) A signal must be costly to carry information; the cost is now zero for all, so every statement is equally perfect or, when the shares, being one mind, advise sounding unrehearsed, identically imperfect. (5) Institutions retreat to the record, which the share cannot produce in a moment; but a change of heart happens in a moment and can only be said, so the second chance has no instrument left. Weakest step: (5). Boards may keep the ritual and never have weighed the words; but the marginal cases, where the file said no and the room said yes, are where mercy lived.
For an ordinary person: A man spends four months rehearsing eleven minutes with his share; so does every man on his tier, and the share tells each not to sound rehearsed. Three board members hear fourteen flawless statements in a day while their own shares summarize fourteen files. Outside, a wife answers an apology with "did you write that?" and cannot be sure either way.
The strongest objection: Speech was never good evidence — con men, coached witnesses, the allocution as ritual — and boards already leaned on files. Coaches and ghostwriters existed for the rich; the share extends them to everyone, which is fairer. Nothing is lost that was not already an illusion.
Either side: If a change of heart can only be spoken and nothing spoken is evidence, does the second chance survive, or was it always a bet on words we are right to stop making? One says mercy that depended on eloquence was mercy for the eloquent; another, that whoever cannot be believed as new cannot become new.
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the hollow machine-written toast. This is not private sincerity but the institutions of judgment losing their instrument at once, because equality means they can neither forbid the share nor discount it; the casualty is the recognizable conversion.
Who it lands on: A man nine years in for a robbery in which someone died, who has changed in the way that changes a person and has rehearsed his allocution like everyone on his tier. He will walk in and say nothing prepared, knowing the board's shares will read that too.

## The two reviewers' critiques of this card (scores 1 to 5 on importance, rigour, novelty, arguability, story; then one sentence per axis and the weakest step)

### reviewer-a — importance 4, rigour 4, novelty 5, arguability 5, story 5

importance: Parole, asylum, admissions and apology between spouses all run on speech as evidence of character, and losing that instrument reshapes mercy for everyone who will ever need to be believed.
rigour: The costly-signalling argument is the cleanest reasoning in the batch — universality plus inalienability really does zero the signal's cost without permitting the usual remedy of banning the tool — though step (5)'s claim that the record cannot register a change of heart is asserted rather than shown, since conduct over years is also evidence of conversion.
novelty: That equality is what destroys the signal (institutions can neither forbid nor discount a universal share) and that the casualty is the recognizable conversion is a genuinely unfamiliar and precise thought, well clear of the hollow-toast cliché.
arguability: "Mercy that depended on eloquence was mercy for the eloquent" is a serious position that a reader might finish the card holding, which makes the two sides real.
story: A man nine years in who has actually changed and will walk in and say nothing prepared, knowing even that will be read, is crisis, want, price and room all at once.
weakest step: Step (5): institutions retreating to the record is treated as fatal to mercy, but years of documented conduct are arguably better evidence of a changed heart than eleven rehearsed minutes ever were, which would make the loss a gain.

### reviewer-b — importance 4, rigour 3, novelty 4, arguability 4, story 5

importance: Mercy, parole, asylum and the recognizability of moral change touch a whole life at its hinge and a society's entire apparatus of second chances, which is large enough.
rigour: Steps (1)-(4) follow cleanly from universality and inalienability, but (5) smuggles in that boards previously weighed spoken words heavily enough that their loss destroys the second chance, and the card's own concession that boards "may never have weighed the words" is not answered so much as waved at with "marginal cases."
novelty: The costly-signalling frame applied to contrition rather than to essays or cover letters, with the twist that equality forbids both banning and discounting the share, is a genuinely fresh angle on a subject usually discussed as cheating.
arguability: Both halves are live — that eloquence-gated mercy was class privilege, and that a person who cannot be believed as new cannot become new — and neither collapses on inspection.
story: A man nine years in, changed, choosing to walk in with nothing prepared while knowing the board's shares will read even that, is a want, a price and a room all at once.
weakest step: Step (4)'s claim that the cost of the signal is "now zero for all" conflates the cost of producing eloquent words with the cost of producing four months of rehearsal and the disposition to do it — effort remains costly and differentially distributed, so the signal is degraded, not annihilated.
