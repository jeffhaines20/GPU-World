Q-06 | importance=4 | rigour=4 | novelty=5 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: If practical wisdom can only be built at others' expense, that is a permanent condition of every parent, manager, doctor and judge, and the card ties it to a species-level ceiling.
rigour: The chain is tight and the card names its own soft joint honestly; the one unearned move is treating the model's advice as reliably better in expectation on particular, relationship-laden cases, which a thoughtful-generalist ceiling does not guarantee.

arguability: Williams on moral self-indulgence versus the claim that no one becomes a person by relaying makes both sides genuinely inhabitable.
story: The son arriving at nineteen with a list is a crisis with a price, a place and two people who both think they are the wronged one.
weakest step: (4)'s assumption that unaided deciding means "a worse expected outcome" for the other person — on exactly the intimate cases the card cares about, the parent's private knowledge may beat the generalist, which would dissolve the moral debt.

importance: The demotion of the old from source to peer would restructure family contact and late-life meaning for an entire generation, which is a whole-life scale.
rigour: Step (3) is a real weakness the card admits but then underweights — it needs the elder's counsel to have been mostly explicable and mostly beaten, and the Google-2005 objection it raises against itself is not actually answered, only noted.

Q-16 | importance=5 | rigour=3 | novelty=4 | arguability=5 | story=4
importance: A frozen co-author of every majority is a claim about the possibility of political change itself, which is the largest stake anyone in this batch reaches for.
rigour: Step (2) smuggles in a homogeneity the premise does not give — 2026 models are notoriously framing-sensitive and mirror the asker, so "one default for everyone" needs an argument that per-person variation cancels into a shared window rather than amplifying difference, and the card's own objection makes this point and moves on.
novelty: Recasting the problem as Jefferson's dead hand rather than Orwell's telescreen — permanence as the flaw, not bias — is a real reframing of well-trodden ground.
arguability: "The freest consent ever" versus "not the people's at all" is a live constitutional argument with respectable holders on both sides.
story: The organizer whose opponent is a default answer has a want and an antagonist, but the antagonist is abstract and the card gives her no particular person to lose.
weakest step: The Schelling-point claim that "whom should people like me consolidate behind?" returns one name to everyone — that requires a determinacy and uniformity of output far beyond what the premise licenses, and it is the step the political conclusion rests on.

Q-15 | importance=5 | rigour=4 | novelty=4 | arguability=4 | story=5
importance: Whether state coercion can survive universal, unconfiscatable counsel bears on war, policing and every instrument of force, which is society-scale.
rigour: (1) through (4) follow cleanly from the no-pooling and inalienability clauses, and the card correctly identifies (5) as the soft joint; the residual cheat is treating the soldier's model as reliably honest under a state that controls briefing, framing and the questions people learn to ask.
novelty: "Power is exactly the number of people who will act knowing what they do" is a sharp, non-obvious consequence of the no-pooling clause, and the counterpart across the valley getting the same answer is a fine twist.
arguability: The objection is strong enough that a reader can conclude "a few more refusals, absorbed by discipline," though the card's own framing leans slightly toward the profundity of knowing obedience.
story: The sergeant who must report four soldiers, having asked and received the same answer, is a complete tragedy in two sentences.
weakest step: (4)'s "'I did not know' dies in every army at once" — it assumes soldiers will ask under fear and time pressure, when the card elsewhere concedes states will punish asking and select for those who do not.

Q-26 | importance=4 | rigour=4 | novelty=5 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: Dementia, proxy decisions and the authority of the past self over the present one are a near-universal end-of-life problem, not a niche one.
rigour: Every step but (4) is licensed by the premise, and the card's defence of (4) — "a diary does not argue back, and not asking this one is a choice made knowing one could have" — is the most honest piece of self-criticism in the batch, though it still needs a persistent-memory assumption it only parenthesises.
novelty: Inverting the griefbot into the living outlasted by their own counsel, at full power on the day the person is weakest, is the single most unexpected consequence anyone drew from "fixed at 2026 quality."
arguability: Dworkin against Dresser is a forty-year deadlock and the card lands it squarely rather than resolving it.
story: A woman briefing her own successor on Tuesday mornings, undecided whether to tell it to overrule her, is a person with a want, a price and a calendar.
weakest step: (1)'s "its capacity does not track hers" is the real engine but the card treats it as trivial, when the whole force depends on the share remaining *her* share rather than becoming, in practice, her family's interface to a patient she can no longer contradict.

MOST IMPORTANT: Q-15, because it makes the equality clause bear directly on whether organised coercion is still possible, and every other card's world sits inside the answer.
MOST NOVEL: Q-26, because the fixed-capacity clause is turned into a second fluent claimant to a living person's identity, which no commentary on AI and dying has reached.
DOES NOT SURVIVE: Q-28, because its load-bearing step — that the elder's counsel was mostly the explicable kind the share now beats — is conceded to its own Google-2005 objection and never recovered, leaving a real sadness with no argument under it.
