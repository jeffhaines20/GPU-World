novelty: The reframing of a promise as prediction-plus-undertaking, and the observation that the innocence of not knowing the base rate is what is destroyed, is genuinely not in the standard AI commentary.

Q-12 | importance=5 | rigour=4 | novelty=4 | arguability=4 | story=5
importance: If dependents become net income for the first time since agrarian child labour, that rewrites family economics, birth incentives, end-of-life decisions and the meaning of guardianship for everyone.
rigour: The derivation is careful and names its own weak joint (whether law quarantines ward-engines), but it imports leasing from another card (Q-09), and the premise as granted says a share cannot be pooled or sold — so the whole edifice rests on an exception the premise appears to forbid.
novelty: The unpriced loophole in a perfectly equal distribution — equality is fiction for anyone who cannot direct their share — is a real economic insight the standard commentary has not made.
arguability: The trust-and-Coogan-account objection is respectable, but the card's framing tilts the reader toward "extraction," and the closing image of Lourdes computing the rent effectively settles the question before it is asked.

weakest step: "Since engine-hours can be leased (Q-09)" — leasing is the entire load-bearing assumption and it is borrowed, not derived, and it contradicts the premise's inalienability unless a card explicitly grants the exception.

importance: What a person's remaining contribution is, once competence is free, is a question that touches everyone's self-respect and the entire professional class's account of itself.
rigour: Step (4) — that fourteen years have made "what only a human can do" a stable public fact — is doing more work than it can bear, since a fixed model ceiling does not fix the frontier of human-plus-model practice, and step (3) assumes the overrule act was previously "buried" rather than simply ordinary.

Q-21 | importance=4 | rigour=4 | novelty=5 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: The daily texture of long partnerships, and which marriages keep their need, is a whole-life matter and the card's class inversion makes it a whole-society one.
rigour: This is the cleanest derivation in the batch — it needs only the granted competence and the granted absence of a body, and it names its real weak joint honestly — though step (3)'s claim that the small-debt trades were load-bearing rather than decorative is asserted from intuition, not argued.
novelty: The inversion — that the professional couple loses its glue while the bodily-trade couple keeps it — is a genuinely unfamiliar thought, and "it competes for the spouse's usefulness, not affection, and wins without trying" is a formulation a reader of this subject has not seen.
arguability: The alibi objection (competence was never the glue, only the story told about it) is strong enough that a thoughtful reader could land on either side and change their mind twice.
story: Margaret, thirty years married, dating the last non-physical request to 2031, not knowing what she is now that the words are free, is the most complete person in the batch with the most precise price.
weakest step: Step (2)'s "help becomes a gesture, not a need" — the card's own objection observes that asking was always a bid for attention rather than information, and the card never answers why a bid that never depended on need would stop being made.
