Q-14 | importance=5 | rigour=4 | novelty=4 | arguability=5 | story=4
importance: A state discovering its constitutional guarantees were budgeted on the poverty of the guaranteed is a whole-society reckoning that touches liberty, not a niche.
rigour: The chain is honest and self-auditing — it names the trial-penalty objection at step (4) and rescues itself with the correct arithmetic at (5), that a system sized for 5% breaks at 20% — though it still leans on the unargued claim that models at paralegal level materially change plea decisions rather than just paperwork volume.
novelty: Access-to-justice is a well-worn AI theme, but the inversion — universal access as a bank run that proves the deposits were never held — is a genuinely fresh framing of rights as an underfunded liability.
arguability: "Expand without limit or ration openly" is a real fork, and the honest-rationing side is genuinely attractive rather than a straw man, so a reader can end on either.
story: The public defender whose life's victory becomes her clients' fourteen-month wait is a person with a want and an exact price, though the material risks reading as procedural rather than intimate.
weakest step: Step (4) as the card admits, but more precisely step (6)'s assumption that the state cannot re-impose cost — it can raise the trial penalty, charge stacking, and pretrial detention, restoring the old equilibrium without ever writing a quota down, which dissolves the card's whole "audible clause."

Q-19 | importance=4 | rigour=3 | novelty=5 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: It reaches the religious practice of billions and the structure of what it means to address anything at all, which is life-scale even if it does not reorganize a society.
rigour: The premise grants an always-answering interlocutor, but the card's load-bearing move — that a change in the *contrast class* changes the phenomenology of divine silence — is asserted rather than argued, and it also quietly assumes near-universal routing of unspoken address to the model.
novelty: Refusing both clichés (AI as god, people praying to AI) and locating the change entirely in the unchanged party is a framing a well-read follower of this subject will not have seen.
arguability: The objection that the serious devout never expected words is strong enough that a reader could finish the card unconvinced, which is exactly the balance wanted.
story: Aisha Bello, fifty years of du'a, suspecting the machine of having taught her the difference between absence and presence, is a complete crisis in one sentence.
weakest step: Step (5) — the inference from "prayer's silence is now unique" to "the weight of that silence changes," which needs a mechanism (habituation to reply, atrophy of the capacity to sit with non-answer) that the card names but never supplies.

Q-03 | importance=5 | rigour=3 | novelty=4 | arguability=4 | story=3
importance: Whether a generation's inner solitude is constitutively shared is as large as questions about persons get, and it applies to every human born after 2026.
rigour: It rests on two stacked assumptions it honestly flags — the dialogical theory of selfhood, and that the always-available voice wins internalization over parents and peers — and the second is the one doing all the work while receiving only a one-clause defense.
novelty: The extended-mind and deskilling discourse is thoroughly trodden, but "their solitudes overlap, and overlap in the same place" is a sharper and less familiar claim than the deficit version it distinguishes itself from.
arguability: The King James and catechism objection is genuinely powerful, though the card slightly tilts the scales by conceding "the cohort may think better," which makes the sceptical side easier to take than it should be.
story: The "we think—" moment is excellent, but the nominated protagonist is a man conducting a month-long abstention experiment, which is a thought experiment with a beard rather than a person with something at stake.
weakest step: Step (4)'s inference from constant availability to internalized voice — a child arguing with a machine that never gets cross and never has skin in the outcome may internalize nothing at all, since what gets internalized in Vygotsky is a relationship with stakes, not merely a frequent conversational partner.

Q-02 | importance=4 | rigour=4 | novelty=5 | arguability=4 | story=4
importance: Whether a culture can have a history of taste when its universal instrument of first judgment is frozen bears on every act of making, and the premise's frozen-weights clause makes it unavoidable rather than speculative.
rigour: The move from frozen capability to frozen *disposition* is flagged and plausibly defended, and the "undiscriminating, not hostile" characterization is the card's best and most earned observation; the weak joint is whether a universally-known-unwise reader has enough authority to suppress anything.
novelty: The whole conversation about frozen models runs on homogenized output; relocating the problem to reception, and identifying the failure mode as timelessness rather than conservatism ("it cannot tell the future from the mistake"), is the most original single idea in this batch.
arguability: The Victorians-kept-one-sensibility objection is real, but the card's own "you cannot know in advance which work is new" rejoinder is strong enough that the anti side is somewhat the weaker position.
story: The eulogy scene is superb and exact — a sentence true and not yet written, praised as "a bold choice," no one else to ask before Thursday — though the mathematician alternative is thinner.
weakest step: Step (4)'s "so the new gets no signal," which assumes the absence of a negative signal is itself suppressive; in fact the card's own step (3) grants the model says "interesting" to everything, which is a *permissive* first reader, and permissiveness historically floods culture with novelty rather than starving it.

Q-23 | importance=4 | rigour=4 | novelty=3 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: The collapse of prose as evidence about its writer touches every apology, condolence and declaration a person sends or receives, which is universal without being civilizational.
rigour: The costly-signalling argument is tight and correctly extended to the clumsiness counter-move at (4), and the equality clause at (6) is the one place the premise's specific shape genuinely earns its keep rather than being decoration.
novelty: "Sincerity is unverifiable once eloquence is free" is among the most-written observations about generative AI, and the card's rescue — one letter from someone you know, not fakes at scale — refines the cliché rather than escaping it.
arguability: "Is an apology someone chose to send but did not compose an apology?" is a clean fork with serious weight on the scribes-and-greeting-cards side, and the card resists settling it.
story: Tomas having his own share draft the clumsy version his brother would have written, and believing that one, is the best single dramatic beat in the batch — a want, a price, and a self-inflicted wound.
weakest step: Step (3)'s claim that recipients *discount* well-formed intimate messages — the card's own objection shows the equilibrium may simply move to "the choosing is the act," and eleven years of silence followed by any message at all is already a costly signal that no drafting tool cheapens.

Q-24 | importance=4 | rigour=4 | novelty=4 | arguability=4 | story=5
importance: It is a fact about every death in the world after 2040 and about what survivors are owed, which is life-scale for everyone twice over.
rigour: It reasons strictly from the premise's inalienability clause rather than importing capability, and it handles the obvious escape (the transcript survives) correctly by distinguishing the witness from the portrait run on another's share.
novelty: The griefbot is the reigning cliché and this is its exact inverse — a world constructed so the griefbot cannot exist, asking whether that is mercy — which is an unfamiliar and well-chosen angle.
arguability: The mercy objection is genuinely strong, but the card's scene work loads the emotional dice toward theft, so the "this spares families a puppet" side is harder to hold at the end than it deserves.
story: Ruth saying "it isn't mine to give," and the daughter's own share replying kindly that it never met her mother, is complete, placed and devastating.
weakest step: Step (3)'s identification of the memory with the share — nothing in the premise says persistent memory is bound to the hardware rather than being ordinary data a person owns, and if it is data, Ruth can simply bequeath the transcript and the card's tragedy becomes a matter of interpretive fidelity rather than impossibility.

MOST IMPORTANT: Q-14, because it turns a universal private capability into a public constitutional crisis and forces a state to say out loud what its guarantees always cost.
MOST NOVEL: Q-02, because relocating the frozen-model problem from production to reception, and diagnosing timelessness rather than conservatism, is a framing this subject's readers have not seen.
DOES NOT SURVIVE: Q-03, because its load-bearing step (a child internalizes the never-cross machine over her parents) is asserted against the grain of the very theory it cites, and its nominated protagonist runs an experiment rather than suffering a crisis.
