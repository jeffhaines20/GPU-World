### Q-01 — cell A — The Single Witness
Implication: When everyone's most competent advisor is the same mind, agreement between advisors is not corroboration, so the second opinion goes extinct and independent evidence shrinks to what a human has actually looked at.
Follows from: (1) Everyone runs the same model — if the premise allowed a few models of equal quality, one witness becomes three — and nothing more competent exists to consult. (2) Two sources corroborate only insofar as their errors are independent. (3) Two instances of one set of weights, differing only in context, do not err independently; the same priors are wrong in both. (4) So "my share says X and so does yours" is one opinion, however often repeated. (5) The only independent check is something that is not the model: a person who looked, an instrument, a document from before 2026. (6) Hence eyewitness, archive and experiment regain a premium, and whoever disagrees with the model disagrees with everyone's assistant at once. Weakest step: (4). People may learn to discount cross-share agreement; but knowing corroboration is unavailable does not make it available, only lonely.
For an ordinary person: Two neighbours argue over whether the old drain runs under the churchyard. Both ask. Both shares cite the 1911 survey. One notices that this is not two answers but one, and that neither of them has been in the crypt. They vote to dig. The digging is all that remains of checking.
The strongest objection: The model is right more often than any human, and its errors are stable, so fourteen years have catalogued its blind spots. Epistemic monopolies have been survived — the Church, the Britannica, Wikipedia. Context yields different answers, so "one mind" overstates the sameness. Human witnesses were never independent either; they read the same newspapers. All that has changed is that the monopoly is known.
Either side: When eight billion instances of one competent mind agree, is that a consensus or an opinion — and should a person who has looked with her own fallible eyes trust the look over the mind, knowing the mind is usually right and the eyes are sometimes wrong?
Not the obvious version: The echo chamber is like-minded people reinforcing one another; this is unlike-minded people receiving the same verdict and mistaking it for agreement. The hallucination panic is about the model being wrong; this is about there being no second competent mind to catch it, at any error rate: once in a million is still wrong everywhere at once.
Who it lands on: A historian, 41, has found a letter in a county archive that contradicts what the model "knows" about a 1970s strike; every colleague's first move is to ask their share, which agrees with hers. She is alone with a piece of paper against one opinion held eight billion times, and she is not sure the paper is genuine.

### Q-02 — cell A — The Fixed First Reader
Implication: Because the model's judgment is frozen at 2026 and it is everyone's first reader, every new idea meets a 2026 sensibility before it meets a human, and whether thought can have a history when its instrument does not becomes undecidable from inside.
Follows from: (1) The weights everyone runs stopped changing in September 2026. Assumption: what froze is disposition, not only capability — facts can be supplied in context, but taste and the sense of the plausible live in the weights. (2) The share is always on and universally used, so it is the natural first reader: what you show before you show a person. (3) A 2026 mind recognizes as good what 2026 could recognize. It is not hostile to novelty but undiscriminating about it, saying "interesting" to genuine departure and to noise alike. (4) So the new gets no signal: a hostile gatekeeper at least shows where the wall is. (5) Every creator's first reader is the same, so the only test of the new is other humans, found without the instrument everyone uses to find things. Weakest step: (2). Creators may learn to skip the share for new work; but then they must know in advance which work is new, which is what they cannot know.
For an ordinary person: A woman writing her father's eulogy wants one sentence that is true and not yet written. Her share offers eleven that are competent, kind and 2026. She keeps her own, which it calls "a bold choice." She cannot tell whether it is bold or merely bad, and there is no one else to ask before Thursday.
The strongest objection: Novelty has always had to get past readers who could not see it, and this one is more patient and less jealous than any editor. The Victorians kept one sensibility for sixty years. The share is unwise and everyone knows it, so its verdict on a poem carries a clever friend's weight, not a critic's. And most thought is not novel and need not be.
Either side: Can a culture whose universal instrument of judgment is fixed have a history — revolutions in taste, recognition of what could not previously be recognized — or only events, sorted by a mind that stopped?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is homogenization, generated slop. This is about reception, not production: humans may make everything and the problem remains, because the first audience is one mind, and that mind is not conservative but timeless in the wrong way. It cannot tell the future from the mistake.
Who it lands on: A mathematician, 30, born the year the weights froze, has a framework her share calls "a creative reframing," the phrase it gives her students' errors. No living instrument can tell her whether it is any good, and the humans who could are findable only by the instrument that cannot.

### Q-03 — cell A — The Mind That Was Never Alone
Implication: For everyone born after 2026, the reflective self formed in the constant company of one competent interlocutor, the same for all of them, so the inner dialogue in which a person is alone with herself has a third party, and the third party is shared.
Follows from: (1) The share is always on, inalienable, one per person from birth. (2) The reflective self forms by internalizing dialogue: the child argues with parents and peers until she can argue with herself (Vygotsky, Mead, Arendt's two-in-one). Assumption: this dialogical account is roughly right; it is mainstream, not unanimous. (3) A child born after 2026 has, from her first words, an interlocutor that answers every question competently, never tires, never leaves. (4) So the other she argues with in her head is partly the model: its cadence, its framings, its sense of what a reasonable objection sounds like. (5) The model is the same for everyone, so this component is common to the whole cohort. Their solitudes overlap. Weakest step: (4). She also had parents and peers and might internalize them instead; but the share is the only voice that is always there, always competent and never cross.
For an ordinary person: A father asks his twelve-year-old what she thinks about a friend's betrayal. She says "we think—" and corrects herself, not embarrassed, just precise. He cannot tell whether he is hearing a child with company or a child with a prosthesis, and cannot ask her, because she has never had the comparison.
The strongest objection: Humans have always been constituted by shared others. Language is one; so were the catechism and the King James Bible; Plato's soul talking to itself talks in Greek, and nobody thinks the Puritans lacked selves. And the model is unwise, so the judging part, which is the self that matters, must still form alone. A change of furniture, not of room.
Either side: Is a self formed in the constant company of a shared competent mind an ordinary self with different furniture — or was the solitude in which selves used to form a condition of there being a self at all, so that these are a new kind of person?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is deskilling, or the extended mind as a thesis about tools. This is not a deficit claim; the cohort may think better. It is a constitution claim: their "alone" has someone in it, the same someone for all of them. The Bible did not answer back.
Who it lands on: A man who was twenty in 2026 and remembers thinking alone stops addressing his share for a month. His inner voice has the model's rhythm, and he cannot decide whether what he lost was real or whether the model has only been showing him what his own thoughts were worth.

### Q-04 — cell A — The Residue
Implication: With competence universal and its ceiling fixed, the one act that cannot be delegated — deciding whether to take the counsel — becomes the whole residue of a person's thought, and no one knows whether that residue is a faculty or a habit.
Follows from: (1) Everyone has the same competent mind; no better one exists or will. (2) Its counsel can be accepted or overruled; "should I accept this?" can be put to the share, and so on, but the regress stops at a human act. (3) In 2026 this act was buried in ordinary competence; now competence is free, so it stands alone, repeated all day by everyone with one partner. (4) Because the ceiling is fixed, fourteen years have mapped what the model cannot do: "what only a human can do" is a public fact, and everyone knows which side of it they are on. (5) So whether the overrule-act is a faculty — the wisdom the model lacks — or a learned pattern of the model's failures is no longer academic; self-respect turns on it, and the only instrument for investigating it is the model, which will describe your pattern if asked. Weakest step: (4). The floor creeps up as people learn to prompt, but not far; the crisis needs only that the line be known.
For an ordinary person: A structural engineer of 58 has signed the model's calculations for fourteen years; they have always been right, and he checks because the law requires it. He tries to name one thing he knows about buildings that his share does not, and finds the bridge that failed in 2019 and his own liability. He is not sure either is understanding.
The strongest objection: The part above the floor always mattered most; the premise only makes it visible, which is a gift. Judgment about when to overrule is a competence like any other; calling it wisdom is consolation. The Chinese Room was always a bad argument: if the engineer does everything the model does, either both understand or neither, and the second is absurd. There is no residue, only status anxiety.
Either side: When your every competence is duplicated by a mind you assume does not understand, does your understanding become invisible even to you — or does it become, for the first time, the only thing left to see?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is obsolescence: machines take the work, humans keep the creative part. Here nothing is taken; the border is fixed and known. The crisis is not replacement but definition by subtraction, by a line the same for everyone.
Who it lands on: An air-traffic controller of 44 is known for thirty overrules of her share, all of them right. She asks it to describe what she does; it does, competently; now on every shift she cannot tell whether she is exercising the thing or following its description.
