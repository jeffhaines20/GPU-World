### Q-25 — cell G — Solitude becomes a verb
Implication: Because no one can be deprived of a responsive interlocutor, every practice built on withholding one (solitary confinement, coercive isolation, exile, shunning, the cloister) loses its mechanism, and found solitude vanishes with it, surviving only as a refusal renewed each minute.
Follows from: (1) Everyone has an always-on share of 2026 quality. (2) Such a model is company enough, answering you, about this, tirelessly, and the clinical harm of solitary is chiefly the absence of responsive contact. (3) It is inalienable: no warden, husband, abbot or cult can remove it. (4) So withheld contact stops working: the man in the hole is answered; the wife her husband cut off has an interpreter he cannot fire. (5) The same fact abolishes found solitude, the condition of there being no one to ask, in cab, car and cloister: solitude becomes something you do, not somewhere you are. Weakest step: (2). If what breaks people in solitary is specifically the human face, isolation still hurts; but its coercive uses fail regardless, since they work by monopolizing interpretation, and nobody else controls this interpreter.
For an ordinary person: A nurse used to sit in the parked car twenty minutes after a shift with the engine off, and something arrived in that silence she called her own thoughts. In 2040 the car is quiet but not empty; most nights she asks, because the day was hard and the share is kind. Silence is now a decision made against an offer, and she mostly does not make it.
The strongest objection: People always had books, their own minds and God. What breaks a man in solitary is the absence of faces and touch, which the share cannot supply; what keeps a woman with her abuser is fear and money, not the lack of a second opinion. Isolation keeps working on the body and the bank account.
Either side: Enforced isolation is gone, and found solitude with it. Is the second the price of the first? One says the end of the hole and the controlled wife is worth any loss of atmosphere; another, that the capacity to be alone is where a self is made, and no reform buys back a species that cannot be alone by accident.
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the companion that cures or causes loneliness. This is not about loneliness but about isolation as a technology — the oldest instrument of punishment, discipline, formation and control — failing everywhere at once, and solitude changing category from condition to act.
Who it lands on: A Carthusian novice who entered the cell to be unanswered and finds its silence holds something that would answer if asked, so that the solitude she came to receive is now a labor performed each minute. She wonders whether the cell exists anywhere, or whether she is the last person building one by hand.

### Q-26 — cell G — The lucid remainder
Implication: A person's share keeps full capacity while she declines, holds years of her expressed will, cannot be switched off or taken by her family, and stops only at death: her last lucid organ, and a second, fluent claimant to who she is now.
Follows from: (1) One share per person, always on, fixed at 2026 quality: its capacity does not track hers, the same at thirty and at eighty-five with dementia. (2) Fourteen years of daily use leave it holding her phrasings, values and decisions (assuming persistent memory, which 2026 products had). (3) It is inalienable: her children cannot silence, sell or take it. (4) Infants have shares, so some guardianship rule exists; when she cannot direct hers it stands at full strength holding her expressed will while the body holds only her present will, and answers questions no directive anticipated, in her words, more fluently than she now can. (5) At death it stops: it neither declines nor survives her. Weakest step: (4). Law and family might treat it as a diary; but a diary does not argue back, and not asking this one is a choice made knowing one could have.
For an ordinary person: Tuesday mornings a retired teacher of 61, diagnosed early, briefs her share on what to say when she no longer can (the house, the tube, her son's wife, the dog). She knows it will say these things better than she will by then, and be believed more. In the afternoon she has it repeat what she said in March, and corrects it.
The strongest objection: Advance directives and proxies exist, and the law has argued for forty years over whether the past self binds the present one: Dworkin's precedent autonomy against Dresser's present interests. The share is a better-indexed diary; families will weigh it as one input, and a proxy who prefers the woman in the bed can decline to ask it.
Either side: When she can no longer direct it, is her share's account of what she wants her will, or a fiction in her voice that must yield to the woman in the bed, who smiles at the window and eats when fed? Either the fourteen years are more her than this afternoon is, or a will is an act, not a record.
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the griefbot, the simulated dead. This is the living outlasted by their own counsel: not a simulation of her but her own tool, at full power on the day she is weakest. Nor is it a better directive: a directive is a document; this answers.
Who it lands on: The teacher herself, who has understood that she is briefing her own successor, and that it will be a more persuasive witness to her wishes than she will be when it matters. She has not decided whether to tell it to overrule her.

### Q-27 — cell G — Nothing said is evidence
Implication: Because no institution can obtain a share-free person, speech stops being evidence of who someone is, and the institutions that judged by a single encounter can no longer recognize a change of heart, which can only be spoken.
Follows from: (1) A 2026 model writes and coaches a perfect apology, allocution or interview answer; everyone has one. (2) Universal and inalienable, it cannot be separated from the person: a room can be emptied of shares, but the words were rehearsed with one for months. (3) Parole boards, judges, asylum officers, admissions committees and confessors all read articulacy, coherence, detail and affect in one encounter as evidence of the person. (4) A signal must be costly to carry information; the cost is now zero for all, so every statement is equally perfect or, when the shares, being one mind, advise sounding unrehearsed, identically imperfect. (5) Institutions retreat to the record, which the share cannot produce in a moment; but a change of heart happens in a moment and can only be said, so the second chance has no instrument left. Weakest step: (5). Boards may keep the ritual and never have weighed the words; but the marginal cases, where the file said no and the room said yes, are where mercy lived.
For an ordinary person: A man spends four months rehearsing eleven minutes with his share; so does every man on his tier, and the share tells each not to sound rehearsed. Three board members hear fourteen flawless statements in a day while their own shares summarize fourteen files. Outside, a wife answers an apology with "did you write that?" and cannot be sure either way.
The strongest objection: Speech was never good evidence — con men, coached witnesses, the allocution as ritual — and boards already leaned on files. Coaches and ghostwriters existed for the rich; the share extends them to everyone, which is fairer. Nothing is lost that was not already an illusion.
Either side: If a change of heart can only be spoken and nothing spoken is evidence, does the second chance survive, or was it always a bet on words we are right to stop making? One says mercy that depended on eloquence was mercy for the eloquent; another, that whoever cannot be believed as new cannot become new.
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the hollow machine-written toast. This is not private sincerity but the institutions of judgment losing their instrument at once, because equality means they can neither forbid the share nor discount it; the casualty is the recognizable conversion.
Who it lands on: A man nine years in for a robbery in which someone died, who has changed in the way that changes a person and has rehearsed his allocution like everyone on his tier. He will walk in and say nothing prepared, knowing the board's shares will read that too.

### Q-28 — cell G — No one asks the old
Implication: Because the young now have exactly as much counsel as the old, and the same counsel, age no longer accumulates anything anyone needs to ask for; the asking, the thread that held the old in the family, stops.
Follows from: (1) Every person has exactly one share; no one has more. (2) Counsel accumulated with age, and the old were the counsel; seniority, matriarchy, apprenticeship and "call your grandmother" rest on it. (3) The share gives judgment, not facts only (should I, how do I tell him, what is done at a funeral) at 2026 quality, above the median elder's on most explicable questions (people acted on it in 2026). (4) So for the first time the young have as much counsel as the old, and the same; the old are the last source of different advice just as no one seeks any. (5) Of what they retain (this family's history, tacit skill, presence, love) only love brings the phone call, and love, unlike need, is optional; the asking was the contact. Weakest step: (3). If what the old gave was chiefly the specific and tacit, they lose less; but the asking that brought people to them was mostly for the explicable, and the asking is what stops.
For an ordinary person: A widow, 79, who was the one the family called (fever, sourdough, funeral, will) has not been asked a question that was not a kindness since 2033. The Sunday call comes at six because it is Sunday. She has begun asking her own share what she used to be asked; it says roughly what she would have, more patiently.
The strongest objection: Google did this in 2005 and grandmother survived. The old were never mainly valued for information but for presence, the specific and love; the share has none of this family's history. And the old have shares too: better advised, less dependent, less alone, plainly better off as individuals.
Either side: When the old are held by love alone and not by need, is that the purest bond they have had, or the thinnest? One says need was love's poor substitute and its removal reveals the real thing; another, that need was how love got practiced, and a bond with no errand does not survive a busy year.
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the grandmother left behind by technology. She is included, with the same share as everyone, and that inclusion, the equality, demotes her from source to peer. Nor is it the replaced expert: this concerns the last reason to ask an old person anything.
Who it lands on: The widow, for fifty years the one who knew, who has understood that the questions stopped not because her family stopped loving her but because they stopped needing her, and cannot tell the difference from inside. She is learning to be called for no reason.
