# The questions-first funnel (DEC-015), framed before any implication exists

**What this is.** The human, after two premise passes, wants the question found before the story: "think of questions
first." This funnel is outside the kit's skills; no check reads its outputs; its rules are declared here before any
agent runs, and its records stay in this folder. The output is five implications, argued and critiqued, for the human
to choose from; a third premise pass then writes to the chosen question.

**The premise, granted.** The world contains one GPU per person, equally distributed among all people, with the power
and other resources to run them. Concretely: every human has, always on, the inference of one frontier model of 2026
quality, faster and cheaper than in 2026 but never much better and never superhuman. Each person's share is theirs;
thinkers assume it is inalienable and cannot be accumulated, sold or pooled beyond the person, unless an implication
is specifically about what happens when it can, in which case the card says so. Business as usual otherwise: no
singularity, no collapse. The world of 2040.

**The cut.** Seven cells, one thinker each, in isolation, four implications each, twenty-eight in all (Q-01 to Q-28).

| Cell | Domain | Narrowing |
|---|---|---|
| A | Philosophical | knowledge, truth, mind, selfhood, expertise, understanding, what it is to know or to think when a competent unwise mind is always at hand |
| B | Moral | responsibility, desert, virtue, care, honesty, promise, forgiveness, what a person owes and can be blamed for when advice is universal and equal |
| C | Economic | value, labour, wages, rent, ownership, money, trust, growth, what is scarce when cognition is free and equal |
| D | Political | power, legitimacy, the state, law, voting, war, borders, what rule and consent become when every citizen is equally advised |
| E | Religious | faith, prayer, authority, scripture, ritual, meaning, the afterlife, conscience, what a church or a believer is when every person has an interlocutor |
| F | Social | family, friendship, status, class, community, language, childhood, old age, what holds people together or apart when nobody is short of counsel |
| G | The one nobody has named | an implication from any domain that the contest's list and ordinary commentary have not reached, argued from the equality itself: one each, no one more than another |

**The card shape**, fixed:

```
### Q-NN — <cell> — <a title of convenience>
Implication: <one sentence>
Follows from: <the chain from the premise to the implication, step by step, each assumption named, and which step is weakest>
For an ordinary person: <what it changes in a day of a particular life, concretely>
The strongest objection: <the best case that it does not follow, or does not matter, made honestly>
Either side: <the question, stated so that a thoughtful person could hold either answer>
Not the obvious version: <the cliché nearest to it, and how this differs>
Who it lands on: <a person for whom this is a crisis, in two sentences>
Status: generated
```

**The review.** Two reviewers, fresh contexts, batches of at most six, order shuffled per batch with recorded seeds,
neither seeing the other. Each implication is scored 1 to 5 on five axes: **importance** (does it matter at the scale
of a society or a life), **rigour** (does it follow from the granted premise, or only from a stronger one), **novelty**
(beyond the contest's list and common commentary), **arguability** (a thoughtful person could hold either side), and
**story** (is there a person for whom it is a crisis, with a want and a price). Each also names, in one sentence, the
weakest step in the argument. Reviewers answer, per batch: which implication is the most important, which the most
novel, and which does not survive.

**The iteration.** The ten strongest by the ranking below, plus any disputed card, go each to a fresh reviser with the
two reviewers' critiques of that card only. The reviser strengthens the argument, narrows the claim, or reports that
it does not survive, and rewrites the card. The revised ten are reviewed again by two fresh reviewers in batches of
five, and ranked again. The five offered to the human are the top five of that ranking; a card that a reviser or
reviewer says does not survive is named to the human as such.

**The ranking, declared now.** As for the premises: a card's total is the sum of five axes; ranking A pulls each
reviewer's total halfway toward that reviewer's pool median and takes the mean; ranking B is the highest single-reviewer
total; a card is disputed at a gap of five or more; ties on A are broken by cell coverage, then by the reviewers'
most-important picks, then by importance plus novelty.

**Cost.** About 1.6M tokens for the whole funnel (seven thinkers, ten reviews, ten revisions, four re-reviews). About
4.45M has been spent; this leaves about 4M for the spine, the draft, the revision rounds and the final, which is one to
two revision rounds at the measured cost; a third round would need the token cap raised.
