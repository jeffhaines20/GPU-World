### Q-17 — cell E — The Confessional After the Hearing
Implication: Where confession is a sacrament, the machine takes over everything in it except the absolution, and so shows, at scale, whether absolution was ever more than the relief of being heard.

(2) Examining one's conscience — naming the sin, its number, its gravity, what excuses it — is the shaming conversation people already take to such a model before any human (assumption from 2024–26 usage). (3) So by 2040 the penitent reaches the grille having done, with a better-read partner, everything a confessor does but say the formula: heard, counseled, relieved. (4) The tradition itself says absolution is God's act through an ordained man, not a feeling. (5) But the sacrament's felt efficacy was always bundled with the hearing, and the bundle has never been split for everyone at once. Weakest is (3): that the machine's hearing gives the priest's relief and not a paler cousin; if it feels like nothing, nothing changes.

### Q-20 — cell E — The Idle Share
Implication: Because the share is one per human and no one else's to use, the state must say where a human begins and ends, and its answer — a machine idling at public expense for someone who cannot use it — is an ensoulment doctrine the churches did not write.

(3) A share for someone who cannot use it does nothing but exist: the first public good spent on a person purely for being one, the form the traditions used for the soul. (4) The traditions' own lines — 120 days in fiqh, conception in Rome, brain death disputed among poskim — each now have a rival at the bedside. Weakest is (3): if families feel nothing when the share starts or stops, it is rounding, not a mark.
