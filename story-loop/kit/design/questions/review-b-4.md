Q-27 | importance=4 | rigour=3 | novelty=4 | arguability=4 | story=5
importance: Mercy, parole, asylum and the recognizability of moral change touch a whole life at its hinge and a society's entire apparatus of second chances, which is large enough.
rigour: Steps (1)-(4) follow cleanly from universality and inalienability, but (5) smuggles in that boards previously weighed spoken words heavily enough that their loss destroys the second chance, and the card's own concession that boards "may never have weighed the words" is not answered so much as waved at with "marginal cases."
novelty: The costly-signalling frame applied to contrition rather than to essays or cover letters, with the twist that equality forbids both banning and discounting the share, is a genuinely fresh angle on a subject usually discussed as cheating.
arguability: Both halves are live — that eloquence-gated mercy was class privilege, and that a person who cannot be believed as new cannot become new — and neither collapses on inspection.
story: A man nine years in, changed, choosing to walk in with nothing prepared while knowing the board's shares will read even that, is a want, a price and a room all at once.
weakest step: Step (4)'s claim that the cost of the signal is "now zero for all" conflates the cost of producing eloquent words with the cost of producing four months of rehearsal and the disposition to do it — effort remains costly and differentially distributed, so the signal is degraded, not annihilated.

Q-01 | importance=5 | rigour=4 | novelty=4 | arguability=4 | story=4
importance: If corroboration becomes structurally unavailable, every institution that runs on independent checks — courts, science, journalism, medicine — loses its error-correction, which is about as society-scale as implications get.
rigour: The one-model-one-error-distribution argument follows tightly from the premise as granted, though step (3) overstates: context conditioning and sampling do produce partially decorrelated errors, and the card's objection paragraph raises this and then does not rebut it.
novelty: "Not an echo chamber but unlike-minded people receiving one verdict and mistaking it for agreement" is a sharper formulation than the standard homogenization or hallucination commentary, though monoculture-of-models is discussed in the field.
arguability: The question of whether to trust your own eyes against a mind that is usually right is genuinely two-sided, and the objection about surviving epistemic monopolies has real force.
story: The historian with a letter she cannot authenticate against one opinion held eight billion times is a person in a place with a price, but her crisis is more an epistemic predicament than a dramatic want.
weakest step: Step (3)'s "do not err independently" is asserted at full strength when the honest version is "err with high correlation," and the whole conclusion about corroboration going extinct needs the full strength, not the honest version.

Q-08 | importance=4 | rigour=3 | novelty=5 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: Promising is load-bearing for marriage, contract, parenthood and every relation built on the future, so a change in what a promise conveys reaches whole lives, even if the mechanism is intimate rather than institutional.
rigour: The card names its own weakest step honestly, but the reply ("mediocre beats nothing, and a number, once seen, is not unseen") is a psychological claim doing philosophical work, and step (1) still asks a 2026 model with a person's history to forecast individual behaviour better than the person's own spouse, which the premise does not grant.
novelty: Splitting the promise into prediction and undertaking and observing that universal forecasting strips the first and exposes the second is an argument I have not seen made about AI, and it is put with real precision.
arguability: "The only honest promise there has ever been" versus "a lie two people agreed to tell each other" are both fully defensible and the card gives each its strongest form, including the Arendt/Nietzsche defence.
story: The father at thirty-five percent, asked by his daughter what the number was, having to choose between the number and the promise, is the most compressed crisis in the batch.
weakest step: Step (2)'s assumption that the promisee's share can produce a "comparable number" about someone else requires her share to have his history, which the premise's one-share-per-person privacy structure does not obviously supply.

Q-18 | importance=4 | rigour=3 | novelty=5 | arguability=4 | story=5
importance: The internal argumentative life of religious traditions shapes how billions live, and the specific claim about ijma' and machloket is a real structural stake rather than a niche curiosity.
rigour: Step (3) is the load-bearing one and the card concedes it may fail — prompts differ, people ask as members of schools, and a 2026 model that is good at Aramaic will happily produce Brisker and Litvish and Salafi readings on request — so the convergence may simply not occur.
novelty: "An unauthorized consensus: ijma' with no isnad, no ijaza, no faith" is the most original formulation in the batch, and the observation that no tradition can build a bigger interpreter than its least learned member already has is exactly the premise's equality doing philosophical work.
arguability: The "common reader it prayed for" versus "the argument it was made of" framing is genuinely balanced, though the card's sympathies visibly sit with the rosh yeshiva.
story: Rav Adler watching the beit midrash go quiet, unable to tell unity from ending, with the only partner who could help him think it through being the cause, is a person, a place and an unpayable price.
weakest step: Step (2)'s parenthetical "assumption: convenience beats deference" is asserted rather than argued, and in precisely the populations the card is about — where deference to a posek is itself the religious act — it is the least safe assumption available.

Q-09 | importance=5 | rigour=2 | novelty=4 | arguability=4 | story=4
importance: If the wage splits into a uniform rent and a small human premium, that reorganizes income distribution, unions, redundancy and the meaning of work for every adult, which is the largest scope in the batch.
rigour: The card admits that pooling is the premise's open question and then simply takes the reading that makes firms possible, and it additionally assumes the rent is large, that engine-hours are the binding constraint, and that the human premium is small — four assumptions stacked, of which the premise grants none.
novelty: Paine's universal proprietorship delivered by accident and received as an insult, with the rentier arriving through ordinary payroll rather than through UBI, is a fresh inversion of the standard automation story.
arguability: "The end of the labour question or the end of the worker" is a real fork, and the objection that the premium is the whole point is strong enough to be a live position rather than a straw man.
story: Tomasz paid most of his salary for no duties and unable to say what he does for a living is a good image, but he is more an illustration of the economics than a man with a want he is pursuing.
weakest step: The pooling concession — if shares cannot be contracted out, the entire economy described does not exist, and if they can, the premise's non-transferability is dead and the card is arguing from a different world than the one granted.

MOST IMPORTANT: Q-01, because the loss of independent corroboration undermines the error-correction of every institution that judges anything, including the ones the other four cards are about.
MOST NOVEL: Q-18, because the collapse of sacred disagreement into an authorless consensus with no chain of transmission is a consequence of equal access that nobody in the AI conversation has named.
DOES NOT SURVIVE: Q-09, because it purchases its entire economy by resolving the premise's pooling prohibition in its own favour and then stacking three further unargued assumptions on top, so its conclusions follow from a stronger and different premise than the one granted.
