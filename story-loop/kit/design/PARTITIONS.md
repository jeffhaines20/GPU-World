# The premise space, cut before anything is generated

Written before the first premise exists, so that six writers in six fresh contexts do not all land in
the same corner. Each cell goes to exactly one `premise-writer`, which sees its own cell and nothing
else.

**Axis 1 — the source of strangeness.** Where the story's science-fictional pressure comes from.

1. A technology and what it costs the people who use it.
2. A biology: another way of being alive, or ours altered.
3. A cosmology: the shape or age or indifference of the universe.
4. A social arrangement made possible, or unavoidable, by a change.
5. An alteration of physics or of time.
6. An alteration of mind: memory, identity, attention, what a self is.

**Axis 2 — the register**, from the brief. Every cell inherits it.

**The cells.** One per source of strangeness. The orchestrator writes the six here with any
narrowing the brief implies, and each writer receives one line: its cell, the register, the taste
brief, and the length target.

| Cell | Source of strangeness | Narrowing from the brief | Writer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A technology and what it costs the people who use it | The personal model as an object in one life: what having one costs a particular person, in money, attention, dependence, standing, or in what they can no longer do alone. Its ordinary success, not its failure. | premise-writer, cell 1 |
| 2 | A biology: another way of being alive, or ours altered | A body or a life stage changed by living beside a tireless interlocutor: children raised with one, the old, the ill, the pregnant, the sleepless, the doctor in every pocket, what a human animal becomes when it is never bored and never unaccompanied. | premise-writer, cell 2 |
| 3 | A cosmology: the shape or age or indifference of the universe | The world's oracle stopped learning in 2026 and the world did not: eight billion copies of one mind that knows the world as it was, faster and cheaper every year and never wiser, in a future it will not quite recognise. Scale, sameness, the indifference of the fixed thing; what it is like to live under a sky that answers. | premise-writer, cell 3 |
| 4 | A social arrangement made possible, or unavoidable, by a change | An institution this makes necessary or absurd: a court, a marriage, a school, a church, a union, a village council, a prison, a border, an election, a market, a funeral — in the places the contest calls the oft-ignored developing world as readily as anywhere else. The arrangement is the strangeness; the model is only what made it. | premise-writer, cell 4 |
| 5 | An alteration of physics or of time | The physical and temporal facts of the thing: eight billion GPUs are objects, hot, powered, sited somewhere, owned by someone; a mind that answers in milliseconds changes what an hour is and what waiting is; a world in which every human day can be doubled, delegated, or spent twice. | premise-writer, cell 5 |
| 6 | An alteration of mind: memory, identity, attention, what a self is | What a self is when something knows you completely and has all day: outsourced inner speech, a memory that is not yours, identity as a transcript, attention as the last scarce thing, the end of being unobserved even by yourself. | premise-writer, cell 6 |

## What every cell inherits

**The register**, from the brief: quiet and strange, with one hard idea. The human's taste in their own words:
"philosophical fiction that is well written and makes you think differently about something you had assumed."
Their instruction: work backward from what a winning story must have — it will have to be somewhat surprising, or
at least not predictable, and have some important insight. Length about 3,500 words. An unhappy or unresolved
ending is allowed. Never: a twist ending that cancels the story; a lecture in dialogue.

**The premise**, from the contest, which every card must live inside: frontier AI progress stops on 1 September
2026. Models get faster and cheaper but never superhuman and never much better. By 2040 every human on Earth has
around-the-clock access to the equivalent of a frontier model, and there are about eight billion GPUs. Business
as usual: no singularity, no takeover, no collapse. The contest asks for the mundane future, evenly distributed,
and neither a simple utopia nor a simple nightmare.

**Already done, and not to be worn as a hat.** Forster, "The Machine Stops"; Stephenson, *The Diamond Age* (the
Primer: the tutor that raises a child — one of the contest's judges wrote it); Chiang, "The Lifecycle of Software
Objects" and "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling"; Kritzer, "Cat Pictures Please"; qntm, "Lena"; Ishiguro,
*Klara and the Sun*; the film *Her*; *Black Mirror* ("Be Right Back", "Nosedive", "White Christmas", "Joan Is
Awful"); Asimov, "The Last Question"; Bradbury, "The Veldt"; Vonnegut, "EPICAC"; Gwern, "It Looks Like You're
Trying To Take Over The World" (another judge wrote it). And the obvious versions, which editors have read: the
AI that is secretly conscious; the lonely person who loves their AI; the panopticon; the tutor that makes everyone
brilliant; the Luddite who refuses one; the person who cannot tell human from machine; the AI that says goodbye;
the person who discovers their friend was an AI all along.

**The rule for the four cards.** Start from a person in trouble because of the idea. Four different stories, not
one story four ways. The turn must be a turn. The last image must be an image. The Question field is a question
the story leaves open.

## How the funnel will rank, declared before any score exists

Two `premise-judge` agents score each surviving card 1 to 5 on five axes (the idea, the person, the turn, the
prose where a probe exists, the aftertaste). A card's total per judge is the sum of the axes it was scored on,
scaled to a 25-point total where "no probe" left an axis unscored. Ranking A, the **shrunk** ranking: each judge's
total is pulled halfway toward the pool median of that judge's totals, and the card's score is the mean of the two
shrunk totals. Ranking B, the **highest single-judge** total. A card is **disputed** when the two judges' totals
differ by five points or more — a full point per axis. Six are probed: five from ranking A with no more than two
from one cell, and the sixth the highest card on ranking B that ranking A did not take. Three are offered: two
from ranking A after the probe round, and the most disputed surviving card.
