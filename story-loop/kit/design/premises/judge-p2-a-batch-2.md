READ BEFORE: P-52 and P-34 are both effectively Asimov's "Franchise" reworked, and both cards say so; P-34 additionally carries the New England assembly of Shirley Jackson's "The Lottery." P-41 is close to the middle movement of Achebe's Arrow of God, and its Sadiq is a softened Ezeulu. P-30 is the teacher-and-prodigy pairing of Stoppard's Arcadia crossed with the withheld correction of Malamud's "The Magic Barrel" school of mentor stories. P-47 and P-36 I have not read before in any form.
WOULD READ / WOULD NOT FINISH: I would want P-36 at five thousand words, because its idea is doing political work no other card here touches and Rowena's want is small enough to carry it without argument; I would not finish P-52, because the forecast, the position change and the man on the porch resolve into their moral in the first thousand words and the remaining four thousand would be watching a candidate feel bad.
MOST NOVEL QUESTION: P-47, whether value under free cognition pools in the mind or in the physics beneath it — so that a community is better off as the landlord of a boiler than the owner of a brain — is the most novel, because it is the only question here about rent, substrate and the tax base rather than about voting, knowing or being watched, and it arrives at a defensible answer no one in the batch would call a happy one.
