### P-13 — cell 4 — La Contraria
Conceit: In a Sierra Norte municipality governed by assembly under usos y costumbres, where the model's recommendation on every question is known in each house before the meeting, the cargo ladder has grown a new rung — la Contraria, an unpaid year of obligatory objection to the recommendation, so that the assembly's vote counts as a decision — and the rare objector who wins skips the rest of the ladder and sits for life with the caracterizados.
Who: Teresa Cruz, 38, the primary-school teacher, five months into her year as Contraria. She wants to finish the year without winning: she thinks the model is right about nearly everything, she has watched what the village did to the family of the last Contraria who won (the road of 2033, built by tequio against the recommendation and washed out twice since), and she does not want to spend the rest of her life at the elders' table as the woman who beat the machine.
Obstacle: The question this month is the state's offer to fold the village primary into the district town, with a bus; the recommendation says accept, with numbers; Teresa is the teacher and agrees with it. But she cannot argue badly — she is the best-spoken person in the municipality and the office has rules about substance — and the assembly, old and afraid of a village with no children in it on weekdays, is listening for permission rather than for argument; the better she objects, the more permission she gives, and the cargo cannot be resigned.
Turn: She objects as well as she can because she cannot do otherwise — the recommendation measures the children at eighteen, the village is measured at seven in the morning — and the assembly refuses the offer; under its own terms it lapses, the district draws its bus route without them, and Teresa is seated with the caracterizados the same night. The post she would have lost either way is now hers for as long as the school stands, which is now on her.
Last image: The road below the village at ten past seven, where the district's bus does not stop: empty, dust unraised; up the hill the school door open on eleven desks, and Teresa's own, with the recommendation she printed for the assembly folded under one leg to stop it rocking.
Question: When the better answer is already in every house, what is an assembly buying when it votes the other way, who pays for it, and is the objector who handed it permission the one who decided?

### P-16 — cell 4 — The Book of Minutes

Obstacle: The assembly has refused three times, and not because it thinks the sentence false. Every loan, marriage and planting in the community for eleven years has been advised on the book as it stands; the model cannot weigh a new line against an old one, only read both; and the present holder, Don Faustino, is not a thief but the son of whoever was.
Turn: She writes it at night, dated and signed. By morning Faustino's model, asked what to plant, mentions the dispute; his daughter's, asked about a loan against the parcel, flags the title; he comes to Rosa's door, not angry, and asks her to read him the sentence in a voice. The assembly meets, strikes the line, and minutes the striking — and from then on every model in the community answers every question about that parcel with both sentences, the claim and its cancellation, and will for as long as the book is the book.
