She put the last flag in the holder and turned it so the cloth hung the right way. Villum had his headphones round his neck and the spoon standing in what was left of the skyr.

"The bar in March, when you were five months old, is three minutes and eleven seconds of me feeding you at ten past three in the morning and telling you I didn't want you," she said. "I've taken it out, and it's gone, and I'm not letting you hear it."

On the shelf above the radiator the two lights took it down, hers amber, his the white it had been since the hospital. He ate the spoonful he had been holding.

"Is it on yours?"

"Yes."

He nodded once, the way he nodded at the price of something, and took the bowl to the sink and went up. She heard him on the stairs and then she heard nothing, and she sat with the flags until the dishwasher stopped.

In the morning there were buns and coffee, and at eight minutes to eight, the minute on his birth certificate, the light on the shelf went from the household white to the blue he had picked in the summer. He went straight to March and put the headphones on over the bar, and the counter under it started, and she stood at the worktop and watched.

She did not need the counter. At nought she was telling him she did not want him. At fifty seconds she was telling him about the hospital, the door on the third floor she had stood at with him in the car seat. He looked up at her there, and down again. At two minutes she was telling him about the cot Mette had already bought. His face was the face he had for music, the one that went nowhere. At three minutes eleven the bar ended and the record came back, and she watched him hear her, fourteen years younger, say *sådan, sådan* while he brought the milk up on her shoulder.
