The passbook was on the kitchen table because she had brought it home in July and never taken it back. Eleven lakh forty in the fixed deposit, five lakh and some in savings, the entries in three hands and the earliest of them hers. The fan clicked on its long chain. Rajan had been asleep since ten.

She had typed the question before, on Biju's phone, to see what the members saw. Tonight it was her own phone, her own model, her own figures. I have sixteen lakh at the cooperative bank. I retire in March. Should I move it.

It answered in the paragraphs she had already read. The insured amount was five lakh per depositor per bank. The rubber plant had closed in July; about a third of the bank's borrowers had drawn their wages there, and the September filing showed the instalments not yet received. Nothing in the record said the bank would fail. She might wish to hold no more than the insured amount here until the next quarter's figures were published. Breaking the fixed deposit early would cost about four thousand rupees. Kerala Bank, the state cooperative, was paying six point one for a year. It could prepare the transfer, if she liked.

She typed: The instalments come tomorrow. I know every one of these people.

I understand, it wrote. I don't have anything that confirms that. If they come, you can move the money back on the 6th at no charge.

Rain started on the roof, stopped, started. She took her glass to the sink and rinsed it and came back and sat. Kerala Bank's app wanted her thumb, then a code sent to the phone she was holding. She left five lakh, which was the guarantee, and sent the rest. The screen said Successful at 23:51 and offered a receipt, and she saved it.

In the morning Biju would print the outward list and bring it to her desk at nine, as he had every morning since August, and her own name would be the last on it, with the time beside it.
