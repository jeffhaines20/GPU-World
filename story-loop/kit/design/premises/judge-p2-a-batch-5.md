idea: The distinction between secrecy and obscurity is a genuinely two-sided question — one can hold that a right not to be read is a right to hypocrisy at scale, or that obscurity was always the whole of practical privacy — and it is enacted by a kettle boiling rather than announced.

P-46 | idea=5 | person=4 | turn=5 | prose=no probe | aftertaste=4
idea: "When what capital wants is your legibility rather than your labour, what does a union withhold" is the freshest formulation in the batch, and the price of the answer — the help degrades exactly because it no longer knows you — is a mechanism, not a theme.
person: Marisol's want is precise and material (7C, and the rent that rises by exactly what she can bear) and the detail of the aide out-earning the accountant is excellent, though her inner life is thinner than P-28's or P-31's and rests mostly on the closing shame of being read by neighbours.
turn: Sixty-one households merge and cannot un-merge, because unmerging would show the hall who blinked first — an irreversibility with a real causal lock, not just an emotional one.
prose: no probe
aftertaste: The taped sheet on the fourth-floor landing with the custody hearing above and the mole below is the image I would carry, along with the idea that a won strike can cost you the only thing that ever knew you.

P-26 | idea=4 | person=5 | turn=5 | prose=no probe | aftertaste=5
idea: The marked-redaction rule is a beautifully specific piece of law and the question of whether the household holds privacy jointly is genuinely two-sided, but the strangeness is closer to the contest's own panopticon prompt (merely reversed) than the others, and the philosophical reach depends on a legal design more than on a social one.

prose: no probe
aftertaste: A boy listening to three minutes of counted silence with headphones on, hearing what was taken out, is the single image in this batch I would be unable to put down tomorrow.

READ BEFORE: P-26 — very close in furniture and moral shape to Ted Chiang's "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling," and adjacent to Black Mirror's "The Entire History of You" (Jesse Armstrong); P-31 — Brian Friel's *Translations*, and the standardization strand of Ken Liu's "The Bookmaking Habits of Select Species" / the language-death essay tradition; P-38 — the shape of Shirley Jackson's "The Lottery" as the card admits, plus Rawls's veil in fictional dress; P-28 and P-46 I have not effectively read before, though P-28 shares a room with *The Lives of Others* (Henckel von Donnersmarck) without repeating it.

WOULD READ / WOULD NOT FINISH: I would want P-28 at five thousand words, because the bylaw scene needs the room to be silent in and its argument is one I cannot settle; I would not finish P-38, because its conclusion is visible from the premise and the meeting-room vote is a debate I have already had.

MOST NOVEL QUESTION: P-46 — what a union withholds when what is wanted from you is legibility rather than labour, and what solidarity costs when the machine's help was only ever good because it knew you alone; novel because it moves the surveillance question from policing to pricing, and then finds a cost that is not oppression but the loss of intimacy with a tool, which is a bargaining problem no labour story has had to hold before.
