### From REV-09 (4 findings carried)

REV-09 F1: MINOR — The ending settles the story's question through three refusals, and each one reaches the reader as a pointer to a reason rather than the reason itself.
- The sentence that carries the turn: "hers, who had left him in the bowl to be found." "Him" has just meant the father ("You wanted him to have to come up"). And nobody was left in the bowl: Kit's bud was the one that was gone.
- "she did not ask it, for the reason she had given Kit no sentence at six." The reader has to rebuild that reason from the scene before, where it was itself only implied.
- "She did not go down. Going down would make Friday hers."

Read with their reasons, these are Tess choosing neither to give nor to receive a sentence about what someone is. Read without them, they are the family's old silence repeating in her ("nothing said"; "That was the whole of it, then or after"). The story is richer for allowing both readings, but the first has to be reachable on a first reading. If it isn't, the ending shrinks to a portrait of people who don't talk, and the choice between one sentence and three gets lost. A reader would notice the strain at the turn and again at the close. This is a finding about whether the idea is earned, not about the idea.

REV-09 F2: MINOR — Outside the family, the people the reader meets in the world of buds are props, and they tip a balance the rest of the story keeps level.
- The client's plan comes "from his ear to his mouth with nothing of his own added."
- His boy "looked at her and at nothing, listening."
- The child indoors says "all right, then, to nobody in the room."

The contest itself is fair. "In three" is a sound plan, "Whole" is a better one, and the wire, which neither man saw, levels it. But the only bud-users the reader sees for any length of time have no self. The few moments the client has of his own ("He'll see what's best"; "looked from the wire to his boy and back") all go to proving the father right or to making the wire's point. That puts life with the bud, outside the house, at the "simple nightmare" end of the premise. It makes the father's bowl look right by default rather than by argument. I am not objecting to the view that the bud empties people out. I am saying this scene asserts it instead of earning it, because the client is given nothing of his own to lose. A reader would notice that he is a type.

REV-09 F3: MINOR — The mother is essential to the story and carries no weight in it.
- The bowl "had started the same week" she went to Manchester.
- Kit's Manchester train times are the only other trace of her.
- The bud's one offer to talk about her ("It may also be worth noting that the earliest entries follow a change in the household") gets "'Go on,' she said, and it went on." The narration does not go on with it.
- Tess was eight when her mother left, and the story gives her no memory of her mother.
- The audit Tess built to find out "what the years in this house had cost her" grades only her father. The story never shows that narrowing as her choice.

The turn reads the bowl as the wish of a man who wanted his children to have to come to him. That is the wish of a man who had been left, and the leaving is the one thing the reader is kept from. The silence could be Tess's own, something she has grown round the way the beech grew round the wire. But the text shows it as the narration's silence, not hers: she asks the bud to go on, and it is the telling that cuts away. This is not a request for a mother subplot. The ending rests on what the bowl means: the father's Friday "Right" and the gift to Kit both depend on it. That meaning is stated at the turn while its root is held back. A reader would notice the gap.

REV-09 F4: NIT — The curve is the one "figure she could read" that the audit produces, and it plots things the audit doesn't contain. The entries are "each an occasion on which her father had decided something about her," yet the curve has "Kit's up in the top right." A related small question: an earpiece "drew" a shape that she "looked at" in a dark room, so where is the figure? It's small, but this is where the workings of the idea show.


### From REV-10 (10 findings carried)

REV-10 F1: MINOR — The audit cannot hear the lights, and the draft never says why. This is a missing mechanism.

The story's whole undecidable rests on the machine knowing that day only from her ("She had told it everything she knew about that day"; three general reasons, "different ones each night"). But the draft's own rules put a listening bud in her ear in the van:
- "outside the house you wore it, like everyone";
- a bud hears what is near it even from the bowl ("because it had heard the keys from the bowl"; "with nothing to do but hear the door and the keys");
- it comments on what it overhears ("He's found it").

The moor and the van at Mytholmroyd are outside the house. A reader who knows the record-and-replay stories (Chiang's "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling"; Black Mirror's "The Entire History of You") will ask at the Thursday reveal why she does not simply replay it. The page has no answer, so the reader must supply one: either she had taken it out, or buds keep nothing. The story's distance from both works, and the one thing only it does, rest on that sentence never having been heard by a machine.

One clause on the moor or in the van fixes it. Two ways, with different costs:
- She had taken it out herself. This leans on one of tonight's three reasons ("she had wanted to be on her own") and so partly settles her motive.
- His hand took it, as on the first night. This keeps the motive open and makes the lights the second time he took it.

REV-10 F2: MINOR — At six on Thursday the draft says its discovery aloud, and the turn then leans on the saying.
- **The best moment.** "The sentence rose in her, in her father's shape... she heard whose wish was in it: hers... and so perhaps his, at the lights" is the best moment in the story.
- **The purpose clause.** The clause after it is "the wish of a man who had put every bud in the house in a bowl so that his children would have to come to him". It states the bowl's purpose as a fact about the man. The "perhaps" hedges whose wish it was, not the purpose clause. It is the one thing about Rob the story otherwise lets the reader earn, from the week the mother went and "He had not come to her door".
- **The pointer at the bowl.** The withheld question is explained by "for the reason she had given Kit no sentence at six". That sends the reader back for a reason the six o'clock paragraph implies but never states.
- **Friday night.** The decision there closes on "Going down would make Friday hers", which the reader has to decode at the moment the turn's meaning is fixed.

Steelman: the insight rises "in her father's shape", so it is one more father, and it is hedged. That holds for "perhaps his". It does not hold for the purpose clause, or for the two pointers, which treat the insight as settled. The cost is that the reader is handed, inside Tess's head, a reading of Rob that the story's method says no one can reach. Friday then drifts toward correction: she will not make Kit have to come to anyone.

The fix:
- Cut the purpose clause and let "and so perhaps his, at the lights" stand.
- Give the reader something to see at the bowl and at the stairs in place of a cross-reference.

REV-10 F3: MINOR — The curve's scope contradicts the audit's. The audit is "Forty-one entries, each an occasion on which her father had decided something about her". But the curve puts "her years down in the bottom left and Kit's up in the top right", and the bud explains it by "a second child being easier than the first". If every entry is about her, Kit has no points on the curve. The curve is the story's one picture of "the child her father learned on", so a reader who tries to see it hits the contradiction two paragraphs after the definition. Either the audit takes in decisions about Kit, or the corners are named by her own ages.

REV-10 F4: MINOR — "He would not have." The second-to-last sentence is the gloss REV-07's sweep asked to keep off the page. It turns the last image into a verdict on Rob's character: he is the man who does not check, set against the daughter who audited him. That leans the close toward the noble refuser at the very moment the ear goes red. "Her father did not check it. He was looking at the rim of Kit's ear, which was going red" does all the work without it.

REV-10 F5: MINOR — The narration walks in the audit's own gait.
- Every remembered entry closes on its grade and a short sentence of her going along with it:
  - the party: "Every run graded it worse than the bud, and the bud said why in the same words every time, and she had never once argued with it";
  - Halifax: "Every run graded that drive comparable to typical advice. She had not asked it twice";
  - the moor: run four, run nine, run ten, tonight's.
- Every habit arrives dated to its wound: "since she was" small, nine, fourteen, fifteen, eight and thirteen; "since August" three times; "since he was one".

By the Halifax drive a reader hears the cadence before the content.

The party is weakened by its own grading, which is the residue of REV-07 F3. It is still scored twice. And in the same breath as "Any scoring of a learner draws it", the bud's "the earliest entries follow a change in the household" excuses it before it is shown.

Steelman: the narration is her ledger, and the form is the point. Keep the form, but break the cadence at least once. The party's cost already carries its case, so the party can lose its score.

REV-10 F6: MINOR — "She could do that sum." Tess was eight the week the bowl began and her mother went to Manchester. She has always held both halves, so the "sum" reads as a reveal staged for the reader, not a thought of hers. The narration then lays out the terms ("He had been one, and she had been eight, and the bowl had started the same week") one paragraph after the purpose clause has already given the answer. The trace works without the announcement: Kit gives the Manchester times "as if she had asked", and she "did not ask him what the times the other way were for".

REV-10 F7: NIT — "He did not talk in the van; he never had." But the lights happened in the van ("the van afterwards with the heater roaring... where he had turned in his seat and told her"). If the absolute is meant to mark the lights as the one exception, it has to say so. A reader who counts will stop here.

REV-10 F8: NIT — "Rob went up." This is the only place the narration calls him Rob. Everywhere else he is "her father" or "their father".

REV-10 F9: NIT — At four on Thursday: "went back up with hers still in... She went back up to see what her father would do." That is two "went back up"s in consecutive sentences, and the second carries the motive.

REV-10 F10: NIT — "Not for a twelfth run; she knew what that would give her" rebuts a reading the reader has not formed. The clause after it ("it was another man in the van with the heater on") is the good half.
