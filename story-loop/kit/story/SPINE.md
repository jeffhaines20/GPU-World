# The spine

One page. Written after the premise is chosen, read by the human at G2, and changed freely until then.

**Point of view and tense:** Third person, close on Tess, past tense. Tess, because the story's evidence is what one person hears through a wall and what a machine tells her in a whisper, and because she is the one who is wrong about where she stands; Rob and Kit stay on the far side of the wall, heard and not entered. Close third rather than first, because first person would hand over the verdict she reached, and close third can withhold what she withholds (the sentence she does not give Kit; the question she does not put to Rob) without lying. Past tense because the passage is in it. The narration notices as a night worker notices, never explains the world, and never says which of the eleven fathers was real.

**Who:** Tess Haworth, nineteen, of Hebden Bridge; a year of nights as a care assistant in Halifax, every care plan followed to the letter; on Saturday she takes the 9:14 to Leeds to train as a nurse, where she will be taught when not to. Her father, Rob, a tree surgeon, has kept every bud in the house in a bowl by the door for eleven years, since the week their mother went to Manchester (Kit one, Tess eight; one clause in the story, at six; no cause). In a valley where every other child has the one voice in its ear, that made the house the one place a child could not check a word said to it. She wants one bill she can believe before she goes: what the eleven years cost her. What she is wrong about: that the one practised on stands outside the practice as its judge. Her judgment was made in the house she is auditing; the harm and the gift are one thing and cannot be totalled apart; and she is a first-year at the bottom of her own curve, whose first departure from a plan is run on her brother and whose second is a decision made for him.

**Obstacle:** The audit holds still where the entry is plain and nowhere else: all eleven runs score the party worse than the bud and draw the same curve, and at the lights they give her eleven men, because the entry holds a man who saw her and a man pleasing himself. Every second opinion is the same bud in another ear, and nobody in the valley is not that voice but the man being graded. The only thing no rerun can vary is a live case, and the only live case in the house is Kit; to use Kit as the case is to do to him the thing she is costing.

**The turn:** Friday evening, at the bowl, in front of her father, Tess finds Kit's bud by trying the small ones in her own ear, puts hers back, and carries his upstairs, and Rob says "Right." and lets her; after that nothing can be said to Kit in that house that Kit cannot check, by his father's leave, and "Give it here" cannot be said again.

**Ending image:** Saturday, twenty past eight, from the door, one bag in her hand and her own bud in her ear, out of the bowl for the last time: Kit, bud in, telling Rob the 9:14 is four minutes late; Rob, not checking, because he would not, looking at the rim of Kit's ear, which has begun to go red. What it costs: the bill she came for, let go at the bowl, where the one man who could answer it stood in front of her and she did not ask; the question leaves the house with her, with what Kit knows the other way along the line. The night before Leeds without the bud she has used every night since August, wanting it and not going down, because going down would make Friday hers; and the girl the party made leaves fifty-four minutes before the 9:14 for a walk of ten. Rob has lost being the only one who knows his boy. And she has given Kit the three she wanted at thirteen and cannot now have; whether three from a bud or one from a father is the better gift, she will not be there to see.

**The question:** Was Rob entitled to learn, on Tess, to be the man who knew his children, or was that vanity charged to a child who could not be asked? And by Thursday, was Tess entitled to let Kit be the case, and by Friday to decide for him? The answer the story refuses: any total. The party (plainly wrong, and still in her at the door), the silence (plainly right, and unscoreable) and the lights (undecidable) do not sum; so nobody says whether the eleven years were a gift or a charge, or Friday a rescue or a spending. The form of the refusal is the lights: an audit that agrees with itself about the party and the curve gives the lights a different man every night, because the entry has two in it.

**Length target:** About 3,500 words; the contest's bounds are 1,000 and 5,000.

## Beats

1. Monday, three in the morning, her week off, a night worker's clock. An hour after Rob goes up, hers from the bowl; in her room she whispers because of the wall, and on the far side of it Kit's bed takes his weight. Run eleven, forty-one entries; first what every run agrees on: the party, worse than the bud; the curve, worse than the bud early and better late, her years bottom left and Kit's top right. Asked, the bud says any scoring of a learner draws that shape, that it cannot tell learning from luck or an easier second child, and that it may also be worth noting that the earliest entries follow a change in the household; she tells it to go on, and the omission is hers. Then the entry the runs will not hold still on: thirteen, out of Calder High at lunch and up Midgley Moor till dark, and at the lights in Mytholmroyd the one time he told her what she was; run four graded him well there, run nine badly, tonight's kinder than last night's; the bud offers three other reasons, and she cannot tell whose is hers. Since that night she has made herself someone nobody need fetch, bar once. At four, going down to put hers back, past Kit's in the bowl: his football boots off the step, knocked clean, under the hall table, as since she was fourteen.

2. Wednesday, a beech in a garden in Heptonstall. Tess is on the client's wall an hour before the van, as since she was nine; the party, at a scene's weight and with no verdict: a birthday in Mytholmroyd that had started before they got there; she asked to go home; sick in the van on the way; the rag from under the seat, "You'll be fine once you're in", and his hand on her back through the door. The earliest entry, scored worse than the bud by every run, whose reasonable father turns the van round at the sick. The client, bud in, relays its felling plan (the big limb, in three) in the voice that grades her father at night; she knows it for hers in another ear. On the ropes below, she has the limb's line before Rob says it, because she learned it from him. Rob lays it whole between the greenhouse and the trampoline; then his chain sparks on fence wire the tree closed over decades ago. "Grew round it." The client's had not mentioned wire. The van home, nothing said; in memory the drive at fifteen, fetched from Halifax without a word, which every run scores comparable to typical advice. The beat ends there.

3. Thursday, four in the morning, going down to put hers back: Kit's place in the bowl is empty, and she knows where he learned it. Her bud gives the plan before she has decided anything (wake him, return it, talk to him later) and the other (put it back herself). She does neither, and goes back up with hers still in, so that at five two places are empty under his keys, hers and Kit's. Once, in her own words, the story says why she goes back up: to see what her father does; and that Kit is asleep. Five: the passage, whole and as written, to "He had not come to her door once since August"; he had two doors to choose from and chose Kit's.

4. Thursday, six, Kit in her doorway. Not the story's question; his: "Did you see mine was gone?" "Yes." The sentence rises in her unasked, in her father's form ("You wanted him to have to come up"), and as it rises she hears whose wish is in it: hers, who left him to be found; and so perhaps his, at the lights, the wish of a man who put every bud in a bowl so that his children would have to come to him. It might be true of Kit; she does not give it. What Kit asks instead is whether the Leeds train goes from Hebden or you change at Halifax, and he knows before she does; he knows the ones the other way as well, to Manchester, where their mother has been since he was one. Tess can do that sum, and does, and does not ask him what for.

5. Friday evening, Rob home; his keys go into the bowl on top of three buds. In front of him she puts one of the small ones in her left ear, and it speaks, so it is hers; she puts it back and takes the other, silent in her ear, Kit's, and leaves hers in the bowl: the rule kept for herself by her own hand, the first night since August, and broken for Kit in front of the man who kept it. Rob has watched all of it. "Right," he says, and goes through; that is the whole of what is said, then or after. The question is in her mouth (was it true, at the lights) and she does not ask it. Kit's room: "Ask it. It'll give you three." She does not stay to see it go in. That night, through the wall, Kit whispers and she cannot hear what it says back. She wants hers, not for a twelfth run but for the small questions about tomorrow everyone asks theirs, and does not go down, because going down would make Friday hers. That is the price; "no run twelve" is not.

6. Saturday, twenty past eight, at the bowl for hers, and at the door with the bag: the last image, as written above, and nothing after it.

## What this story must not become

The nearest bad version is the one where the list gets read: Tess sets the audit before Rob, he defends himself or breaks, and they reconcile or part. Nothing from the audit is ever said to Rob. Its neighbours:

- The atrophy story and its mirror, the noble refuser. The bud is not always right (the wire, the drive) and not a fool; Rob's judgment is real (the beech) and was once plainly wrong (the party).
- The faulty instrument. Where the entry is plain, all eleven runs agree; the eleven fathers are what the lights permit. An audit a reader could average is the wrong audit.
- The 2025 swap. A bud silent in the wrong ear is a lock, and a phone has one. The story's 2040 is the client's bud being hers in another ear, the plan arriving at four before she has decided, and the bud grading her father through the wall as he speaks.
- The other-lives story. No run is a life she could have had; each grades the same life, and she envies none.
- The kindness reading. Thursday's plain sentence guards Thursday; Friday is guarded by being done in front of him and by its price, the night without the bud and the question let go. "No run twelve" is never the sacrifice, and Friday is not where Rob learns anything.
- The lecture. "Practised on", "raised or", "consent", "judgment", "vanity", "counsel" and "model" do not appear; the bud may say "good practice". No verdict on the drive: "It was the silence" is not said, nor anything like it.
- The bud as a character. It never wants, apologises or asks to be worn; it hedges, offers three, and grades.
- The stated metaphor. "Grew round it" is said once, of the wire; nobody says a child grows round a father. The curve is one image, once; the only percentage-shaped phrase is "comparable to typical advice".
- The mother story. One clause, at six, no scene, no cause; Kit's trains a trace, never a plot: Tess does the sum once and tells nobody.
- The house style of the five outlines: no cut from a hard line to an ambient sound; no "said it the way [a job] reads [a thing]"; no "did not remember"; no "looked at the window, where"; no verdict withheld at the end.
- Kit as a device. He is twelve, says "Stuff", "Bit" and "Dunno", and never asks the story's question.
