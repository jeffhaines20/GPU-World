# The spine

One page. Written after the premise is chosen, read by the human at G2, and changed freely until then.

**Title:** Working Height

**Point of view and tense:** Third person limited to Noor, past tense, close enough to hear the doppler with her and no closer. The one private fact is a sound only she heard: first person makes that a question about her honesty; a wider third, or a line inside Sanne's head, settles it. Close third lets the reader hear it with her at ten past two and lose it by day eight, and keeps Sanne opaque, so her listening at the end is her own act, not Noor's verdict. Past tense because the story is a record played back.

**Who:** Noor Brouwer, 24, last month of training in Zierikzee; concordance 97.1, the lowest in Zeeland. She wants to be a midwife who knows, not one who relays. What she is wrong about: that whether 02:14 was hers turns on whether she heard anything, and that an outcome, a recording or a face could settle it; the weight against her ear that night is not what the departure was made despite but what made it one. Marga Dekker, 65, trained from 1998 on women who had no counsel; six departures in nine years of scoring, all transfers, one the cord at Goes and five women moved for nothing; retires 31 January. Sanne, first baby: heard "Let me" through the bedroom door at 02:14 without knowing what it was; said "All right" at 02:16 against her own counsel; has not played the recording she has had since Wednesday; sent the complaint on day eight with Marga's name in it, knowing from the review what that does. Her error: that the review told her what happened to her.

**Obstacle:** The counsel is right far more often than she is. The simulator is the counsel's and plays only hearts the counsel can hear. The misses are the other test, and everyone has failed it: births a counsel heard nothing wrong in and something was, mixed blind with births where nothing was, played to every midwife to see who can pick the ones that went wrong; everyone scores at chance, so no recording has yet held an ear. A woman at eight centimetres, asked to be the test, would say stay. So the only place left is a live birth, and afterwards the record reads wanting, never hearing, so the one act that might have been judgment looks, on paper, exactly like the thing it might also have been.

**The turn:** Sanne, who sent the complaint that morning, asks not whether Noor was right but what she heard, and after the recording says "Again", and after that the review's fair account cannot be the whole of her daughter's birth again: she has begun spending the verdict the night bought her on the one thing it cannot hold.

**Ending image:** Sanne on the bed still on its blocks, her daughter asleep on her chest, Noor's phone at her ear. Her own counsel, asked, has given her the place to listen at and told her, fairly, that nothing is there and that no one hears such a thing on a recording better than chance. She listens anyway: not a test of Noor's ear but a woman listening to her own daughter's birth against her own counsel's finding, the whole weight told her first, as it was told Noor at 02:14. What it costs her: the clean account, sent this morning with Marga's name in it, is not the whole of her daughter's birth if she listens for herself. Noor gets no pardon: the complaint stands, her sign-off is a stranger's, she cannot hear it any more, and what she wants, watching, is what the review read: to hear it through Sanne, which is spending her again. She counts on Sanne's face toward the place where the heart comes back: the last sentence is hers, has that place as its object, no verb of hearing, and not the word "second".

**The question:** May an apprentice depart from the counsel on a stranger's birth, against everything the evidence says, to become a midwife whose judgment is her own, when the stranger can audit what it cost her and why she wanted it, but never what she heard? The answer refused: whether 02:14 was a judgment becoming Noor's own or vanity paid for by Sanne and Marga. It does not turn on whether she heard anything: a departure with the evidence on its side is agreement; one made against her counsel, Marga's ear, the misses and her own "No" is a departure because of that weight; and it can be judgment and wrong. Against: the counsel was right about all it could hear; a woman paid, and Marga's number; everyone scores at chance and Noor knew it; the want is unreformed in the last line. For: Marga's ear was made on women who had no counsel, which cannot be done again; if nobody departs, the ear stops where the counsel's does; Marga's cord says an ear is real; the one who paid asks for it, then listens against her own counsel.

**Length target:** About 3,500 words; hard bounds 1,000 and 5,000. The passage (about 330 words) sits inside beat 6 and is the register.

## Beats

1. **The bedroom under the dike, Zierikzee, night of 12 to 13 November 2040.** Sanne on the bed on its hired blocks, at working height; Joost at her head; Marga in the chair by the door, the bag beside it; Noor with the doppler and her counsel agreeing with everything. Eight centimetres, all normal. Once, Marga says "Left side" a breath before the counsel does: her ear at work, scored as agreement. At 02:10 Noor hears the heart come back after a contraction a fraction late. The counsel, through the same doppler, hears nothing wrong. At 02:12 she hears it again, the same return, while Marga listens to it and shakes her head: twice live, by one ear of two.

2. **The landing, 02:14.** The counsel's four sentences: no indication; a birth on the bridge; three more contractions; can she say what she heard? "No." Marga: "I didn't hear it." Noor thinks of January, and of Marga's cord at Goes in 2039, and of June, the same word and nothing. "Let me." "Your call." Both logged at 02:14, Noor's eighth this year and Marga's seventh in nine; both figures move. January is each one's unsaid reason: after it nobody who has seen Noor at a bed is there to let her or stop her. None of it names the want. Between contractions Sanne hears "Let me" through the door, pulled to, and does not know what it is (unsaid here). Noor tells them. Joost looks first to Marga, already packing the bag by the chair, who does not stop. Their counsels say what hers says. "Why?" "I can't tell you." "All right." Sanne agrees because one professional is afraid and the other is packing.

3. **The Zeelandbrug, 02:40, and Goes.** Five kilometres of white lamps over the black Oosterschelde; Sanne on all fours on the stretcher; the counsel updating the odds of a birth on the bridge; Noor's figure, checked once, at 96.9. There is no birth. The contractions space out: the danger she chose passes and another begins. At Goes labour stops at eight centimetres; section at 07:40. The obstetrician lifts the cord: a true knot, loose. Noor photographs it. Marga, who never looks at her figure, takes out her phone at 07:50 and looks, and Noor sees her do it. Nobody mentions the knot.

4. **The practice, day two.** Noor reads the review at the practice table, written to Sanne as "you": three in five; the knot "not evidence"; "Let me" and "Your call" at 02:14; no late deceleration in four contractions; her seven earlier departures listed with what each was (which side to lie on, when to examine, when to call the maternity nurse) and none followed by harm or any difference the record can show; that Marga could have overruled and did not, and that a complaint naming her cannot conclude before 31 January and would reach nobody; "consistent with a training decision". A complaint drafted, not sent. Day three, the board through her own counsel: live cases suspended on notice; her hours to the simulator; sign-off deferred until a complaint is settled, or until 12 December if none comes; a complaint, once received, runs twelve weeks, and Marga retires in ten. Her father's one message: a heart and Goes's section statistics.

5. **The practice's back room, Friday.** Her counsel plays her a birth; she listens for anything to overrule and scores a hundred. The misses, in one clause: births a counsel heard nothing wrong in and something was, mixed blind with births where nothing was; she sat them in 2038 and scored at chance, like everyone. Marga is packing the practice into boxes. Noor holds out the phone with ten past two on it. Marga does not take it; she puts a box in Noor's hands instead: the practice's birth books, handwritten, 2002 to 2030. Nobody says what is in the box; Marga says nothing about the night. The fading of ten past two is the passage's to tell, not this beat's.

6. **The house under the dike, day eight.** Morning: the complaint reaches the practice, naming the departure at 02:14 with both midwives quoted and carrying the review's own sentence that naming Marga Dekker reaches nobody; the sign-off is a stranger's now. Sanne has asked for her; Marga says she need not go; she goes because it is the room where she heard it. Joost at the door, one line: Sanne has had it since Wednesday and has not played it. The passage, to "Again." "As it had been said" rests on the door: Sanne heard it on the night; Noor may register it in a clause. No second play on the duvet: Sanne asks her own counsel, aloud, where in it the midwife heard what she heard; it answers at her collar, and Noor hears it: the place, to the second; nothing there; no one, midwives included, hears such a thing on a recording better than chance. Sanne takes the phone to her ear. Noor counts on Sanne's face toward the place where the heart comes back. End there, before the play reaches it.

## What this story must not become

- The obvious version: the midwife overrules the AI and is proved right or wrong. The knot stays loose; no one says it justified the transfer; the photograph is not offered or praised. The replay finds nothing: no second sound, no twist in the file. The misses are a fact, not a scene. Sanne's face at the end is not read as hearing or not hearing.
- The hearing as the question. The curve, twice live, four on the first night, one in five by Friday, none since Sunday, reads as an ear worn out by four hundred plays or as a projection going out. Judgment is not being right: the night can have been hers and wrong.
- Bohjalian: a trial. The complaint is administrative and exact; no lawyers, no hearing, no verdict scene.
- The pardon, or growth. "Again" is not forgiveness. Sanne does not withdraw the complaint, say Noor was right, say what she hears, touch her, or ask whether Noor knows; nothing in beat 6 is warmer than the passage. Noor's want is neither reformed nor punished.
- AI makes us dumb. The counsel is not the antagonist and has no will: fair, patient, fallible, right about all it could hear, nothing a good 2026 model would not say.
- A lecture. No one says Gawande's argument or "practising on people"; the review is quoted, not explained; Marga explains nothing; the box is not read. The father is one message, no scene; the baby sleeps, unnamed.
- The batch's reflexes, which a critic will search for by name: the cut from a hard line to an ambient sound (furnace, radiator, fridge, rain ticking); "said it the way [a job reads things]"; "did not remember" a small act of the hand; "looked at the window, where". None. "As it had been said" once; "gallop" in the passage only.
- A verdict withheld as a pose. The ending is an act, and its last sentence is as the ending image says: "second" stays out of it, since it carries the time, the play and the departure.
