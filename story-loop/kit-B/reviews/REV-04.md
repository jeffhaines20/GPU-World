# REV-04 — depth-critic, M2, the spine of story B, "Working Height" (2026-09-25)

## Reading

1. **What the story is actually about.** A fair and complete account of a harm cannot hold the one thing the harm was spent on, and the person who paid still chooses to listen for that thing. That is not the plot, which is a transfer, a section, a review and a visit. The beats carry it without the spine's commentary.

   - Beat 4's review holds everything: "Let me" and "Your call" with their times, the seven departures, the motive ("consistent with a training decision"), and ten past two "reanalysed, no late deceleration".
   - Beat 6 asks the one question the review cannot answer. It is the question from the landing, asked again. At 02:14 the counsel asked whether she could say what she heard, and Noor said "No." On day eight Sanne asks "What did you hear?" Noor answers by pressing play, and "Noor heard a heart."

2. **Is the question real?** Yes, as built. A thoughtful reader could hold either answer.

   - **The payer's side has a body.** A section. "Every future birth in hospital" for a woman who hired blocks to give birth in her own bedroom. "Three in five". A complaint. Noor checking her figure on the bridge.
   - **The departer's side rests on something this world makes true.** The simulator "plays only hearts the counsel can hear", and Marga retires in January. So the only place to learn whether a human ear is ever right where the counsel is not is a live birth that someone else pays for.
   - **The reader hears it too.** Close third lets the reader hear the late heart at 02:10 and then lose it.
   - **The father's kind of good is real** ("as a good thing, and it is").

   The text risks deciding in two places, and it leans a different way in each.

   (a) **The knot leans to "proved right".** It gets the lift, the photograph, and approval from the only senior voice: "Marga: 'Keep that.'" In beat 6, "Noor did not offer it" reads as the restraint of someone who may be right. Against all that stands one quoted clause, "not evidence that transfer was needed", which a lay reader will hear as the institution defending itself. To a lay reader, a true knot looks like a cause.

   (b) **Apart from the knot, the case for departing has no body.** The story's one ear that has outheard the counsel is shown only:
   - not hearing (beats 1–2);
   - leaving (beat 4);
   - refusing to listen (beat 5).

   Its record is half a clause on the landing: "the two of Marga's 0.4 she was at, a cord twice round the neck and nothing". A cord round the neck is the same kind of finding as a loose knot. The spine's question says "that is how every midwife who ever outheard the counsel was made". No beat shows Marga's sense working. None shows that she too was made of women like Sanne, though her "nothing" also had a payer. That is the story's Omelas, and so far it lives only in the spine's commentary.

   If the knot is made as neutral as the spine intends, the question leans to "vanity paid for by others". If it is not, the question leans to vindication. This is a finding about earning, not a disagreement: the idea is sound, but its second half is not yet on the page.

3. **Does it earn its idea or announce it?** Mostly it earns it. The idea is used where the strangeness changes what someone does.

   - **The review's form.** Noor reads the review "in that form", addressed to Sanne as "you". Because the audit comes from an equal model, she meets her act as a thing done to someone.
   - **The figure on the bridge.** On the Zeelandbrug, with Sanne on all fours on the stretcher, Noor checks her figure. The metric decides where she looks.
   - **The simulator.** She "listens for anything to overrule" and scores a hundred. The ceiling becomes a task with no outside.
   - **Sanne departs twice.**
     - At 02:14 she goes against her own counsel ("Their counsels say what hers says") because a professional is afraid.
     - On day eight she goes against it on her own, with everything in front of her. Her counsel gives her the second, says nothing is there, and she listens anyway.

     The payer's two departures bracket the apprentice's, and the second is the first she makes for her own reasons. This is the spine's best structure, and the spine may not know it has it.
   - **The bed on its blocks** is the cost made into an object. It was raised for a birth that happened in Goes instead, and no birth of Sanne's will be at home now. It stays raised for the section's stitches. The ending puts the payer at a midwife's working height, doing a midwife's listening. The title earns itself there.

   Two places risk announcing the idea: "the counsel has never made a heart it could not hear", and Marga's five-word "why" (F5). The review is the argument in the form of a document. It is safe only while it stays quoted in its own fair voice, as the spine requires.

4. **Do the people behave like people?** Mostly, and at their best very specifically.

   - Noor checks her number on the bridge, photographs the knot, and stops asking the counsel because "the patience is worse".
   - Sanne consents at eight centimetres "because a professional is afraid", which is exactly what a labouring woman does.
   - Sanne sends the complaint and asks for Noor on the same morning. That is credible: closing the account is what shows her it does not close.
   - The father sending a heart plus Goes's section statistics is exact.

   **The moment the plot needs is Marga's "Your call".** Just before it:
   - Marga has listened through a contraction and shaken her head.
   - She has said "I didn't hear it".
   - The counsel has given its four sentences.
   - Noor has said she cannot say what she heard.

   Then the responsible midwife hands a first-time mother at eight centimetres to a sound her trainee cannot name. The spine gives Noor her reasons (January, the 0.4) and gives Marga none. Afterwards the review quotes the line with its time, the board acts on Noor alone, and Marga packs boxes.

   A reader will either not believe "Your call", or will see that Marga spent Sanne's birth as surely as Noor did and wonder why the story never looks at that. There is a reason the reader could see without being told: a retiring midwife who knows what her own ear once cost other women. It would also answer 2(b).

5. **What does the ending cost?**

   **For Noor, something real and on the page.**
   - The complaint stands.
   - Her sign-off goes past Marga "into a stranger's reading of her record".
   - The sound has been gone from her own hearing since Sunday. Now it passes into the ear of the one person with a right to it: "she cannot hear this play".

   That last is the ending's true price. The stranger is now listening for what Noor spent the stranger's birth to find out, and Noor cannot hear it.

   **For Sanne, the new cost is thin on the page.** The spine counts "a scar, a hospital for every future birth", but she paid those at 07:40. The ending's only new cost to her is the doubt: the review's verdict stops being enough. The page shows the verdict as fair ("It was very fair") and as a wound ("probably a training decision"). It never shows it as somewhere she could have rested, and the sending of the complaint happens offstage. So her listening can read as hope, which is a gain, rather than as the spending the turn claims (F4).

6. **Would a reader be different afterwards?** Yes, in a small way. They would be:

   - less sure that a fair, complete account of a harm is an account of what happened;
   - less sure that the person harmed wants a verdict more than the thing it cannot hold;
   - left uneasy that where everyone carries the same good second opinion, a first opinion can only be grown on someone else's birth, and that the someone may want to listen.

   That is the brief's "think differently about something you had assumed". To be sure of it, the reader must want the thing no record holds and be unable to conclude. That needs two things:
   - a knot that stops short of concluding (F1);
   - a body the reader has seen for the reason anyone should ever outhear the counsel (F2).

## Findings

F1: MINOR — The knot leans toward the answer the spine refuses. Beat 3 ends it on the senior voice's approval ("Marga: 'Keep that.'") and beat 6's "Noor did not offer it" reads as the restraint of someone who may be right, so against one quoted clause of the review a lay reader is likely to take the knot as a cause and hear "Again" as the start of the pardon the must-not list forbids; "Keep that" is the line that turns the reader's wanting into concluding.

F2: MINOR — The case for departing is argued, not shown: the story's one ear that has outheard the counsel is only seen not hearing, leaving and refusing to listen (beats 1, 2, 4, 5), its whole record is half a clause (beat 2, "a cord twice round the neck and nothing", and 0.4 of what is never said), and "how every midwife who ever outheard the counsel was made" (The question) appears in no beat. Once the knot stops leaning, the question leans to "vanity paid for by others" and becomes one the story has already answered.

F3: MINOR — Marga's "Your call" (beat 2) is the line the plot needs: just after "I didn't hear it", against her own ear and the counsel's four sentences, she hands a first-time mother at eight centimetres to a sound her trainee cannot name, and the spine gives Noor reasons (January, the 0.4) but Marga none. Afterwards the review quotes it (beat 4), the board acts on Noor alone and Marga packs boxes (beat 5), so the story must either show what "Your call" costs her or show that it knows it cost her nothing.

F4: MINOR — The ending's only new cost to Sanne is that the review's verdict stops being enough, because she paid for the scar and the hospital (listed in the ending image) at 07:40, and beat 6 shows the verdict as fair and as a wound but never as somewhere she could have rested; the sending that morning is offstage. Without one sight of what the complaint closed for her, her listening reads as hope, a gain, rather than as the spending the turn claims.

F5: MINOR — Beat 5 holds the two lines most likely to announce the idea: "the counsel has never made a heart it could not hear" is a thesis if the narration says it instead of the hundred showing it, and Marga saying "why in five words or fewer" breaks the spine's own rule that "Marga explains nothing". A why in five words is an aphorism, and at that point it would be the story's thesis in her mouth.

F6: MINOR — The fading of the sound is told twice: beat 5 plays it night by night, and the passage, kept "as written" in beat 6, repeats it almost word for word ("By Friday she heard it one time in five ... the patience was worse") as recap at the climax. The replay that drains is the story's most borrowed element (the spine itself names Blow-Up) and should be spent once.

F7: NIT — The turn says Sanne "holds the price to the decimal", but the review's figure is "three in five" (beat 4). If the audit's exactness is part of what removes "the vague bill", either the figure should read like a model's or the phrase should change.
