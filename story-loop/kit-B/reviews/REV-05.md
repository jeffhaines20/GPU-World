# REV-05 — adversarial-critic, M2, the spine of story B, "Working Height" (2026-09-25)

**Verdict:** NEEDS CHANGES (covers the spine as read today)

## The load-bearing claim

The sentence the story can't survive losing is in the Obstacle: "The only place to learn whether she is ever right where it is not is a live birth." That sentence does three jobs:
- It makes "Let me" the old necessity of apprenticeship rather than one choice among several.
- It lets the Question say "that is how every midwife who ever outheard the counsel was made".
- It keeps "both sides stay strong" true. Once the counsel has removed necessity-for-care, the only standing weight on the judgment side is that "a hearing past 2026 is the only way past the ceiling".

The turn rests on it too. Sanne can spend her verdict on "the one thing it cannot hold" only if there was nowhere else to look for that thing.

Steelman. This is a closed argument, and a good one. The world takes away both supports of Gawande's bargain: necessity (a 2026 obstetrician on every collar) and the vague bill (the review prices it at three in five). What's left is aspiration, and the spine seals every exit but one:
- The simulator is the counsel's and "plays only hearts the counsel can hear".
- Marga's sense will not go into words.
- A woman at eight centimetres, asked to be the test, would say stay.

So an apprentice who wants to know must spend a stranger. The stranger holds the same model and can audit everything: the cost, and the want, read off the timing, the concordance and January. She can audit everything except what was heard. The story then hands that one unaudited thing to the payer and ends with her listening for it against her own fair counsel.

The attack. The argument is not closed. The list of sealed exits leaves out the one this world has plainly built: the record.
- The story's central object is a recording, played "perhaps four hundred times".
- The review reanalyses it to the minute.
- Outcomes are logged: the Apgars, the time of the section, the knot photographed.

Fourteen years into such a world there is an archive of real heartbeats recorded in labour, with outcomes attached. It includes the births where a counsel heard nothing and a baby was hurt. For anyone who wants to know whether her ear hears where the counsel does not, that is the first test: listen blind to the counsel's misses mixed with normal births, and score. No stranger pays.

Beat 5 almost proposes this itself. "The counsel has never made a heart it could not hear" invites the reply: then play her the hearts it didn't make.
- If that door is open, Noor chose a living woman over the dead. "Let me" is vanity by construction, and the balance is lost before the review is written.
- If it is shut, the spine has to say why.

The readers this story is entered for (the contest's judges include Gwern Branwen) will ask about a held-out test set at the simulator.

I also attacked the turn. It held (see What survived), but the attack broke something narrower: the ledger. The Question books "the one who paid asks for it" to the judgment side, so the story's last act weighs on one side only. Sanne's listening does not say whether Noor heard anything. It does say the question was worth the payer's attention, and readers slide from there to "so it wasn't vanity". The only thing that can put vanity into the final image is what Noor wants while she watches. The spine leaves that blank (F4).

## The sweep

- **An ending that explains itself:** cleared. The counsel's "nothing is there" in the coda is the condition that makes the listening a departure, not a gloss. Watch: the spine's "What it costs" material (the clean account, "no one to send it to") is gloss, and none of it may reach the page as a sentence.
- **A character who exists to be told things:** cleared. Joost carries one reason (the stitches). The father gets two gestures. Sanne makes the turn.
- **A middle that repeats the first act at a higher volume:** cleared as a middle. Beats 4–5 get quieter as the night is replayed by other listeners (the review, then Noor); they are not beat 1 played louder. Found elsewhere: beat 6 repeats beat 5 (F3).
- **Strangeness that is decoration:** cleared. The concordance figure moves at 02:14. The review is addressed to "you" and read by the person it accuses. The simulator "has never made a heart it could not hear". Each of these does work.
- **A theme stated in dialogue:** at risk in one line, Marga's unwritten five words (F6). "It says I was probably a training decision" is cleared: it is the verdict Sanne holds, quoted, and the pivot needs her holding it.
- **A first page that is throat-clearing:** cleared at spine level; the late heart arrives on page one. Watch: 2029, "lowest in Zeeland" and the father belong inside the night, not in front of it.
- **A last line reaching for profundity:** at risk, seeded by the spine's own "she cannot hear this play" (F8).
- **A plot that would not change if the science-fictional element were removed:** cleared. This is the spine's best mechanism (see What survived).
- **The spine's own must-not list** (the obvious version, Bohjalian, the pardon, the sibling confrontation, AI makes us dumb, the lecture, the father as foil, the batch's reflexes): all clear in the passage. "As it had been said" appears once and does work.

## Findings

F1: MAJOR — Missing mechanism at the load-bearing sentence. The Obstacle rules out the simulator, Marga and the woman, but not the record. This world records births (the file of ten past two, played "perhaps four hundred times"), reanalyses them (beat 4, "ten past two reanalysed, no late deceleration") and logs outcomes (Apgar, the section's time, the knot photographed). Fourteen years in, that is an archive of real heartbeats with outcomes attached, the counsel's misses among them. It would be a blind test of whether Noor outhears the counsel, paid for by no one. Beat 5 almost proposes it: "the counsel has never made a heart it could not hear" invites "then play her the hearts it didn't make". If the archive is open, "Let me" chooses a living woman over the dead. The Question's "that is how every midwife who ever outheard the counsel was made" is then false in-world, and "both sides stay strong" fails. If the archive is shut, the spine owes the reason: a clause in the Obstacle and a line in beat 5, not new machinery. Every candidate reason is stronger material than what is there now: nothing recorded since 2026 is pooled; or everyone, Marga included, scores at chance on tape; or Noor has taken the test and knows her score. Each one changes what the fading of her hearing in beat 5 means, so choose before drafting.

F2: MINOR — Three people departed at 02:14 and the spine prices only one. Marga's "Your call" is quoted with its time and has no price. The board's consequences all fall on Noor, and Marga packs boxes for January. Sanne "agrees because a professional is afraid" after "their counsels say what hers says" (beat 2), so the payer departed from her own counsel first. In the spine's account of the review her counsel writes to her as "you", this is never said. Sanne doesn't say it either ("You asked her"). A fair counsel would say both. One line each would pay twice. January becomes the reason Marga could say "Your call". And the listening at the end becomes Sanne's second departure, made knowing what the first one cost.

F3: MINOR — The passage retells beat 5 at the hinge of the story. Beat 5 says: "the first night after every contraction, by Friday one time in five, from Sunday not at all. She stops asking the counsel, because its answer is the same each time, patiently, and the patience is worse." Beat 6 takes the passage "as written". The passage gives the same four facts in the same order and ends on "the patience was worse". It places them between "What did you hear?" and "She set the phone on the duvet". The recap has a job: it lays the shadow of the fading over "Again". But two pages after beat 5, a reader will meet it as repetition at the one moment the story can least afford a pause. Decide which beat tells the fading as a scene and cut the other to one line. "Four hundred times" is the only new fact in the passage's version.

F4: MINOR — The weight of the last image is unassigned. The Question's ledger puts "the one who paid asks for it" on the judgment side, so the final act weighs on one side only. The spine gives the last sentence to Noor watching. It does not say what she wants while she watches, or why she came when Marga said she need not. If she wants Sanne to hear it, her want is still spending the stranger, now in doubt rather than a scar, and vanity is in the final image. If she wants Sanne not to hear it, the reader reads growth. The page need not say which, but the spine must know, because a gesture will show it.

F5: MINOR — The spine's only evidence that any human has ever outheard the counsel is half a clause: "a cord twice round the neck" (beat 2; the other half is "and nothing"). The must-not list's "right about all it can see" is also a notch stronger than the world's "fallible". A world that lists Noor's seven departures holds a record of Marga's 0.4 and what those departures found. The spine never says what that record shows, so the judgment side rests on the ceiling argument alone. One fact is needed, even if the fact is that a career of departures is too few for the record to tell.

F6: MINOR — Marga's five words. In beat 5 she refuses ten past two and "says why in five words or fewer". The must-not list says "Marga explains nothing, and the story does not translate her". A reason is an explanation, and the blank is the line most likely to state the theme outright ("It isn't on the tape"). Write the five words into the spine, or replace "says why" with something she does.

F7: MINOR — The plan's account of the audit is less exact than the story needs. "Three in five" prices how avoidable the section was in beat 4 ("probably avoidable, three in five"). The Ending image then uses it to price motive ("She was probably a training decision, three in five"). The review's word for motive is "consistent with", and a fair counsel does not put odds on a motive. Separately, "a review can take eight weeks" (beat 4) gives the board's process the same name as Sanne's counsel's report. Sanne's merged "It says I was probably a training decision" is good in her mouth, but only if the narration never repeats that merge as fact.

F8: NIT — "She cannot hear this play" (Ending image) has a double sense (the playback, and the thing itself) that a last sentence will be tempted to land on. Give the last sentence an object, not a verb of hearing.

F9: NIT — "After every contraction" (beat 5 and the passage) sits against a file called "ten past two". If the file holds one contraction, write "every time". If it holds several, Noor heard the lateness after contractions no one else examined, including the one Marga listened through. That is evidence, and the spine should know it has it.

F10: NIT — "A horse going hard on sand": the galloping horse is the stock description of a doppler heartbeat. "On sand" earns some of it back, and "the gallop slowing; the gallop coming back" spends it again.

## What survived

- **The test of removing the science fiction.** Without the counsel, a student's worried transfer is the cautious default: nobody complains, nothing is priced, and beats 4–6 have nothing driving them. The counsel reverses which choice counts as caution. Staying becomes the safe act and the transfer becomes the departure. That is why the bridge is dangerous and why the scar has a price. It is the story's most original mechanism.
- **The turn.** I tried reading "Again" as pardon, as a mother's hunger for a heart recorded before birth, and as prosecution ("show me where"). The coda settles it: she asks her own counsel for the second, is told fairly that nothing is there, and listens anyway. That is a departure, the apprentice's act repeated by the payer with the stake moved onto herself. The hand rising on the small back keeps love in the scene without warming it. It is a turn.
- **Sanne's presence without her inner life.** "As it had been said": she reproduces the delivery of a line spoken on the landing, outside her room, so she has been listening to the record too. It is the one plant for "Again" that Noor's point of view can see. Together with the complaint drafted on day two and sent on day eight, and the review addressed to "you", it keeps Sanne in beats 4–5 without once entering her head.
- **The ending is not the premise restated.** It rhymes with 02:10 (someone listens for what the counsel says isn't there), but the rhyme changes who pays, so it moves the story on rather than repeating it.
- **The audit's asymmetry.** "Blind only to what did not happen" holds. The review prices the harm that happened, cannot see a harm that was prevented, and dismisses the knot fairly.
- **Not derivative.** Blow-Up's ending (hearing the ball that isn't there) is turned round: the sound is withheld and the listener is seen from outside. The replay device from The Conversation is used here to drain certainty, not to reveal something. Bohjalian is avoided. Gawande is argued with, not borrowed.
- **The facts.**
  - 13 November 2040 is a Tuesday, so "Friday" and "Sunday" fall on days four and six and the calendar of the fading holds.
  - The bed blocks at working height, the five-kilometre Zeelandbrug, Goes, and hospital for every birth after a section are all correct, and all do work.
  - So does the father's kind of good.
- **The passage's best lines:** "Mine drafted it. It was very fair." "The review had given it a paragraph." "Noor heard a heart."

## The sharpest question for the writer

In a world that records every heart and logs every outcome, why can't Noor find out whether she outhears the counsel from the births it has already got wrong, and if she can, what did she want from a living one?

## The one thing only this story does

It ends with the woman who paid for an apprentice's departure making one of her own, listening against her own fair counsel for a sound the apprentice can no longer hear, so that the one thing an exact audit cannot reach passes to the person it cost, out of the reader's hearing.
