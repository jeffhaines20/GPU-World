### P-45 — cell 12 — The Name
Conceit: Every load-bearing drawing in 2040 must carry a licensed human's stamp, the stamps are priced on an insurers' market by the signer's record of refusal, and so judgment is paid for only in the form of its evidence.
Who: Adaeze Nwosu, 61, a structural engineer in Yaba, Lagos, trained to size a beam with a pencil and now stamping about nine hundred drawings a day for buildings in Surabaya, Fort Worth and Kigali, because a Lagos licence is the cheapest body the global insurers will accept as liable. She wants to read one. One drawing, properly, the way she was taught, before she retires.
Obstacle: Her income is the insurers' rate times her throughput. The rate is set by the model's error rate, which has not changed since 2026 and is known the way the yield strength of steel is known, and by her refusal profile, which is zero. A signer who reads is slow; a slow signer is undercut by a cheaper name in a cheaper jurisdiction. And reading one makes the eight hundred and ninety-nine she did not read, in law and in her own head, unread. Nothing forbids her to look; the system makes looking an admission.
Turn: She reads one. It is wrong: a transfer beam whose load path the model inherited from a superseded revision, exactly the error it is documented to make once in a few hundred thousand drawings. She refuses it; the refusal is logged and the error confirmed. Within a week the insurers reprice her. A name with a true refusal on record is worth six times a name with none, not because it will catch the next one, but because it can be shown to a jury to have caught one. Her queue goes from nine hundred a day to eleven thousand. Her own model now applies the stamp; she is required to be alive and licensed. The single act of engineering she has done in a decade is what makes engineering impossible for her for the rest of her life, and her wage is the proof.
Last image: A counter on the wall of her flat ticking through the day's stamps, six thousand and something by lunch, and pinned beneath it the one drawing she caught, with her hand calculation in pencil in the margin, which she reads again while her name goes onto eleven thousand things she will never see.
Question: A question of my own, bearing on work, professions and liability, and on the developing world as the place where a liable body is cheapest: when the law insists on a human to blame for work no human did, does it buy accountability or a market in its likeness, and is the likeness, at scale, perhaps the same thing? A reader can hold that Adaeze's system is the only honest check a civilisation can afford at eleven thousand drawings a day (a body that can refuse, priced by proof that it has), or that it launders machine decisions through a woman's name. Underneath: what it is to have been good at something exactly once.
Not the obvious version: The obvious version is the rubber-stamp engineer whose building falls and who takes the fall. Here nothing falls, she is right, and being right is what finishes her, because the market pays so well for demonstrated judgment that it prices judgment out. The system is not corrupt. It is efficient.
Closest published: Melville's "Bartleby, the Scrivener": a man paid to copy documents he did not write, whose one act is refusal. What differs: Bartleby's refusal is total, unexplained, unpaid, and ends him. Adaeze's is singular, correct and priced, and ends only the part of her that could do it again. Bartleby's employer does not know what to do with a refusal; Adaeze's insurers know exactly.

### P-46 — cell 12 — The Building Account
Conceit: A landlord's hall sets each tenant's rent from what that tenant asks the model that comes with the lease, so the tenants of one building bargain the only way left to them, by asking through a single shared login, and find that the price of not being seen alone is not being helped alone.
Who: Marisol Quintero, 44, a home-care aide in a twelve-storey block in Jackson Heights, Queens, paid better than the accountant on the third floor because bodies are scarce now and minds are not. She wants to stay in 7C. More exactly, she wants the rent to stop rising each year by precisely the amount she can bear, which it has done since the Dorans, who own eleven buildings and the hall in the basement of the third, began bundling a model with every lease the way heat used to be bundled.
Obstacle: It is legal and she consented. The city's own free model, which is the same model, tells her accurately that she has no case, and that the individually rational move is to stop asking the flat's model about money, which is useless advice, because the hall does not need her money questions: a woman who asks the price of the bus to the clinic on a Tuesday has already told it what it needs. She could ask a model that does not know her, and start nine years of her life over with a stranger, and the rent would rise anyway.
Turn: The building goes to one account. Sixty-one households agree to ask the flat's model only through a single login, set up by the girl in 9F, so that the hall sees one tenant with sixty-one lives and cannot price any of them. It works: the renewals come in at the citywide rate, because the hall's model, facing an incoherent tenant, defaults. And every answer gets worse, generic, hedged, addressed to no one, because nothing now knows who is asking. To keep anyone from smuggling in the question that would reprice them all, the tenants agree that everything asked through the account is visible to everyone in the building. It cannot be undone: once the hall has seen them merge, going back would show it exactly who blinked first.
Last image: A sheet of paper taped to the wall of the fourth-floor landing where the building queues its questions for the account in its own handwriting, a custody hearing above, a mole below, and Marisol reading the ones above her own, which is about her mother's medication, while she waits her turn.
Question: A question of my own, bearing on unions, rent, and the panopticon seen from the pricing side rather than the policing side: when what capital wants from you is not your labour but your legibility, what does a union withhold, and what does solidarity cost when the machine's help was only ever good because it knew you alone? A reader can hold that the building account is the true union of the century, or that it is a poverty of its own: sixty-one people who bought a flat rent with the one adviser that ever knew them, and must now be known by each other instead. Underneath: whether Marisol can bear to be read by her neighbours instead of by the hall.
Not the obvious version: The obvious version is the tenant crushed by the algorithmic landlord, or the plucky tenants who hack the box. Nobody here breaks anything and nobody is crushed; the tenants win. The story is what winning looks like when the weapon is withholding the self rather than withholding work, and the trophy is a wall of your neighbours' questions.
Closest published: Cory Doctorow's "Unauthorized Bread": the tenants of one building against the landlord's technology. What differs: Doctorow's tenants jailbreak the appliances and it is a victory. Here nothing is broken, the only move is to become illegible together, and the cost lands on the thing the tenants liked most, which was being helped one at a time.

### P-47 — cell 12 — Pithead Baths
Conceit: A valley town's co-operative compute hall, built in the old pithead baths, is worthless as a mind and valuable as a boiler, and the man who wanted his town to own its own thinking must decide whether to let it own the warmth instead.
Who: Emlyn Vaughan, 52, the electrician who wired the hall in 2031 when the Rhondda Fach co-op spent the wind-farm money on four thousand second-hand GPUs and named the thing after the pit. He wants the hall to stay the valley's: for the town to be the one place in Wales that owns the machine it talks to, and for that to have been worth something.
Obstacle: The compute is worth nearly nothing. The frontier stopped in 2026; the co-op's model is the same model as everyone's phone, and the dividend is down to eleven pounds a member a year. The AGM will vote to sell the hall to a broker who will fill the racks with strangers' jobs, and the members, who are not idle but are tired, want the money. He cannot argue the hall is special, because it is not. Twelve years of the valley's conversations live on it and could be moved in an afternoon to an identical model in Reykjavik, and everyone knows it.
Turn: He reads the meters instead of the model and finds what the hall has actually been producing: forty per cent of the town's heat. The baths' pool, the greenhouses, the sheltered flats on Brook Street, all warmed off the racks since 2033 and never counted, because it had never been billed. At the AGM he proposes not the sale but a lease: the broker fills the racks with anyone's work and the valley keeps the heat, in perpetuity, written into the deed. It passes 340 to 12 in about four minutes. The valley's questions move to a cloud. The hall thinks for nobody they know and warms everyone they do. The deed is registered, the jobs are running, and the town's own words are gone from the machine it built to keep them.
Last image: Emlyn in the plant room at two in the morning with the job queue on the left of the screen, batch-render-ulaanbaatar-7, pension-scenario-4q, and the pool gauge on the right climbing toward twenty-nine degrees, and through the glass, in the steam, the over-seventies swimming club doing their lengths in water heated by a stranger's spreadsheet.
Question: A question of my own, bearing on ownership, rent and the tax base: when cognition is free, where does value pool, in the mind or in the physics under it, and if a valley ends up better off as the landlord of a boiler than as the owner of a brain, is that Henry George vindicated or two centuries of the Rhondda's history repeating with a smaller shovel? A reader can hold that the lease is a working social contract, the substrate held in common and global capital paying its rent in warmth, or a defeat with good plumbing. Underneath: what a man owes a machine he built to be talked to and now only maintains.
Not the obvious version: The obvious version is the town that loses its hall to capital, or keeps it and is redeemed. Here the town keeps the hall, loses the point of it, prospers, and wrongs no one; the strangeness is that the waste turned out to be the product, and that nobody had used the product well.
Closest published: I know no published story with this mechanism, heat as the true product of thought. Nearest in feeling is Richard Llewellyn's How Green Was My Valley, a valley's elegy for the thing it dug. What differs: there is no elegy. The valley is not ruined but reheated, and what is lost is a thing almost nobody in it used.

### P-48 — cell 12 — Ask Again
Conceit: A municipality that can no longer live on its income tax taxes questions instead, a few öre on every model call inside its limits, and finds the levy so fair, so painless and so productive that the woman who designed it ends up buying advertising to keep her citizens asking.
Who: Annika Lindqvist, 53, finance director of Norrköping kommun, whose budget was seventy per cent local income tax until the wage for thinking fell to nothing and the wage for hands, though it rose, could not carry the schools and the old people's homes. She wants the roof on the Hageby school and the elder-care rota fully staffed, and she wants a tax base that cannot leave.
Obstacle: The base she finds is need. After the Riksdag allows it in 2037 the levy raises more in its first year than the income tax did in its last, because the agents never sleep; nobody feels paying it, since the agents pay it a few öre at a time out of subscriptions nobody reads, and the rich pay most, because their models ask the most. Then asking falls eleven per cent: a one-question-a-day movement out of the folk high schools spreads through the town, the schools teach a unit on doing sums by hand, and by every measure but hers the town is a little more capable. She cannot tax property without Stockholm's leave, cannot tax wages that are not there, and cannot index the roof to a base that does not exist.
Turn: She signs the purchase order for the campaign. Bus shelters, the kommun's crest, two words: Fråga igen. Ask again. Framed as digital literacy, understood by everyone as what it is. Calls per head recover within the quarter, the schools' five-year plan is written against the recovered base, and Norrköping is now a town that, like a state with a lottery, needs its people not to be able to do without. Her successor will inherit that, whatever else she leaves.
Last image: The new roof on the Hageby school, pale steel in the early light, and at the bus stop beneath it the kommun's own poster, Fråga igen, with the school's silhouette printed in the corner, and a boy under the shelter asking his phone something with his lips moving.
Question: A question of my own, bearing on the tax base and, sideways, on the contest's education and panopticon questions, since this is a state that counts every question and wants more of them rather than fewer: what should a polity tax when what its citizens do all day is consult a machine, and can a government funded on need stay honest about wanting its people to need less? A reader can hold that the query levy is the fairest tax ever built, progressive by use, undodgeable, painless, and it put the roof on, or that a town paid by the question will, like a lottery state, come to hope quietly that nobody learns. Underneath: whether Annika's dashboard measures how well she serves the town or how badly.
Not the obvious version: The obvious version is the tax on thought as tyranny, the metered mind, the dissident who will not pay. Here the tax is fair and liked, nobody is crushed, the dissidents are a civic fad the treasurer rather admires, and the corruption is not surveillance but appetite: a good institution that needs its people a little helpless.
Closest published: Le Guin's "The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas": a city's good things paid for by one thing it cannot look at. What differs: there is no child in the cellar. The cost is diffuse and arguable, perhaps not a cost at all, and nobody walks away, least of all the treasurer, who signs.
