Diarmuid was in the drain to the tops of his wellingtons, working the graip under the rushes where the shore came out, and did not stop when she came down her side.

"Thursday," he said. "Isn't it."

She had not known he had the date. The water backed brown behind the fork and went through, and he stood and let it go past him.

"After Thursday it's your word and mine," he said. "Whatever you say about it, I'll say aye, that's how it was. To the priest. To anyone. You can have it any way you like it."

"And if I said you never did it."

"Then I never did it." He lifted the fork and a black plug of silt with it. "That's yours as well."

She stood on her bank and he in the water, and the rain came on over Sliabh an Iarainn, and between them the drain cleared and ran.

She drove to Carrick with the wipers on slow. Through Leitrim village, the river high and the colour of tea, a lorry of pallets ahead of her. Her model had the map up and said nothing.

The sergeant, who had a mug with Longford on it, took her into a room with a radiator and a window onto the car park, and she told him whose kitchen and what year.

"And the other party."

"Diarmuid Gormley."

He put it in. "Same as yourself."

"My brother."

He set a bud in his ear and her model gave the night to his. Thirty-eight minutes; the number had sat in her memory view for seven years and she had never pressed it. She watched the car park. A squad car went out and the rain filled its place. His face did nothing. He did not move it on and did not speak, and the radiator ticked, and at thirty-eight minutes he took the bud out and laid it on the table.

"That's lodged," he said. "You can't withdraw it now. Nor can he. Nor can we."

"I know," she said. "I voted for it."
