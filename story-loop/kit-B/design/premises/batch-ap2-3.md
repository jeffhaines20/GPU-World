### P-33 — Nobody Ran

Who: Sreelatha, 54, secretary of the town's cooperative bank for twenty-six years, who has lent the town's money to the town on her knowledge of who repays. She wants the members to leave their deposits where they are until the 5th, when the quarterly instalments come in and the filings will show the loan book sound.
Obstacle: The rubber-processing plant that employed a third of her borrowers closed in July; the closure is in the record — the filings, the notices, the news, everything the models read — and the repayments are not yet. Asked "is my money safe," every member's model gives the same measured answer: the guarantee limit, the plant, "you may wish to hold no more than the insured amount here." The Gulf families who hold most of the deposits do the measured thing, one household at a time, on the same afternoon. There is no queue, no rumour, no fear: nothing to answer. She knows the borrowers will pay because she knows the borrowers, and that knowledge is exactly the kind the record cannot hold; a secretary's word about her own bank is, to the mind everyone asks, the claim of an interested party. She cannot stand in the road and tell eight thousand calm people not to be calm.
Turn: On the night of the 4th, with the bank three days from the liquidity line at which the registrar steps in, she asks her own model about her own retirement deposit — as a member, not as a test — and it tells her what it told everyone. At 23:51 she moves the portion above the guarantee to the state cooperative bank, which is the reasonable thing, and which is timestamped. On the 5th the instalments arrive in full into a bank already below the line, and the state bank absorbs it by noon.
Last image: The afternoon of the 5th, the sign already changed. Sudhakaran, who owes on three acres of rubber, counting his instalment out in notes onto the same counter, on time, and Sreelatha taking them.
Question: The old public sphere carried panic, and panic could be met with a face and a voice; the new one carries prudence, and prudence cannot be argued with. When every person consults the same careful mind, collective judgment loses the independence that made crowds occasionally wise, and the witness who knows more than the record loses her standing entirely. She means to be a better sahukar than her father — fairer, cleaner, a woman of the new kind — and she has the model to keep the book.
Obstacle: The book ran on a gap. Her father lent at his rate, collected at his discretion, and each Diwali, after the new ledger was blessed, tore one page from the old one in front of the street — a forgiveness that was also a display, and that bound the forgiven to the house for a generation. The model closes the gap from both sides at once. It keeps her book more forgivingly than he did, in total, and does it precisely, privately, by message, with no page to tear. And every borrower carries the same mind, which tells each of them what a fair settlement is: the number she sees. His daughter Jean, who loves him and who found him in the yard in his pyjamas at four on a November morning, has applied to be his deputy for property and affairs.
Obstacle: The Mental Capacity Act asks whether a man can understand, retain, use or weigh, and communicate the information a decision needs — and says he must not be found unable until every practicable step to help him has been tried, and that retaining the information only briefly does not count against him. The model is a practicable step. With it he can do all four; without it he cannot hold the price of a gimmer for the length of an auction. Jean's case is that the model is not helping him decide, it is deciding. His case is that he decided, fourteen years ago, to trust it, and the deciding has gone well. Neither can simply be right, because the Act was written for help that was a person in the room, and a person in the room can be asked to step outside.
Turn: The district judge asks that the model be switched off for the rest of the hearing, so the court can see Mr Ridley. Alec agrees; he is proud and he thinks he will do well. For forty minutes in a room in Newcastle his daughter, his solicitor and the judge watch a man who cannot find the year, who calls Jean by her mother's name, who explains lambing beautifully and then explains it again. Then it is switched back on and he is himself, and he knows what they have seen. The judge finds that he has capacity, with the tool, as a man with spectacles has sight. Everyone leaves the room having seen him without it, and no order of the court can put that back.
Last image: Alec driving the Land Rover home up the fell road he has driven for sixty years, the quiet voice in his ear telling him, kindly, to take the left fork at the cattle grid, and him taking it.
