idea: A self-declared spending ceiling printed on the insurance card like a blood type is a genuinely strange, hard institution, and the card runs it entirely through the mother's two uses of it rather than describing the policy — with a real question a reader could answer either way (honest rationing versus manufactured consent to die cheaply), plus a sharper sub-question about whether the well chooser and the sick sufferer are the same person.
person: Yu-hsuan wants her mother alive and has exactly the money, and the mother wants her own number honoured; both want concretely, and both pay — the flat deposit that will never be spent, the rebate taken to close the door — with the alterations-in-the-front-room detail doing real class work.
turn: The permanent lock at the counter is irreversible by construction and is a choice, not an accident, and the daughter typing her mother's number into her own form is a second irreversibility that reframes the first.
prose: no probe
aftertaste: The unspent deposit and the inherited number would sit with me for days, mostly as the question of whether the daughter has honoured her mother or been recruited by her.

P-51 | idea=4 | person=4 | turn=4 | prose=no probe | aftertaste=4
idea: The hard idea — that a reconstruction which can update makes the dead person unfinished, so a probated will collides with a living record of the man — is real and both-sidable (a will was always a poor proxy; finality is what frees the living), though it sits nearer the familiar griefbot furniture than the other cards, and the card is careful to disown that.
person: Miriam's two wants (stay in the house, do right by Paul) are genuinely the same want until August, and she pays with the house and the Danforth flat; Daniel not asking is a good restraint, but her interior life is thinner than P-40's or P-25's — she is more a position than a person on this card.
turn: Selling the house is irreversible, but the sharper irreversible thing is that she has granted the dead standing and cannot un-ask, which the last image (her hand stopped above the speaker) lands well.
prose: no probe
aftertaste: The line about the next question also having an answer is the part that follows you home — an inheritance you can keep re-litigating forever with a witness who is always available.

P-43 | idea=5 | person=5 | turn=5 | prose=no probe | aftertaste=5
idea: That a dying language survives as one garrulous bore's idiolect, and the state canonizes it because it is the only version on paper, is a strange and precise idea about standardization — the politics of whose dialect wins, decided by who talked most into the record — and it is enacted at a table rather than argued.
person: Dolma's want is exact and small (her daughter should have her language, not the road's), her cost is that she must speak her disliked father-in-law's version to be understood by her own child, and the married-in-from-three-hours-away detail gives her a real position in a real place.
turn: The child's word for her mother's village cannot be untaught, and the moment Dolma has to use the model's word to correct her is a one-way door; the first language is his and stays his.
prose: no probe
aftertaste: I would keep turning over the claim that a heritage can be held by a keeper who is not a member — and the uneasy sense that every standard language got here the same way, only slower.

P-44 | idea=4 | person=4 | turn=4 | prose=no probe | aftertaste=4
idea: The idea that visible correct rationing destroys the legitimacy that luck was quietly lending the old system is a real question with two defensible sides, and the card uses it (the third child is fine, which proves nothing), though the mechanism — a triage ranking with an override requiring a written reason — is a smaller strangeness than the other cards' institutions.
person: Meseret wants to send the boy who came first and pays with her standing in a village she has served ten years; the bench, the slips and the roof money are good concrete stakes, but her inner life is defined mostly by the dilemma rather than exceeding it.
turn: The irreversible thing is nicely displaced from the seat to the perception — the village now reads ten years of luck as ten years of choosing — which cannot be unseen, even if it is a change in others rather than in her.
prose: no probe
aftertaste: The reframing of past luck as retroactive choice is what stays: every ration you never had to defend becomes a decision the moment someone shows you the list.

P-25 | idea=5 | person=5 | turn=5 | prose=no probe | aftertaste=5
idea: A referendum-mandated rolling seven-year deletion with the State as the only permitted rememberer inverts the panopticon question in a way I have not seen, and the card makes the idea do the work — the brother's relief, the closed consent exemption that was written to protect her and traps her instead.
person: Nuala's want is exquisitely specific and self-defeating (to keep the proof and never use it, so that what happened does not depend on her), she believes in the law she is destroyed by, and she pays with her brother, her parish and the year that went grey around the one lit night.
turn: A lodged record cannot be withdrawn by design, and the brother is named in Dublin forever a day before he would have been clean — irreversible on both sides at once.
prose: no probe
aftertaste: The image of one night still lit in the grey, with the edge receding a day a day, plus the cruelty that the only way to keep a private thing is to make it public — I would be arguing with myself about the amendment tomorrow.

READ BEFORE: P-51 — Black Mirror, "Be Right Back" (Charlie Brooker) for the widow-and-reconstruction shape, and Byatt's Possession for intentions read off a record, though this card's legal frame is its own; P-44 — Godwin's "The Cold Equations" for a computation that is right and costly, with Le Guin's "The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas" behind the village's need not to know what it chooses; the others, none.
WOULD READ / WOULD NOT FINISH: P-25 at five thousand words, because the law is sympathetic, the villain is a clock, and the trap closes without anyone behaving badly; P-51 I would not finish, because the griefbot furniture means I can see the shape of the middle from here and the card's own energy is in a legal argument rather than a scene.
MOST NOVEL QUESTION: P-43 — whether a language belongs to its speakers or to whatever kept it, when the keeper is not a member and the canonized version is simply whoever talked most into the record; novel because it is nowhere on the contest's list, it reaches past preservation into the politics of standardization and archival bias, and it makes the ordinary fact that every standard dialect once won a fight suddenly visible by compressing that fight into one dead man's opinions of the next valley.
