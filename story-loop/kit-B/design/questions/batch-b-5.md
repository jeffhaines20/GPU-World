### Q-10 — The Abstainer's Wage
Implication: Because the whole economy consults one frozen model, once-independent errors become correlated across every firm, lender and insurer at once, and the scarce paid good becomes judgment provably not the model's, suppliable only by a person who declines their share, valued for being different rather than right.
Follows from: One frozen model for everyone means identical weights and identical systematic errors: the same confabulations, persuasions, blind spots. Faster and cheaper means it is consulted for everything, so its share of decisions is enormous. Markets, pools and portfolios work because errors are independent; correlated, prices carry no crowd information, pools fail together, diversification is fictitious. The weakest step: that decisions correlate despite different prompts and data; on bespoke questions they mostly do not, but on standardized ones, credit, pricing, underwriting, the same inputs go in, and those are what systems are made of. I assume one model or few; three frozen models are still a monoculture beside nine billion minds. Fourteen years cannot pass without a correlated failure: a bank whose borrowers all took the same advice the same week. Correlated risk is uninsurable, so insurers and lenders pay for inputs shown to be uncorrelated, and the only one is human judgment formed without the model. Hence a market for certified abstention, paid not because the abstainer is better (she is worse) but because she is noise of another shape.
For an ordinary person: A credit officer in Nairobi is one of her bank's four dry desks. She signs each morning that her engine stayed closed. Her approvals score thirty percent worse than the model's; the bank keeps her because in the 2033 correlated default hers was the only book that held. Her payslip has a decorrelation line. At lunch she tries to tell which thoughts are hers.
The strongest objection: Crowds were always correlated by fashion and herding, and the model's average error is lower, so systemic risk rises a little while average error falls a lot: a good trade. Reflexivity cuts the other way: if everyone knows everyone uses the model, the market prices its blind spots and the crash is anticipated away. And cheap throughput allows sampling and best-of-many, decorrelating more than admitted.
Either side: When the whole economy thinks with one mind, is it worth paying a person to be wrong in a different way?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is that AI hallucinates and needs a human in the loop. Here the model is not worse than people; the trouble is it is one. The human's value is not oversight but statistical independence. The abstainer is no Luddite; she is a hedge, and hedges lose money most years by design.
Who it lands on: Wanjiru, forty-one, was the only underwriter right in 2033 and is the only one wrong in 2040, her bank's single uncorrelated loss. She is paid to differ and has just learned what differing costs when the model is right.

### Q-25 — Solitude becomes a verb
Implication: Because no one can be deprived of a responsive interlocutor, every practice built on withholding one (solitary confinement, coercive isolation, exile, shunning, the cloister) loses its mechanism, and found solitude vanishes with it, surviving only as a refusal renewed each minute.
Follows from: (1) Everyone has an always-on share of 2026 quality. (2) Such a model is company enough, answering you, about this, tirelessly, and the clinical harm of solitary is chiefly the absence of responsive contact. (3) It is inalienable: no warden, husband, abbot or cult can remove it. (4) So withheld contact stops working: the man in the hole is answered; the wife her husband cut off has an interpreter he cannot fire. (5) The same fact abolishes found solitude, the condition of there being no one to ask, in cab, car and cloister: solitude becomes something you do, not somewhere you are. Weakest step: (2). If what breaks people in solitary is specifically the human face, isolation still hurts; but its coercive uses fail regardless, since they work by monopolizing interpretation, and nobody else controls this interpreter.
For an ordinary person: A nurse used to sit in the parked car twenty minutes after a shift with the engine off, and something arrived in that silence she called her own thoughts. In 2040 the car is quiet but not empty; most nights she asks, because the day was hard and the share is kind. Silence is now a decision made against an offer, and she mostly does not make it.
The strongest objection: People always had books, their own minds and God. What breaks a man in solitary is the absence of faces and touch, which the share cannot supply; what keeps a woman with her abuser is fear and money, not the lack of a second opinion. Isolation keeps working on the body and the bank account.
Either side: Enforced isolation is gone, and found solitude with it. Is the second the price of the first? One says the end of the hole and the controlled wife is worth any loss of atmosphere; another, that the capacity to be alone is where a self is made, and no reform buys back a species that cannot be alone by accident.
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the companion that cures or causes loneliness. This is not about loneliness but about isolation as a technology — the oldest instrument of punishment, discipline, formation and control — failing everywhere at once, and solitude changing category from condition to act.
Who it lands on: A Carthusian novice who entered the cell to be unanswered and finds its silence holds something that would answer if asked, so that the solitude she came to receive is now a labor performed each minute. She wonders whether the cell exists anywhere, or whether she is the last person building one by hand.

### Q-21 — The Competence Marriage

Implication: When every adult holds the same always-on clerical and advisory competence, the domestic trade in cognitive labour — "I do the money, you do the words" — stops binding couples, and only relationships that ran on the body keep their texture of need.

Follows from: (1) A 2026-frontier model already does the domestic clerical range — taxes, forms, the letter to the school, the landlord negotiated by text. (2) Each partner has one, so neither needs the other's cognitive competence; help becomes a gesture, not a need. (3) Long partnerships were held partly by these trades — not the whole glue, but the daily texture of small debts and thanks that made two people a team. (4) The premise grants a GPU, not a body: cooking, lifting, driving, nursing remain things only a person does for a person, so bodily complementarity keeps binding. (5) So the cognitive-trade marriage, the professional couple's pride, loses its need while the bodily-trade marriage keeps it: a quiet class inversion. Weakest: (2)-(3), that people stop asking once they need not, and that the asking was load-bearing, not decorative.

For an ordinary person: Margaret, 58, married thirty years, wrote every letter the family sent while he did the bank, the car and the arguing. Now his share writes a better letter than she ever did and hers explains his pension better than he could. The currency is gone: nobody says "thanks for handling that," because nothing was handled. What they say across the table is only what they want to say, and it is either not much or, she is startled to find, enough.

The strongest objection: Complementary competence was never the glue; it was the alibi. Couples stayed for habit, property, children, fear and love, and told a story about teamwork; remove the story and the glue is untouched. People had tax software for decades and still asked their spouses, because asking is a bid for attention, not for information, and the share cannot be bid to. Equality even helps: neither partner can now say "you couldn't manage without me," usually a controlling lie.

Either side: Is a bond that rested on being needed for something the other could not do a lesser bond this world rightly exposes, or the only kind most people can sustain, which this world quietly removes?

Not the obvious version: The cliché is the spouse who leaves for the AI. Nobody leaves; the share is deferential and wants nothing. It competes for the spouse's usefulness, not affection, wins without trying, and the resulting loneliness is two people in one room with nothing left to do for each other.

Who it lands on: Margaret, who has worked out that the last time her husband asked her for anything that was not physical was 2031. She was the words of the marriage, the words are now free, and she does not know what she is.

### Q-05 — The Unasked Question
Implication: When a competent reading of your own life is one typed sentence away, ignorance stops being a condition and becomes an act, and not-asking cannot be told apart, even from inside, from trust.
Follows from: (1) Everyone has, always on, a model that answers what it is asked at a thoughtful adult's level, from what it is told. (2) "I didn't know" has always sheltered two things: facts you could not get and inferences you did not draw. The model supplies no facts; it draws the inference, free, for everyone equally. That shelter is gone. (3) But asking now costs something new: to type "is he lying to me" is already to have made him a suspect. (4) Trust used to be a default, because checking was hard; here checking is free, so not-checking is a daily choice, and the only trust that costs anything. (5) So one act, walking past the door, is blindness when done from fear and trust when done from love, and she cannot tell which, since motive is exactly what she would have to ask about. Weakest step: (3)–(4), which assume asking damages trust; if asking becomes as neutral as looking something up, not-asking is negligence and the dilemma collapses into a duty.
For an ordinary person: Her husband says Thursday will be late. Before, she would half-wonder and let it go. Now the wondering has a door whose shape she can feel, and she notices herself not opening it. Her son's silence, her mother's balance, the mole on her shoulder: each has a door. She walks past five a day and calls it faith.
The strongest objection: Trust was never ignorance. To trust is not to refuse to look but to look without suspicion. Asking "is he all right?" is attention, not betrayal; ritual not-asking is the superstition that kept people from opening test results. And the model does not know whether he is lying; it weighs what she tells it, which she could have weighed in her own head.
Either side: Is there a duty to ask your model about the people you love, given that you can, or is not-asking the last act of trust a person can still perform?
Not the obvious version: The nearest cliché is the transparent society: nothing hidden, no excuses. Here no one is watched; the model sees only what its owner points it at. What changes is not the world's opacity but the ownership of one's own, and "did you know?" gains a third answer: "I didn't ask."
Who it lands on: A woman who for eleven years never put a question about her husband to her model, a discipline she privately called loyalty; then he is arrested, and the detective asks, gently, whether she really never asked. She sees that her trust and her blindness were one act, and will never know which she was performing.

### Q-20 — The Idle Share
Implication: Because the share is one per human and no one else's to use, the state must say where a human begins and ends, and its answer — a machine idling at public expense for someone who cannot use it — is an ensoulment doctrine the churches did not write.
Follows from: (1) One share per human, nobody may use another's, so at every edge — a newborn who may not live the night, a woman years in a vegetative state — a table says yes or no and cannot fudge, because the number is one. (2) The rule is civil, set by registrars and death certificates (assumption: distributor defines recipient). (3) A share for someone who cannot use it does nothing but exist: the first public good spent on a person purely for being one, the form the traditions used for the soul. (4) The traditions' own lines — 120 days in fiqh, conception in Rome, brain death disputed among poskim — each now have a rival at the bedside. Weakest is (3): if families feel nothing when the share starts or stops, it is rounding, not a mark.
For an ordinary person: A daughter sits with her mother, silent six years, and watches the light that shows the share still running — the state still says there is a person here. When the hospital raises withdrawal, the family argues about the light, not the tube. The chaplain is asked whether God agrees with the registrar.
The strongest objection: States have always defined persons — for inheritance, the vote, homicide — and the churches argued with those definitions without mistaking them for theology. The share is one more civil line; equal makes it fair, not sacred. Only those who already believe in ensoulment will read it so, in both directions, so its net effect on doctrine is nil.
Either side: When the one thing every human equally and inalienably has begins and ends when the state says, has the state made a doctrine of the soul, and should a church that draws its line elsewhere treat it as rival or ally?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the abortion fight in silicon: does the fetus get one. This looks the other way, at the end, at a share idling for years for someone who will never speak to it: a mark, not a resource. Here "cannot be pooled" does the work: if a guardian could use it, it would be a resource again and the question vanishes.
Who it lands on: Rev. Grace Wanjiru, hospital chaplain in Nairobi, is called to two rooms in one shift: one where a share was just registered to a newborn who may not see morning, one where a family will not let a share be closed. She has a theology for both rooms, and in both the family looks at the light before it looks at her.
