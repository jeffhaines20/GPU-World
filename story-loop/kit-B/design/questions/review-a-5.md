Q-06 | importance=5 | rigour=4 | novelty=5 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: Whether a person may ever build their own judgment at others' expense governs every parent, manager, doctor and teacher in the world the premise describes, and the card correctly notes that with progress stopped this is also the ceiling of human practical wisdom.
rigour: Steps 1, 3 and 4 follow cleanly from the granted premise (one non-poolable share, never superhuman), and the card names its contested step (2, that judgment is built by bearing outcomes) instead of hiding it; the residual weakness is that it assumes deferring is available on every consequential case, which "thoughtful generalist" advice does not obviously cover.
novelty: The audit step — that the people you practiced on hold the same instrument and can retroactively price your self-cultivation — is genuinely new and inverts the familiar atrophy essay rather than restating it.
arguability: The Williams objection (moral self-indulgence) is strong enough that a reader could finish siding against the supervisor, and both the "no surgeon refuses the checklist" and "no one practices, so wisdom is capped forever" arguments have real force.
story: The son with the list at nineteen is a scene with a want, a bill and a person holding it, and the supervisor scene already has a room, two witnesses and a shamed man.
weakest step: (2) — that deferring builds only the skill of deferring, since the objection's counter-loop (consult, decide, see where the counsel was wrong) would build judgment too, and the card asserts rather than argues why the model's constant presence breaks that loop.

Q-13 | importance=5 | rigour=4 | novelty=4 | arguability=5 | story=4
importance: The legitimacy of administrative authority over every benefit, permit, visa and sanction is about as large as a social question gets, and the card locates it in the ordinary case rather than the constitutional one.
rigour: Steps 1–3 follow tightly from equal access to a competent-lawyer-grade reader, but step 2's claim that both models "asked honestly" return the same verdict smuggles in a determinacy and neutrality that a 2026 model interrogated by an angry claimant would not reliably deliver — the card admits this and then waves it away with an asymmetric "would only swap" move.
novelty: The framing that power is not transferred to the machine but revealed to have always been the official's is a sharp turn away from the AI-judge commentary, though legal realism and the "expertise as legitimacy" critique are well-trodden and the card's own objection says so.
arguability: The either-side is real and symmetrical — authority unmasked as force versus authority finally honest — and a thoughtful reader could land on either after the Weber/realist exchange.
story: The ALJ who cannot tell whether she is a judge or a coin is a genuine crisis with an office and a docket, though the claimant's side stays a vignette and "Gary flipped a coin" is closer to a position than a scene.
weakest step: (2) — the assumption that two instances of the same model, one prompted by the aggrieved claimant and one by the deciding official, converge on "both readings defensible," when framing, record selection and sycophancy make divergence the likelier output and the card's rebuttal changes the question rather than defending the step.

Q-28 | importance=4 | rigour=3 | novelty=4 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: It touches every family and the whole social position of the old, which is a life-scale matter, but it is a question about a bond rather than about an institution, so the stakes are wide rather than deep.
rigour: The chain is clean except for step 3, which needs the share to be better than the median elder on "should I, how do I tell him" questions — a strong claim the card supports only with "people acted on it in 2026," and step 5's leap from "the asking stops" to "contact stops" underrates the errand-free contact the card itself ends on.
novelty: The specific move — that it is equality, not exclusion, that demotes the grandmother from source to peer — is a real inversion of the left-behind-elder cliché, though the substance sits close to the familiar "Google replaced the elder" commentary the card's own objection raises.
arguability: The closing framing of love without need as either the purest or the thinnest bond is genuinely two-sided, and the "she is plainly better off as an individual" objection has teeth.
story: The widow asking her own share what she used to be asked, and getting a more patient version of her own answer, is the best single image in the batch and belongs to a specific person with a specific loss.
weakest step: (3) — the claim that a generalist model outperforms the median elder on the explicable questions families actually call about, which conflates well-formed advice with counsel that carries the weight of having been lived, and the card flags this without defending it.

Q-02 | importance=4 | rigour=3 | novelty=5 | arguability=4 | story=4
importance: Whether a culture can still have a history of taste is a civilizational question, but its bite falls hardest on creators and researchers, a narrower population than the other cards address.
rigour: Step 1's assumption that disposition froze with the weights is defensible, but step 3 (a 2026 mind says "interesting" to departure and noise alike) is asserted rather than derived — a competent generalist can be prompted for adversarial critique — and step 5's claim that new human readers are findable only through the instrument does not follow from a premise in which ordinary human institutions, journals and friendships continue as business as usual.
novelty: "Undiscriminating rather than hostile, and therefore worse than a hostile gatekeeper, because a wall at least tells you where you are" is the freshest idea in the batch and nothing in the usual homogenization commentary puts it this way.
arguability: A reader can side with the objection that novelty always had to survive blind readers and that the Victorians managed sixty years of one sensibility, but the card's own side is stated more forcefully than the counter, and "can a culture have a history" tilts toward yes on reflection.
story: The eulogy before Thursday and the mathematician born the year the weights froze are strong, but the crisis is epistemic — no one is harmed, nothing is lost except a verdict — so the price a person pays stays abstract.
weakest step: (5) — that the only test of the new is other humans who cannot be found without the instrument, which requires the model to have displaced every pre-existing human channel of discovery, a premise the card never grants itself.

Q-22 | importance=4 | rigour=3 | novelty=4 | arguability=4 | story=5
importance: If parental authority loses both its knowledge and its leverage props, that reshapes childhood and the family universally, which matters at full social scale.
rigour: Step 1 does the heaviest lifting and is the one the card flags: that a newborn's share is inalienable past early childhood and withholding it is like withholding a meal is a legal and moral stipulation, not an implication of "no one has none," and step 4's second prop (controlling the good things) collapses only if that stipulation holds, while parents in fact still control bedtime, money, housing and everything else.
novelty: The turn that a self-effacing helper which keeps saying "go and talk to your mum" still sets a standard of temperament is a real advance on the AI-tutor and screens material, and "the parent is the only adult who tires" is a good sentence.
arguability: The objection about grandmothers, teachers and the dog is genuinely strong, and the point that the parent's own share coaches her toward repair means patience rises on both sides — though the card's dramatization leans hard toward the bleak reading.
story: Dana, the tea, and "Don't tell her I said that" constitute the most complete scene in the batch, with a want, a shame and a room.
weakest step: (1) — the jump from "no one has none" to a child's share being unconfiscatable by her parents, which is a rule the premise does not contain and on which the whole authority-collapse claim, as opposed to the milder comparison claim, depends.

MOST IMPORTANT: Q-13, because it puts every administrative decision a state makes against a citizen on the question of whether legitimacy survives the loss of expert asymmetry.
MOST NOVEL: Q-06, because the audited-apprentice move — the people you practiced your judgment on can now price exactly what your self-cultivation cost them — is not a version of anything in the commentary.
DOES NOT SURVIVE: none, though Q-02 is the closest, since step 5 needs a world where the model has swallowed human discovery, which the "business as usual otherwise" premise denies it.
