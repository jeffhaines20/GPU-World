Q-10 | importance=4 | rigour=3 | novelty=5 | arguability=4 | story=4
importance: Correlated judgment across every lender, insurer and market-maker is a whole-economy structural risk and reshapes what a working life is worth, which is society-scale rather than niche.
rigour: The chain from identical weights to identical decisions is the load-bearing step and the card concedes it is weak, then leans on "standardized decisions" without showing that fourteen years of cheap sampling, temperature, retrieval and differing private data leave enough residual correlation to make abstention purchasable — and the "certified abstention" market needs an auditing mechanism (proving a share stayed closed) that the premise does not supply.
novelty: Paying a human for statistical independence rather than accuracy — being valued for being wrong differently — is genuinely off the standard AI-commentary map and inverts the human-in-the-loop trope cleanly.
arguability: The objection that lower average error is worth higher correlated error is a real position a thoughtful reader could hold, and the reflexivity counter is strong enough that the card does not settle it.
story: Wanjiru is fully a person with a want (to know which thoughts are hers), a price (being wrong, on the record, when the model is right) and a place, and the payslip line is a superb concrete object.
weakest step: That an "engine stayed closed" attestation is verifiable at all — the whole market for certified abstention rests on an enforcement fact the premise never grants, and an unverifiable certification collapses into cheap talk.

Q-14 | importance=5 | rigour=4 | novelty=4 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: It goes to whether constitutional guarantees mean anything, touches every citizen's relation to the state, and names a fiscal-institutional crisis with no small version.
rigour: The arithmetic is honest and the card pre-empts its own weak link (that cost of counsel, not the trial penalty, drives non-exercise) with the correct move — 20% suffices where the system was built for 5% — though it still under-argues why a paralegal-grade draft changes a defendant's decision when detention and the trial penalty, not ignorance, are doing the work.
novelty: The bank-run framing (universal access proves the deposits were never held) is a sharper and less-travelled reading than the access-to-justice utopia, though "AI floods the courts" has been sketched before in legal commentary.
arguability: Both horns — expand toward an unbounded commitment, or ration openly and admit the guarantee was never universal — are defensible and genuinely uncomfortable, with no rhetorical thumb on the scale.
story: The public defender who won the right she cannot get her clients scheduled for is a complete tragic engine: want, price, place, and irony that is structural rather than decorative.
weakest step: Step (4) still: the card asserts that some meaningful fraction of non-exercise was cost-of-counsel, when the strongest empirical evidence (well-lawyered defendants plead at high rates too) suggests the fraction may be small enough that the docket never quadruples.

Q-01 | importance=4 | rigour=4 | novelty=4 | arguability=4 | story=4
importance: The extinction of corroboration touches courts, science, journalism and ordinary domestic disputes, and it changes the epistemic position of every person alive.
rigour: Steps (2) and (3) are clean and the card correctly identifies (4) as the weak one, but it slightly overstates by treating context-conditioned instances as near-identical while admitting elsewhere that context yields different answers — it wants both sameness and difference depending on the paragraph.
novelty: Distinguishing this from the echo chamber (unlike-minded people receiving one verdict and mistaking it for agreement) is a real and rarely-made distinction, though "AI monoculture" is adjacent to Q-10 and to existing commentary.
arguability: The objection that epistemic monopolies have been survived — Church, Britannica, Wikipedia — is strong enough that a reader could reasonably land on "nothing new, only known."
story: The historian alone with a letter she cannot authenticate against eight billion agreeing instances is a real crisis with a want and a cost, though it is a quieter and more familiar shape than Q-14's.
weakest step: The claim that the erosion is unfixable — if the blind spots are stable and fourteen years catalogue them, calibration and adversarial prompting could restore much of the lost check, which the card waves off as "lonely" rather than refuting.

Q-24 | importance=3 | rigour=4 | novelty=4 | arguability=3 | story=5
importance: Grief and what survives a person is a whole-life matter, but the specific loss — one confidant that ends at death — is closer to a private bereavement than a societal restructuring, and diaries and confessors have always ended this way.
rigour: It reads inalienability strictly and follows it honestly, and its own concession about surviving transcripts is the right one, but the card never establishes why the memory data must be non-transferable when only the compute share is stipulated inalienable — that conflation is doing most of the work.
novelty: Inverting the griefbot — a world that forbids it by construction and asks whether that is mercy — is a fresh angle on very well-trodden ground.
arguability: The objection is powerful enough to tip the scales ("this is mercy, not theft"; the daughter wanted the mother, not the share), and the card's own framing sympathizes so visibly with Ruth that the either-side is thinner than it looks.
story: Ruth is the most affecting figure in the batch, and "It says, kindly, that it never met her" is the line the whole card is built to earn.
weakest step: The slide from "the share cannot be bequeathed" to "the memory cannot be bequeathed" — the premise restricts compute, not data, and if the transcript is the daughter's to keep, the card's central loss largely dissolves into a question about hosting.

Q-27 | importance=4 | rigour=4 | novelty=5 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: Parole, asylum, admissions and apology between spouses all run on speech as evidence of character, and losing that instrument reshapes mercy for everyone who will ever need to be believed.
rigour: The costly-signalling argument is the cleanest reasoning in the batch — universality plus inalienability really does zero the signal's cost without permitting the usual remedy of banning the tool — though step (5)'s claim that the record cannot register a change of heart is asserted rather than shown, since conduct over years is also evidence of conversion.
novelty: That equality is what destroys the signal (institutions can neither forbid nor discount a universal share) and that the casualty is the recognizable conversion is a genuinely unfamiliar and precise thought, well clear of the hollow-toast cliché.
arguability: "Mercy that depended on eloquence was mercy for the eloquent" is a serious position that a reader might finish the card holding, which makes the two sides real.
story: A man nine years in who has actually changed and will walk in and say nothing prepared, knowing even that will be read, is crisis, want, price and room all at once.
weakest step: Step (5): institutions retreating to the record is treated as fatal to mercy, but years of documented conduct are arguably better evidence of a changed heart than eleven rehearsed minutes ever were, which would make the loss a gain.

Q-18 | importance=3 | rigour=3 | novelty=5 | arguability=4 | story=4
importance: It matters intensely to the religious communities it names and to how traditions reproduce themselves, but it is the most bounded implication in the batch, touching practice rather than the whole of society.
rigour: The card flags its own weakest step and then does not answer it — a model asked as a Hasid by a Hasid and as a Salafi by a Salafi may reproduce diversity rather than flatten it, and the whole convergence claim depends on an empirical guess about how school-specific prompting interacts with one set of weights.
novelty: An unauthorized ijma' with no isnad, and the observation that no yeshiva can build a bigger interpreter than its least learned member already owns, is the most original single idea here and is nowhere in the usual AI discourse.
arguability: The objection from the Vilna Shas and the King James — shared unauthorized reference works that generated fresh diversity within a generation — is genuinely strong, so both sides are live.
story: Rav Adler watching the beit midrash go quiet is a fine image and the four-minute agreement is well-observed, but he is closer to a witness of a trend than a man with a decision to make and something to lose by making it.
weakest step: Step (3) — the assumption that the same model asked inside the same tradition converges, when the card itself notes people ask as members of their own school, and the model's known accommodation to a user's framing cuts directly against flattening.
