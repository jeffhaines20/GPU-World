## Q-01

### reviewer-a — importance 4, rigour 4, novelty 4, arguability 4, story 4

importance: The extinction of corroboration touches courts, science, journalism and ordinary domestic disputes, and it changes the epistemic position of every person alive.
rigour: Steps (2) and (3) are clean and the card correctly identifies (4) as the weak one, but it slightly overstates by treating context-conditioned instances as near-identical while admitting elsewhere that context yields different answers — it wants both sameness and difference depending on the paragraph.
novelty: Distinguishing this from the echo chamber (unlike-minded people receiving one verdict and mistaking it for agreement) is a real and rarely-made distinction, though "AI monoculture" is adjacent to Q-10 and to existing commentary.
arguability: The objection that epistemic monopolies have been survived — Church, Britannica, Wikipedia — is strong enough that a reader could reasonably land on "nothing new, only known."
story: The historian alone with a letter she cannot authenticate against eight billion agreeing instances is a real crisis with a want and a cost, though it is a quieter and more familiar shape than Q-14's.
weakest step: The claim that the erosion is unfixable — if the blind spots are stable and fourteen years catalogue them, calibration and adversarial prompting could restore much of the lost check, which the card waves off as "lonely" rather than refuting.

### reviewer-b — importance 5, rigour 4, novelty 4, arguability 4, story 4

importance: If corroboration becomes structurally unavailable, every institution that runs on independent checks — courts, science, journalism, medicine — loses its error-correction, which is about as society-scale as implications get.
rigour: The one-model-one-error-distribution argument follows tightly from the premise as granted, though step (3) overstates: context conditioning and sampling do produce partially decorrelated errors, and the card's objection paragraph raises this and then does not rebut it.
novelty: "Not an echo chamber but unlike-minded people receiving one verdict and mistaking it for agreement" is a sharper formulation than the standard homogenization or hallucination commentary, though monoculture-of-models is discussed in the field.
arguability: The question of whether to trust your own eyes against a mind that is usually right is genuinely two-sided, and the objection about surviving epistemic monopolies has real force.
story: The historian with a letter she cannot authenticate against one opinion held eight billion times is a person in a place with a price, but her crisis is more an epistemic predicament than a dramatic want.
weakest step: Step (3)'s "do not err independently" is asserted at full strength when the honest version is "err with high correlation," and the whole conclusion about corroboration going extinct needs the full strength, not the honest version.
