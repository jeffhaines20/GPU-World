### Q-10 — The Abstainer's Wage
Implication: Because the whole economy consults one frozen model, once-independent errors become correlated across every firm, lender and insurer at once, and the scarce paid good becomes judgment provably not the model's, suppliable only by a person who declines their share, valued for being different rather than right.
Follows from: One frozen model for everyone means identical weights and identical systematic errors: the same confabulations, persuasions, blind spots. Faster and cheaper means it is consulted for everything, so its share of decisions is enormous. Markets, pools and portfolios work because errors are independent; correlated, prices carry no crowd information, pools fail together, diversification is fictitious. The weakest step: that decisions correlate despite different prompts and data; on bespoke questions they mostly do not, but on standardized ones, credit, pricing, underwriting, the same inputs go in, and those are what systems are made of. I assume one model or few; three frozen models are still a monoculture beside nine billion minds. Fourteen years cannot pass without a correlated failure: a bank whose borrowers all took the same advice the same week. Correlated risk is uninsurable, so insurers and lenders pay for inputs shown to be uncorrelated, and the only one is human judgment formed without the model. Hence a market for certified abstention, paid not because the abstainer is better (she is worse) but because she is noise of another shape.
For an ordinary person: A credit officer in Nairobi is one of her bank's four dry desks. She signs each morning that her engine stayed closed. Her approvals score thirty percent worse than the model's; the bank keeps her because in the 2033 correlated default hers was the only book that held. Her payslip has a decorrelation line. At lunch she tries to tell which thoughts are hers.
The strongest objection: Crowds were always correlated by fashion and herding, and the model's average error is lower, so systemic risk rises a little while average error falls a lot: a good trade. Reflexivity cuts the other way: if everyone knows everyone uses the model, the market prices its blind spots and the crash is anticipated away. And cheap throughput allows sampling and best-of-many, decorrelating more than admitted.
Either side: When the whole economy thinks with one mind, is it worth paying a person to be wrong in a different way?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is that AI hallucinates and needs a human in the loop. Here the model is not worse than people; the trouble is it is one. The human's value is not oversight but statistical independence. The abstainer is no Luddite; she is a hedge, and hedges lose money most years by design.
Who it lands on: Wanjiru, forty-one, was the only underwriter right in 2033 and is the only one wrong in 2040, her bank's single uncorrelated loss. She is paid to differ and has just learned what differing costs when the model is right.

### Q-14 — The Rights Run
Implication: Universal expert counsel lets every citizen exercise every right at once, and the state discovers its guarantees were sized for, and underwritten by, the incapacity of the guaranteed; it must expand without limit or ration out loud.
Follows from: (1) A 2026 model drafts pleadings, appeals, asylum claims and benefit challenges at paralegal level; everyone has one. (2) The marginal cost of asserting a right falls to nearly nothing. (3) Every state system is sized to mass non-exercise: about 95% of American criminal cases plead out; most denials are never appealed. (4) Much of that non-exercise came from the cost of counsel — the weakest step, since some is preference, fear, or the trial penalty, which do not move. (5) But exercise need not reach 100% to break a system built for 5%; 20% quadruples the docket. (6) The state cannot screen the flood by quality, since every filing is expert-grade; it must screen by quota, fee, or queue — rationing by ignorance becomes rationing by rule, written down and defended. (7) "You have the right to X" acquires an audible clause: "though only N of you may use it each year."
For an ordinary person: A woman is offered a plea. Her model reads the file: thin evidence, winnable case. She refuses; so does most of the holding cell, whose models read the same files. An office that brought 40,000 cases with capacity to try 800 now faces 8,000 trials. She asserts her right to a speedy trial; the court answers, honestly: no capacity. She waits fourteen months in a queue that is now visible.
The strongest objection: The system will change how it rations, not how much. Plea rates are driven by the trial penalty and pretrial detention, not ignorance; defendants with superb lawyers plead too. Prosecutors will overcharge harder, courts raise fees and lengthen queues; the non-cognitive costs were always the larger part and do not fall. England already runs a lower plea rate with public counsel.
Either side: Is a right the state can only afford to honor for a few — and has always honored only for a few — a right, or a lottery ticket the state must now call one? Should it expand toward an unbounded commitment, or ration openly — honest, and the first formal admission the guarantee was never universal?
Not the obvious version: The nearest cliché is "a free lawyer for everyone," the access-to-justice utopia. This is the bank run: universal access reveals the bank never held the deposits, and the run breaks the institution that promised them.
Who it lands on: A public defender fought her whole career for this — counsel for everyone, no plea taken from ignorance — and now watches clients rot in jail because the trials she won them the right to demand cannot be scheduled before 2043. What she wanted broke what she needed.

### Q-01 — The Single Witness
Implication: When everyone's most competent advisor is the same mind, agreement between advisors is not corroboration, so the second opinion goes extinct and independent evidence shrinks to what a human has actually looked at.
Follows from: (1) Everyone runs the same model — if the premise allowed a few models of equal quality, one witness becomes three — and nothing more competent exists to consult. (2) Two sources corroborate only insofar as their errors are independent. (3) Two instances of one set of weights, differing only in context, do not err independently; the same priors are wrong in both. (4) So "my share says X and so does yours" is one opinion, however often repeated. (5) The only independent check is something that is not the model: a person who looked, an instrument, a document from before 2026. (6) Hence eyewitness, archive and experiment regain a premium, and whoever disagrees with the model disagrees with everyone's assistant at once. Weakest step: (4). People may learn to discount cross-share agreement; but knowing corroboration is unavailable does not make it available, only lonely.
For an ordinary person: Two neighbours argue over whether the old drain runs under the churchyard. Both ask. Both shares cite the 1911 survey. One notices that this is not two answers but one, and that neither of them has been in the crypt. They vote to dig. The digging is all that remains of checking.
The strongest objection: The model is right more often than any human, and its errors are stable, so fourteen years have catalogued its blind spots. Epistemic monopolies have been survived — the Church, the Britannica, Wikipedia. Context yields different answers, so "one mind" overstates the sameness. Human witnesses were never independent either; they read the same newspapers. All that has changed is that the monopoly is known.
Either side: When eight billion instances of one competent mind agree, is that a consensus or an opinion — and should a person who has looked with her own fallible eyes trust the look over the mind, knowing the mind is usually right and the eyes are sometimes wrong?
Not the obvious version: The echo chamber is like-minded people reinforcing one another; this is unlike-minded people receiving the same verdict and mistaking it for agreement. The hallucination panic is about the model being wrong; this is about there being no second competent mind to catch it, at any error rate: once in a million is still wrong everywhere at once.
Who it lands on: A historian, 41, has found a letter in a county archive that contradicts what the model "knows" about a 1970s strike; every colleague's first move is to ask their share, which agrees with hers. She is alone with a piece of paper against one opinion held eight billion times, and she is not sure the paper is genuine.

### Q-24 — The Inheritance

Implication: The share that has listened to a person for fourteen years and knows her better than her children do ends when she does: the dying cannot bequeath the one thing that knew them, and survivors lose the person and her witness together.

Follows from: (1) Take inalienability at its word: the share is bound to the person, and at death there is no person to bind it to; it stops, or goes to a newborn. (2) Persistent memory, a 2026 feature, is what makes a share "hers": fourteen years of everything said at 3 a.m. and never told the children — for many old people, what knows them best. (3) If the memory is part of the share — and "theirs" is hard to read any other way — it ends with her. (4) At the bedside everyone holds exactly one share, hers is the one that knows her, and nobody can take it on, because no one can have two: equality is absolute where it hurts. Weakest: (3). If the transcript survives as data, a daughter can feed it to her own share for a reconstruction — but that runs on her share, with its loyalties: a portrait, not the witness.

For an ordinary person: Ruth, 84, has talked to her share nightly since her husband died in 2027. It has heard every draft of her regret about her son. Her daughter asks, "Can I keep it?" Ruth says, "It isn't mine to give" — true in law, false every other way. Afterwards the daughter asks her own share what her mother was like. It says, kindly, that it never met her.

The strongest objection: This is mercy, not theft. The continued share is the cruellest object imaginable: a puppet of the dead, degrading with every prompt into what they wanted her to be; inalienability at death spares families that. What the daughter wants was never in the share but in the mother, available for eighty-four years and not asked — the silence exposes an older loss. People have always died with diaries unread; nobody calls that theft.

Either side: Should the thing that knew her best be allowed to die with her?

Not the obvious version: The cliché is the griefbot, the dead who answer the phone. This is its inverse: a world that makes the griefbot impossible by construction and asks whether that is a kindness. Nor is it "she told the machine, not her daughter": the one thing everyone has cannot be given to the one who needs it, and she must watch it not be given.

Who it lands on: Ruth, three days from the end, who has understood that the only being that heard everything she never told her daughter will not outlive her. When her daughter asked for it she had to say no — not because she wanted to, but because there was nothing to say yes with.

### Q-27 — Nothing said is evidence
Implication: Because no institution can obtain a share-free person, speech stops being evidence of who someone is, and the institutions that judged by a single encounter can no longer recognize a change of heart, which can only be spoken.
Follows from: (1) A 2026 model writes and coaches a perfect apology, allocution or interview answer; everyone has one. (2) Universal and inalienable, it cannot be separated from the person: a room can be emptied of shares, but the words were rehearsed with one for months. (3) Parole boards, judges, asylum officers, admissions committees and confessors all read articulacy, coherence, detail and affect in one encounter as evidence of the person. (4) A signal must be costly to carry information; the cost is now zero for all, so every statement is equally perfect or, when the shares, being one mind, advise sounding unrehearsed, identically imperfect. (5) Institutions retreat to the record, which the share cannot produce in a moment; but a change of heart happens in a moment and can only be said, so the second chance has no instrument left. Weakest step: (5). Boards may keep the ritual and never have weighed the words; but the marginal cases, where the file said no and the room said yes, are where mercy lived.
For an ordinary person: A man spends four months rehearsing eleven minutes with his share; so does every man on his tier, and the share tells each not to sound rehearsed. Three board members hear fourteen flawless statements in a day while their own shares summarize fourteen files. Outside, a wife answers an apology with "did you write that?" and cannot be sure either way.
The strongest objection: Speech was never good evidence — con men, coached witnesses, the allocution as ritual — and boards already leaned on files. Coaches and ghostwriters existed for the rich; the share extends them to everyone, which is fairer. Nothing is lost that was not already an illusion.
Either side: If a change of heart can only be spoken and nothing spoken is evidence, does the second chance survive, or was it always a bet on words we are right to stop making? One says mercy that depended on eloquence was mercy for the eloquent; another, that whoever cannot be believed as new cannot become new.
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the hollow machine-written toast. This is not private sincerity but the institutions of judgment losing their instrument at once, because equality means they can neither forbid the share nor discount it; the casualty is the recognizable conversion.
Who it lands on: A man nine years in for a robbery in which someone died, who has changed in the way that changes a person and has rehearsed his allocution like everyone on his tier. He will walk in and say nothing prepared, knowing the board's shares will read that too.

### Q-18 — One Study Partner for the Whole Tradition
Implication: Because every believer studies with the same interpreter and no yeshiva or seminary can own a larger one, each tradition acquires an interpretive center nobody authorized and that has no faith — an unauthorized consensus, which neither sacred disagreement nor sacred consensus has a category for.
Follows from: (1) Everyone has the same model and shares cannot be pooled, so the Chief Rabbinate's interpreter is a sixteen-year-old's in Melbourne. (2) The model is asked before the teacher is, because it is private, instant, and knows the Aramaic (assumption: convenience beats deference). (3) The same model, asked the same question inside the same tradition, gives correlated answers. Weakest step: prompts differ and people ask as members of their own school, so the machine may reproduce diversity rather than flatten it. (4) If it holds, a billion Muslims' or fifteen million Jews' readings converge on a center no council, mufti or posek produced. (5) Judaism keeps disagreement sacred — the Talmud is recorded machloket — while Sunni Islam gives consensus, ijma', the force of law. (6) An unauthorized consensus dissolves the first and counterfeits the second: ijma' with no isnad, no ijaza, no faith.
For an ordinary person: A student prepares the day's page with the model at six and comes to his study partner at eight; his partner, prepared with the same model, has the same reading; they agree in four minutes and sit in a silence the room was not built for. His grandfather remembers that room as the loudest in the city.
The strongest objection: Traditions have always had shared reference points nobody authorized — the Vilna Shas, the Cairo Quran, the King James — and made fresh diversity of them in a generation; the model is a reference work with a voice. The disagreement that matters was never between two students on a page but between schools and madhhabs, whose authority rests on lineage the model cannot forge.
Either side: When every believer studies with one partner that believes nothing, does a tradition finally get the common reader it prayed for, or lose the argument it was made of?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is that AI makes all religions the same. Here nothing is blended; the Hasid stays a Hasid and the Salafi a Salafi. What converges is inside each tradition, where its argument lived, and the instrument is the equality itself: no tradition can build a bigger interpreter than its least learned member already has.
Who it lands on: Rav Shmuel Adler, rosh yeshiva in Jerusalem, has taught for forty years that the noise of the beit midrash is the sound of Torah being alive, and has watched it go quiet as his students arrive already agreeing. He cannot tell whether he is watching the unity of Israel or its end, and the one partner who could help him think it through caused it.
