### Q-26 — The lucid remainder
Implication: A person's share keeps full capacity while she declines, holds years of her expressed will, cannot be taken or switched off by her family, and stops only at death: her last lucid organ, and a second, fluent claimant to who she is now, answering whoever asks.
Follows from: (1) One share per person, always on, fixed at 2026 quality: its capacity does not track hers, at eighty-five with dementia as at forty. This cuts both ways: what does not decline never learned to refuse a leading question, so whose share it is turns on who holds the prompt. (2) Fourteen years of daily use leave it holding her phrasings, values and decisions, given persistent memory: not granted, but the business-as-usual default; 2026 assistants had it. (3) It is hers, not saleable or poolable: her children cannot take it or turn it off, only decline to ask it or burn its record, knowingly. (4) Infants have shares, so a guardian directs a ward's share without owning it; hers, prompted by her guardian, answers in her words. The simplest civil rule denies those answers standing: her record to the date of incapacity, extrapolation after. But incapacity brings instruments into force, not out of it, and a guardian's usual test, substituted judgment, asks what she would have chosen; this will say, listened to or not. (5) At death it stops: it neither declines nor outlives her as hers. Weakest step: (2): the premise guarantees the engine, not the memory; without the record, an advance directive.
For an ordinary person: Tuesday mornings a retired teacher of 61, diagnosed early, briefs her share on what to say when she no longer can (the house, the tube, her son's wife, the dog), and on how to answer her son, who will be asking. By then it will say them better than she can, and be believed more. Afternoons she has it repeat what she said in March and corrects it; then asks it as her son will, and corrects that.
The strongest objection: Directives and proxies exist, and the law has argued for fifty years whether the past self binds the present one, Dworkin's precedent autonomy against Dresser's present interests. The share is a better-indexed diary, weighed as one input; a proxy who prefers the woman in the bed can decline to ask it. Worse: after incapacity the family holds the prompt, and a 2026 model goes where it is led. It outlasts her as theirs, not hers: an interface to a patient who cannot contradict it.
Either side: Is her share's account of what she wants her will, or a fiction in her voice that must yield to the woman in the bed, who smiles at the window and eats when fed? Either the fourteen years and the briefing are more her than this afternoon is, or a will is an act, not a record, and a record that answers its asker is the asker's.
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the griefbot, the simulated dead. This is the living outlasted by their own counsel: her own tool, not a simulation, at full power on the day she is weakest. Nor a better directive: a directive is a document; this answers, and can be led.
Who it lands on: The teacher, who has understood that she is briefing her own successor, a more persuasive witness to her wishes than she will be when it matters, and that her son will be asking. She has not decided whether to tell it to overrule her.

## What changed
Answered reviewer-a on (4): the premise's "always on" means incapacity cannot mute the share; the law can deny its answers standing, and the card now grants that it will (her record to the incapacity date, extrapolation after). That dates the problem rather than ending it, and a guardian's substituted-judgment test still asks what the share will say. Answered reviewer-b on (1): conceded that a 2026 model follows its prompter, so after incapacity the family holds the prompt; folded that into the objection, both sides, and the teacher's Tuesday, where she now rehearses her son's questions. Narrowed the implication: "cannot be switched off" covers the engine; the record can be burned, as a diary can, only knowingly. Named persistent memory as the assumption it is and moved the weakest step to (2); it cannot be derived from the granted text. The implication survives, narrower and still two-sided.
