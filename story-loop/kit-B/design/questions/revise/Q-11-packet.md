# Revision packet for Q-11

## The card as it stands

### Q-11 — The Ghost Estate
Implication: Since compute cannot be accumulated, the only cognitive capital that grows is context, the years of memory, corrections and taught habits fitting one person's identical engine to their life, and because context is a file, the inalienable share casts an alienable shadow, the most valuable thing a person can sell or leave.
Follows from: Identical engines that cannot be pooled differ only in configuration: instructions, memory, tools, years of feedback. A 2026-quality model with fourteen curated years is far more useful for one life or trade than a blank one, though no smarter: the ceiling holds, the floor rises. That is the weakest step: the premise says never much better; I claim fittedness, not intelligence. Context compounds, so inequality re-enters through context, not compute. Context is data, and business as usual makes data property: copyable, inheritable. This is the case the premise flags: the engine stays locked to the person; its state does not. Markets follow: a surgeon's context licensed to a hospital, a parent's willed to children; estates list it beside the house. But a context runs only on an engine, and every buyer has one. The heir who loads her father's context runs her one engine as him, at the cost of her own: what can be inherited needs what cannot, and a firm can host a ghost on an employee's engine; an individual must evict herself.
For an ordinary person: A carpenter in Oaxaca inherits his uncle's fourteen-year context: every job, every client, every correction. Loaded, his engine quotes like the old man, drafts like him, greets clients by nickname. Unloaded, it is a stranger's blank tool. He cannot run both, so he chooses daily, and clients have noticed which days are which.
The strongest objection: Contexts do not transfer: a surgeon's knows her hands, not yours, so the market is thin and the estate sentimental. Taken seriously, never much better means the gap between blank and taught is small. And ordinary law may block it from both sides: privacy rules could make contexts non-transferable personal data, and employers could claim whatever was taught on their hours.
Either side: Should the shape of a mind be inheritable when the engine that runs it is not, and is loading a dead man's context a way of keeping him or of losing yourself?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is digital immortality, the upload, the ghost with rights. This is not a copy of anyone but a 2026-quality model wearing a dead man's habits, and runs only by displacing the living. The story is not the ghost's claims but the heir's opportunity cost, which is her whole allotted mind.
Who it lands on: Ines, twenty-nine, inherits her father's context, appraised at more than his house, and learns that keeping him means spending her one engine being him, while being paid means letting a stranger's engine be him. She has not opened her own in three months.

## The two reviewers' critiques of this card (scores 1 to 5 on importance, rigour, novelty, arguability, story; then one sentence per axis and the weakest step)

### reviewer-a — importance 4, rigour 3, novelty 5, arguability 4, story 5

importance: It is the card that explains how inequality and inheritance re-enter a world engineered to abolish them, which is a societal-scale claim about property, estates and class, not a niche.
rigour: The card is admirably honest that "fittedness compounds" is its load-bearing assumption, but it rests on that assumption twice over — that fourteen years of context is worth more than a house, and that a context is portable enough to price — and the premise's "never much better" pushes against both.
novelty: The inalienable share casting an alienable shadow, and the heir paying her whole allotted mind to host a dead man, is a genuinely new turn on inheritance that I have not seen in the AI commentary.
arguability: A reader can defend both "loading your father is keeping him" and "it is self-eviction," though the card's own objection (contexts do not transfer; the market is thin) tilts toward deflation slightly harder than the card admits.
story: Ines, who has not opened her own engine in three months, and the carpenter whose clients have noticed which days are which, are both crises with a visible daily price.
weakest step: The leap from "a taught context is more useful" to "a context is the most valuable thing a person can sell or leave" — a scale claim with no argument, only the assertion that it compounds, in a world where the ceiling is fixed and every buyer already has fourteen years of their own.

### reviewer-b — importance 4, rigour 4, novelty 5, arguability 4, story 5

importance: If inequality re-enters an equal-compute world through an inheritable asset, that is the central economic fact of the premise and shapes everyone's prospects.
rigour: Strong and self-aware — it derives from the granted inalienability plus "business as usual makes data property," and the one stretch (fittedness versus intelligence) is named and correctly distinguished from capability gain; the mutual-exclusion constraint (one engine, one context at a time) is a real consequence rather than a smuggled one.
novelty: The alienable shadow of an inalienable share, with inheritance costing the heir her own mind rather than granting her a ghost, is the freshest economic move in the batch and is nowhere in the common commentary.
arguability: Both sides are live — sentimental thin market and legal blocking versus compounding context capital — though the card's own strongest objection slightly undercuts its premise rather than opposing it evenly.
story: Ines, not having opened her own engine in three months, is a person, a place, a want and an exact price, and the Oaxaca carpenter whose clients notice which days are which is a whole story on its own.
weakest step: The move from "context compounds" to "context is worth more than a house" — if "never much better" is taken seriously the blank-to-taught gap may be modest, and the card's market, estate and displacement drama all need that gap to be large.
