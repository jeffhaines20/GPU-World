Q-08 | importance=4 | rigour=3 | novelty=5 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: Promising is load-bearing for every intimate relationship and for the whole idea of binding oneself over time, so a change in its structure reaches across a life, not a niche.
rigour: The chain from "always-on model with years of your notes" to "everyone now silently holds a number before every promise" needs the model to hold enough biography *and* enough predictive skill to make the number feel authoritative, and the card's own reply ("the story is set there") narrows the premise rather than deriving from it — the daughter's model, holding only her view of him, plausibly gives a number so noisy it changes nothing.
novelty: The move from "AI predicts you" to "the private availability of a base rate strips the predictive content out of promising and leaves the undertaking naked" is a genuinely fresh cut that a reader steeped in AI commentary would not have met.
arguability: The Arendt/Nietzsche objection is strong enough that a thoughtful reader can land either on "purified promising" or "an agreed lie," and the card does not stack the deck.
story: The sober father at thirty-five percent, with three exits all of which cost him something, is a fully realised crisis with a want, a price and a room.
weakest step: Step (4)'s claim that the disjunction is inescapable — since asking is free but *believing* a mediocre number is not compelled, a person may simply hold the forecast in contempt, which the card never tests.

Q-12 | importance=5 | rigour=3 | novelty=5 | arguability=4 | story=5
importance: It identifies the single structural leak in an equal distribution — that equality of ownership is not equality of direction — and follows it to household economics, disability, and end-of-life decisions.
rigour: The card is honest that its load-bearing step is legal (that law lets a guardian direct a ward's engine outward rather than quarantining it), but the argument for why no legislature would quarantine is asserted through a rhetorical flourish ("no legislature chooses that") rather than argued, and the premise's own ban on pooling reads naturally as a ban on exactly this.
novelty: Dependents as compute assets, and the resulting quiet de facto pooling inside families where nobody signs anything, is well outside the usual AI-inequality discourse.
arguability: The care/extraction question is live, though the Coogan-account objection is somewhat weaker than the case it answers, tilting the reader slightly toward "extraction."
story: Lourdes running two translators under one name, unable to find where her wanting ends and the sum begins, is a crisis with a body, a bill and a decision pending.
weakest step: The inference from "no legislature idles ward-engines" to "guardians may sell what ward-engines produce" skips the obvious middle option — direction permitted only for the ward's own benefit, with output non-commercial — which the card waves off as "a door, not a fence" without showing why the fence fails.

Q-26 | importance=4 | rigour=4 | novelty=4 | arguability=5 | story=4
importance: Who speaks for a person after capacity goes is a question every family eventually meets, and the card makes it a question about a whole life's relation to its own record.
rigour: The derivation is careful and self-critical, and the one granted extra — persistent memory — is defended as a 2026 default rather than smuggled, though the card's admission that "a 2026 model goes where it is led" partly dissolves its own claim that the share is a *second claimant* rather than a family-operated puppet.
novelty: Distinguishing this from the griefbot (the living outlasted by their own counsel, at full power on the day they are weakest) is a real distinction, but the substituted-judgment debate it feeds into is well-trodden ground the card mostly re-equips.
arguability: Dworkin against Dresser is the paradigm of a question thoughtful people genuinely split on, and both sides are given their best form here.
story: The Tuesday-and-afternoon ritual of briefing and correcting a successor is a striking image, but the crisis stays interior and the son remains a function rather than a person with his own want.
weakest step: Step (4)'s leap from "substituted judgment asks what she would have chosen" to "her share's answers therefore have standing" — since a leadable model prompted by an interested guardian is evidentially worse than the fixed record it draws on, the law's simple rule (record yes, extrapolation no) is not obviously defeated.

Q-06 | importance=5 | rigour=4 | novelty=4 | arguability=5 | story=5
importance: If the frontier is frozen and everyone shares one ceiling, whether anyone may still develop judgment past it is a question about the species' practical wisdom and about every parent, teacher and supervisor in it.
rigour: The chain is tight and the card concedes the contested step, but it leans on "counsel precedes every decision" as a behavioural fact when the premise grants only availability — a person who does not consult faces no chosen departure at all, and the card never argues why not consulting is unavailable.
novelty: The auditability twist — the apprentice is audited by her material, the son running his childhood back through his own share — takes this well past the familiar deskilling-and-atrophy commentary.
arguability: Williams's moral self-indulgence is a genuinely dangerous objection to the card's own thesis, so either verdict is reachable.
story: The son arriving at nineteen with an itemised list and the question "was I raised or practiced on" is the sharpest personal crisis in the batch.
weakest step: Step (2)'s claim that a chosen departure is therefore an imposition without consent — most of ordinary life involves deciding without running the best available advice, and the card asserts rather than shows why the mere presence of counsel converts ordinary risk into a debt owed.

Q-21 | importance=3 | rigour=3 | novelty=4 | arguability=4 | story=4
importance: It matters for the texture of long marriages, which is most people's central relationship, but the stakes stay domestic and the card's own class inversion is sketched rather than developed.
rigour: The argument requires that the share be *strictly better and free* at exactly the tasks spouses ask each other for, and then that the pretext dies rather than migrating — but the objection's point (pretexts are infinite; "look at that bird" costs nothing) is not answered, only restated, and the card concedes it cannot say how many people can make a naked bid.
novelty: "The share takes not the wish but its dress" is a fine, unfamiliar formulation, and the observation that bodily needs keep their cover while cognitive ones lose it is a distinction the commentary has not made.
arguability: Both readings of the ending — nothing in it, or nothing in the way — are genuinely available, though the card's warmth toward Margaret nudges the reader toward loss.
story: Margaret dating her husband's last non-physical request to 2031 is a precise, moving detail, but the crisis has no event, no deadline and no price anyone pays.
weakest step: Step (2)'s inference that a false need cannot dress a bid — couples sustain transparently false pretexts constantly ("tell me if this tie works"), and the card gives no reason why knowing the need is false destroys the dress here but not there.

MOST IMPORTANT: Q-06, because a permanently frozen ceiling makes the right to cultivate judgment past it a question about whether human practical wisdom continues at all, and it lands on an ordinary parent.
MOST NOVEL: Q-12, because dependents becoming net assets and equality leaking into de facto family pooling is a structural consequence no one in this discourse has named.
DOES NOT SURVIVE: Q-21, because its central step — that a known-false need can no longer dress a bid for attention — is contradicted by how couples actually use pretexts, and its "crisis" is a mood with no event, no deadline and nobody paying.
