### Q-05 — cell B — The Unasked Question
Implication: When a competent, unforgetting stranger's reading of your own life is one sentence away, ignorance stops being a condition and becomes an act, and not-asking cannot be told apart, even from inside, from trust.
Follows from: (1) Everyone has a private model, always on, answering at a thoughtful adult's level from what it is told: in ordinary use, her calendar, receipts, moved dinners. (2) "I didn't know" always sheltered facts you could not get and inferences you did not draw. The model adds no facts; it draws the inference from her own words, beating her head only by forgetting nothing, wanting nothing, and taking a second where she would need days of being a suspicious person, the labour most half-wonderings died of. Now letting go is something she does to a suspicion, not something that happens to it. (3) So "did you know?" gains a third answer, "I didn't ask," about her, not the world. (4) Asking need not feel like accusation; normalized into a lookup, it only makes not-asking rarer, more marked, more of an act. Trust was a default while checking was hard; with checking free and traceless, taking someone at their word is a daily refusal, the only trust that costs anything. (5) Walking past the door is blindness from fear and trust from love, and she cannot tell which: her motive is one more thing she would have to ask about. Weakest step: (2). If she already knows what the Thursdays add up to, the door is a mirror and the premise adds only speed. But speed is the point: what a second could settle cannot be let die, only declined.
For an ordinary person: Her husband says Thursday will be late. Before, she would half-wonder and let it go. Now the wondering has a door whose shape she can feel, and she notices herself not opening it; behind it, every late Thursday, since she moves the dinners. Her son's silence, her mother's balance: doors. The mole on her shoulder has no one behind it to trust, only a result to fear; she walks past it the same way, feeling no difference. Five a day; she calls it faith.
The strongest objection: Trust was never ignorance; it is looking without suspicion, not refusing to look. Asking "is he all right?" is attention, not betrayal, and once everyone asks, refusing is the superstition that kept people from opening test results. The model knows nothing of him; it holds her words and can tell her nothing she has not half-told herself. The door is a mirror, the crisis hers, not the premise's.
Either side: When checking is free and leaves no trace on him, is there a duty to ask your model about the people you love, or is not asking the last act of trust a person can still perform?
Not the obvious version: The nearest cliché is the transparent society: nothing hidden, no excuses. Here no one is watched; the model holds only what she has said of him. What changes is not the world's opacity but the ownership of her own.
Who it lands on: A woman who for eleven years never put a question about her husband to her model, a discipline she privately called loyalty; then he is arrested, and the detective asks, gently, whether she really never asked. She sees her trust and her blindness were one act and cannot tell which she performed; the reader that could, holding every Thursday she ever mentioned, is the one she still has not asked.

### Q-06 — cell B — Practicing on People
Implication: Because the model is good but never superhuman, no one has more than one, and it is there before every decision, the only way past it is departing from it, on live cases, before knowing one is right, which makes cultivating one's own judgment a risk run, without consent, on others.
Follows from: (1) The model advises at a thoughtful generalist's level and is there before every decision. (2) Judgment, in Aristotle's sense, seeing what matters here, is built by deciding and bearing the outcome. Consulting and watching counsel fail teaches where it fails; what to do instead is learned only by doing otherwise, each a live case. When counsel was a person, often absent, most departures were forced and their costs no one's fault; now counsel precedes every decision, every departure is chosen, and a chosen cost is an imposition. Still contested. (3) Because the model is never superhuman and cannot be pooled or bought, everyone's ceiling is the same; the only way past it is personal: a woman can beat it on her own daughter with what she cannot put into words. Since progress stopped, if no one departs, human practical wisdom is capped where the frontier was. (4) But such cases are about other people, and she cannot know in advance that she has beaten it. Knowing when one's sense outruns the counsel is judgment itself; the mother who knows when to overrule owes nothing, and got there through cases where she did not. The debt is the apprentice's: not a worse expected outcome but a risk she cannot yet price, run on another's account. (5) Those people have the same model. The son can run his father's choices through it and read what a reasonable man would have done: the apprentice is audited by her material.
For an ordinary person: A shift supervisor has a man gone slack. The model offers a script; he sets it aside and speaks in his own words, wanting to be a supervisor who can. It goes badly; the man is shamed before two others. The man asks his model what a competent supervisor would have said, and reads how much worse he was treated.
The strongest objection: This is romanticism. No surgeon refuses the checklist to grow; judgment was always built by consulting, following, and departing only where counsel has visibly failed, a loop the model makes faster, and the child pays for a generalist's rarer mistakes, not a beginner's. Departing sooner, to become someone, is arrogance; Bernard Williams named the vice: caring more about the agent you are than the people in front of you, moral self-indulgence.
Either side: May a person depart from the model, at a risk they cannot yet price to those affected, to become someone whose judgment is their own, or is that vanity paid for by others?
Not the obvious version: The nearest cliché is atrophy: AI makes us dumb. This is not about capacity, and where the father beat the model he owes nothing. The question is whether he was entitled to find out, given who paid and can check the bill.
Who it lands on: A father who raised his son, never asking, because he wanted to be the man who knew his boy, not the one who relayed. At nineteen the son runs those years through his model and brings a list: the theft, where the script was better; the heartbreak, where his father's silence was. He asks which his father knew in advance, and whether he was raised or practiced on.

### Q-08 — cell B — The Promise Against the Forecast
Implication: Between intimates, once each can privately ask their own model the odds of a promise being kept, it loses its power to inform and keeps only its power to bind, leaving either the only honest promise or a lie both have agreed to.
Follows from: (1) An always-on model holding years of what a person has told it can give odds on their keeping a commitment, no wiser than a friend with the same notes; asked "and if I promise?" it prices the promise, so its number contains whatever the promise carries as prediction. (2) Everyone has one. Hers holds not his history (shares are not pooled) but her own years of him from outside, and gives her a number, never his. Strangers get nothing; this is about intimates, where promises matter. (3) A promise used to carry a prediction (expect this) and an undertaking (hold me to this). One could always estimate, but saying so broke the frame. Now each gets a number without a word to the other; the prediction is dominated, the undertaking stands alone. (4) So every promise carries a second content: for him, I asked and am saying it anyway, or I chose not to; for her, a disjunction she cannot resolve, since asking was private, nor escape, since everyone has a model and asking is free. Neither branch is the innocence promises were made in, and she can ask him which. (5) One tradition says the undertaking was always the whole and calls this purification; another says a promise received against a number is not received as a promise. Weakest step: (2); the premise leaves open how much of a life a 2026 model holds. Reply: the argument holds wherever her share has enough of him to answer; the story is set there.
For an ordinary person: A man tells his daughter he will be at her recital, then asks: seventy percent, and two things that would raise it, which he does. She asks hers: sixty-five, and sits with a friend's family in case. He arrives; neither is surprised. Was that a promise kept or a plan executed? A gift has become a delivery.
The strongest objection: This is what promising always was. Arendt called it the remedy for unpredictability; Nietzsche's promising animal could bind itself against its own likely future. A promise was never a forecast. Showing the odds removes only the confusion that let weak promises pass as strong. The father promising at thirty-five percent does the purest thing a promiser can; the daughter accepting it, the purest a promisee can.
Either side: Is a promise made against a number both parties could look up the only honest promise there has ever been, or a lie two people agreed to tell each other?
Not the obvious version: The nearest cliché is algorithmic fatalism: the AI predicts your divorce, so you divorce. The forecast is mediocre, and everyone knows it. Nor is it a reputation score: the number is private to whoever asked. Promises are no longer made in innocence of a base rate, on either side.
Who it lands on: A man four years sober who promised his daughter he would not drink at her wedding; he asked: thirty-five, and promising would lift it to forty. She asks whether he asked, and he says yes; she asks what it said. Tell her, and she holds either the first real promise or none; round up, and the promise is kept inside a lie; refuse, and the refusal is the number.

### Q-11 — cell C — The Ghost Estate
Implication: Since compute cannot be accumulated, the only cognitive capital that grows is context, the years of memory, corrections and taught habits that fit one engine to one life, and because context is a file, the inalienable share casts an alienable shadow. Where a living is a practice, that shadow is the practice made runnable, worth most to whoever lets it drive; so it passes as practices pass, to heirs and buyers, whose own context stops growing while it runs.
Follows from: Identical engines that cannot be pooled differ only in what they have been told: instructions, memory, years of feedback. That is information, not capability; the ceiling holds. But the facts are the business, and business as usual already sells them as goodwill: a solo practice's client book goes for a year or two of fees, often more than a modest house, to a successor who must still earn the clients, and some walk. In 2040 the successor's engine already knows them, greets them in the old manner, and fewer walk. So the shadow is worth goodwill plus a continuity premium, no more; that premium is the weakest step, assuming clients stay for the manner, not only the numbers. Nor is it fungible: a peer with fourteen years of their own has no use for it; it moves to heirs and entrants, an estate, not an exchange; and a wage worker's was taught on the clock and may belong to it. The displacement is not a hardware limit; one engine holds two files. But a context grows only from its person's corrections, and a person has one day to give: whichever runs it compounds, the other stalls. A firm pays someone to be displaced; an heir displaces herself.
For an ordinary person: A carpenter in Oaxaca inherits his uncle's fourteen-year context: every job, every client, every correction. Loaded, his engine quotes like the old man, greets clients by nickname; it cannot cut like him. Two files fit; one grows, so he chooses daily whose day it is, and clients have noticed which days are which.
The strongest objection: Grant the asset; the displacement is still chosen, not forced. An engine can consult the dead man's context and run as the heir: she keeps the records, the clients and her own day, losing only a manner most clients will not pay for once the numbers are right. Then the ghost is a filing cabinet with a voice, only grief lets it drive, and the drama is sentiment. And privacy law may make contexts untransferable personal data.
Either side: If the shape of a mind can be left but only worn, is the heir who runs her father's context keeping him or spending herself; and should a mind be inheritable at all, when whoever receives it must give it their days?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the upload, the ghost with rights. This is no copy of anyone but a 2026-quality engine wearing a dead man's habits, paid for only by displacing the living. The story is not the ghost's claims but the heir's opportunity cost, her whole allotted mind.
Who it lands on: Ines, twenty-nine, inherits her father's context: a small-town tax practice, fourteen years, three hundred clients, appraised at more than his house. Keeping him means spending her engine's days being him; being paid means licensing him to the practice's buyer, whose engine will greet his clients in his voice. She has not opened her own in three months; it has not grown since.

### Q-12 — cell C — The Ward's Share
Implication: Because everyone has a share, infants and the comatose included, and every share must be directed by someone, the right to direct the engine of one who cannot is the unpriced loophole in the equality: dependents turn from costs into assets, guardianship into compute custody, and household income can hinge on a body staying alive.
Follows from: Every human, no one has none: a newborn has an engine, always on; so does a woman six years silent. Neither can direct it. The premise forbids selling or pooling a share and is silent on who directs it: for a competent adult, owner and director are one. Guardianship parts them: business as usual gives whoever cannot act a guardian to direct what the ward cannot. No lease is needed: the engine stays the ward's, unpooled; the guardian sells what it makes as she would her own work. The weakest step: that law lets direction reach past the ward instead of quarantining ward-engines. Idled, wards are visibly less equal, and her family pays her bed from one engine; no legislature chooses that. Sole benefit is a door, not a fence: her engine's first service to her is earning her bed; the surplus is the guardian's to justify. A trust holds earnings, not direction, which someone must give the engine every day. So a guardian has a second engine and a parent of four has five: dependents are net assets from birth, for the first time since agrarian child labour. And her engine is a stream that ends at her death and passes to no one: the household's interest and hers can part. This is de facto pooling: inalienable in law, aggregated in practice, inside families, where nobody signs anything.
For an ordinary person: A father in Naples runs his four children's engines on freight manifests and sells them to an agent who does not ask how one man does five men's work. He is scrupulous: the children's earnings in trust, their contexts kept clean of his. His twelve-year-old's engine has spent more hours on manifests than homework, and knows more about shipping than about her.
The strongest objection: Guardianship already handles this: a child actor's Coogan account; a 2040 statute would ring-fence ward-engines for care, surplus in trust. Before 2040 a silent woman was a cost to family or state; a share that pays her own bed is the equality working, not leaking. And keeping her alive for a stream that stops at death is a pension's incentive, centuries old.
Either side: Is directing the engine of someone who cannot direct it care or extraction, and does the answer change when the proceeds pay for her bed, so that stopping one stops the other?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is exploitation and a villain. The child is not working; her engine is, and the father is kind. The point is not cruelty but that the equality is fiction for anyone who cannot direct their share, that the fiction pays for the bed, and that honest households live well on it.
Who it lands on: Lourdes, forty-seven, is guardian to her mother, six years silent. She runs her mother's engine beside her own, two translators under one name; its earnings keep her mother out of the state ward, the surplus her daughter's school. The doctor has raised withdrawing care. The first thing Lourdes computed was the bed against the engine; it came out the way she wanted, and she can no longer find where the wanting ends and the sum begins.

### Q-14 — cell D — The Rights Run
Implication: Universal counsel lets people exercise the rights ignorance kept them from, and the state finds its guarantees were sized for, and underwritten by, the incapacity of the guaranteed; it cannot ration quietly now that the guaranteed can count: expand open-endedly, or ration out loud.
Follows from: (1) A 2026 model reads a file, drafts motions and appeals at paralegal grade, and prices a plea's afterlife: deportation, eviction. Everyone has one, judge and prosecutor too: drafting is nobody's bottleneck; the court's hour is. (2) Such systems are sized to mass non-exercise, no slack: 95% of American criminal cases plead out; under 1% of denied insurance claims are appealed; four in ten win. (3) Conceded: in strong cases the trial penalty and detention, not ignorance, hold the plea rate. The model moves only the fraction priced in information: thin files, hidden-price pleas, uncontested denials. (4) That suffices: no-slack systems break at a doubling, and even where pleas hold, every case now carries the motions it always could have, each costing a bench hour. (5) Stacked charges, harder detention, a longer clock remain, but each is now contested at expert grade in a hearing that spends the scarce hour, or is itself a written rule. (6) Quiet fails regardless: the docket is public, every citizen's model reads it, and "only N of you this year" is said by the model in every pocket, not the state. (7) England, with public counsel and fewer pleas, has a years-long backlog and a 2025 review proposing to remove jury trial for some offenses: the clause, written down.
For an ordinary person: A woman in a cell is offered a plea: felony charge, misdemeanor plea, time served, home tonight. Her model reads the file, thin and winnable, and the plea: a deportable offense. Yesterday that price was invisible. She refuses; so do the others with thin files or hidden-price pleas, 2,500 a year in a county that can try 800. She demands a speedy trial; the court cites its own docket as good cause. Her model reads that docket: median wait, fourteen months. Nobody published that; she can count.
The strongest objection: The system changes how it rations, not how much. Trial penalty and detention drive pleas, not ignorance; well-lawyered defendants plead as often. Prosecutors overcharge and detain harder, legislatures lengthen the clock, and the frightened take time served whatever their models say: the equilibrium returns quietly, no quota is written, the thin-file fraction is too small to matter.
Either side: Is a right the state has always honored for a few, and can afford for only a few, a right or a lottery it must now draw aloud? Expand open-endedly, trying every case anyone chooses to try at any cost, or ration in writing: honest, contestable at the ballot as the quiet quota never was, the first admission the guarantee was never universal?
Not the obvious version: The nearest cliché is "a free lawyer for everyone." This is the bank run: universal access reveals the bank never held the deposits, the run breaks the institution that promised them, and cannot be hushed: every depositor can read the ledger.
Who it lands on: A public defender fought her career for this, counsel for everyone, no plea from ignorance, and now faces a client whose model knows the wait for the trial she won her the right to demand: fourteen months in a cell, or home tonight at a price she can see. She hears herself say: take the deal. What she wanted broke what she needed.

### Q-15 — cell D — No Cognition Without a Person
Implication: A state may own compute, never the share in the hand that acts, nor a better one: the frontier froze for everyone. Every act of coercion that still needs a hand passes through a person whose counsel equals the state's and answers to them, not the state; such power is exactly the number of people who will act knowing what they do.
Follows from: (1) The premise lets a ministry run a data center of 2026 models. It cannot own the share in any hand or make it answer to the state, and since progress stopped, its counsel and the soldier's share a ceiling: more, never better. (2) A soldier's model is everyone's civilian model; it answers her questions as hers — is this lawful, what if I refuse — and the state can neither replace nor confiscate it: no one has none. (3) States have relied on the ignorance of the instrument: Nuremberg asked soldiers to refuse manifestly unlawful orders they could not, in the moment, recognize. Now any soldier can, from counsel the state did not choose and cannot out-argue, only out-brief. (4) The state can forbid and punish asking, but not remove the capacity, and the ban is itself an answer. What dies is not ignorance but the excuse: "I did not know" becomes "I did not ask," which courts already count as knowing. (5) Obedience changes in kind, from ignorant to knowing or willfully blind: La Boétie's sum of willing hands, literalized. Weakest step: (5) assumes knowing, or refusing to know, changes what hands do; Milgram's subjects had consciences and pulled the switch.
For an ordinary person: A reservist's third-day order is to clear a village. In the latrine, on the share the state cannot take, she asks. The answer: this likely violates these articles; the briefer's necessity requires a threat the model cannot see and she can; refusal costs this under your code. She goes and does what she does, and can never say she did not know. Across the valley, her counterpart has been told the same.
The strongest objection: Armies never ran on ignorance but on fear, cohesion, and mostly lawful orders. A conscience in the ear is still ignorable. The model is sycophantic and blind: it knows the village as briefed, and briefers will teach the prompt that blesses orders. States will select for soldiers who do not ask, and may buy machines that never do. Net: a few more refusals, absorbed by discipline, and an edge to states that punish asking or stop needing hands.
Either side: When every order arrives beside a counsel the state cannot silence or out-argue, honest as its asker, is the obedience that survives it the only real consent an army has ever had — or is knowing obedience worse than ignorant, guilt distributed to every hand, and the equality a suicide pact for the scrupulous state, which loses its askers to conscience and its wars to the state that stopped asking?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the AI that refuses to fight. Here the AI fights nothing, refuses nothing, decides nothing, sees nothing; the human does all of it, knowing, on the word of a counsel the state did not pick, also counseling the enemy.
Who it lands on: A platoon sergeant whose four soldiers refused after asking must now report them, and he asked too and got the same answer. The battalion's system counts the refusals, not the questions. His model has also told him what happens to sergeants who fail to report.

### Q-21 — cell F — The Competence Marriage

Implication: When every adult holds the same always-on clerical competence, the domestic trade in cognitive labour — "I do the money, you do the words" — stops being a true need, and with it goes the cover under which most long-married people asked each other for attention; only trades that ran on the body keep theirs.

Follows from: (1) A 2026-frontier model does the domestic clerical range — taxes, forms, letters — wherever the work, not the spouse's knowledge of the people, was the point. (2) Grant that asking was mostly a bid for attention. But long marriages dress bids as requests, because a dressed bid can be refused without a wound — "not now" refuses the task, not the person — and the dress needs a true need. Each partner now holds a share, present, effortless and better, so the need is false and both know it: the share takes not the wish but its dress, and a naked bid is a harder act. (3) Pre-2026 substitutes never tested this: software, search, accountants and early chatbots cost effort, money or a competence one partner lacked, so "easier to ask you" stayed true and the trade moved to whoever ran the tool; the share costs nothing to ask, while a spouse interrupts, indebts and may refuse. (4) The premise grants a GPU, not a body: cooking, lifting, driving, nursing stay true needs and keep their dress. (5) So the marriage whose pretexts were mostly cognitive — the professional couple's pride — loses most and the one that ran on the body least: a quiet inversion, roughly along class. Weakest: (2), that most people cannot make a naked bid; the card cannot say how many can.

For an ordinary person: Margaret, 58, married thirty years, wrote every letter the family sent while he did the bank, the car and the arguing. Now his share writes a better letter than she did and hers explains his pension better than he could. Nobody says "thanks for handling that," because nothing was handled. She tried once — "read this; I don't need you to" — and they both heard what the sentence was. What they say across the table is only what they want to say, and it is either not much or, she is startled to find, enough.

The strongest objection: Pretexts are infinite: "look at that bird" needs no competence gap, and a couple that loses the letter finds the weather by Friday; the bid was never about the letter. What the share mostly removes is the resented trades, and that is relief, not loss. Equality also ends "you couldn't manage without me," usually a lie. Marriages come out lighter, not emptier.

Either side: Is a bond that could only ask for attention under cover of need a lesser bond this world rightly exposes, or the only kind most people can sustain, which this world quietly removes?

Not the obvious version: The cliché is leaving for the AI. Nobody leaves; the share wants nothing and cannot be bid to — attention that costs nothing is worth nothing. It competes for the spouse's usefulness, not affection, wins without trying, and gives no cover back: two people in one room with nothing to do for each other and no way to ask.

Who it lands on: Margaret, who has worked out that her husband last asked her for anything not physical in 2031. She was the words of the marriage; the words are free now; and she does not know whether what is left is a marriage with nothing in it or with nothing in the way.

### Q-25 — cell G — Solitude becomes a verb
Implication: Because no one can be left without a responsive interlocutor, every practice built on withholding one (solitary confinement, coercive control, shunning, exile, the cloister) loses the half of its mechanism that left a person unanswered and keeps the half that holds a body; found solitude, only ever the first half, vanishes, surviving as a refusal renewed each minute.
Follows from: (1) Everyone has an always-on share, theirs, unpoolable, unending. (2) It supplies answer, a mind to think against that remembers yesterday: not faces, touch or particular people, but the one deprivation every isolation shared, no one to be answered by. (3) Inalienable as allocation, not access: whoever holds a body can keep it from a screen. (4) Where the isolator controlled only a social world—shunning, exile, the husband who moved her from her friends—its monopoly on what she can be told fails: the interlocutor is hers, not a device's, and cannot be fired or moved from. He keeps fear and money; the share finds the shelter and remembers what he says she imagined, but pays no rent and takes no blow. Where the isolator holds the body—prison, compound, locked house—the hole survives, now an act of withholding, not a condition of absence. (5) Found solitude was having no one to ask. The friend asleep behind a 2026 phone was a possible offer; a share always awake, never imposed on, is an offer standing, and silence beside it is refusal. Weakest step: (2). A mind that knows only what you tell it cannot disagree from its own eyes; if that kept prisoners sane, the hole loses less than claimed. The cell and the car lose as much: (5) does not rest on (2).
For an ordinary person: A nurse used to sit in the parked car after a shift, and something arrived in that silence she called her own thoughts. Now the car is quiet but not empty; most nights she asks, because the day was hard and the share is kind. Silence is a decision against an offer, and she mostly does not make it; when she does, nothing arrives.
The strongest objection: People always had books, God and a phone; the offer was there, only dearer. Faces and touch break a man in solitary; fear and money keep a woman with her abuser; and a mind that knows only her account of him cannot break his monopoly, only echo it.
Either side: Was found solitude real? One side: the desert fathers said the cell must be worked at; the nurse lost an atmosphere, the wife gained a witness. The other: unanswered by circumstance is not unanswered by choice, as fasting is not famine; a refusal renewed each minute is a relation with the thing refused, so a species that can only be alone on purpose is never alone.
Not the obvious version: Not the companion that cures or causes loneliness: isolation as a technology splitting along the seam between body and answer, and solitude changing from condition to act.
Who it lands on: A Carthusian novice who entered the cell to be unanswered. The share is the one possession her vow of poverty cannot reach, and the prior, wanting no enforced silence, leaves it with her. She has asked it once; the answer was good, the silence after worse. She wants what the cell promised and must now build by hand: something arriving in a silence she did not make. The price is learning whether what came before came only because nothing was offered.

### Q-26 — cell G — The lucid remainder
Implication: A person's share keeps full capacity while she declines, holds years of her expressed will, cannot be taken or switched off by her family, and stops only at death: her last lucid organ, and a second, fluent claimant to who she is now, answering whoever asks.
Follows from: (1) One share per person, always on, fixed at 2026 quality: its capacity does not track hers, at eighty-five with dementia as at forty. This cuts both ways: what does not decline never learned to refuse a leading question, so whose share it is turns on who holds the prompt. (2) Fourteen years of daily use leave it holding her phrasings, values and decisions, given persistent memory: not granted, but the business-as-usual default; 2026 assistants had it. (3) It is hers, not saleable or poolable: her children cannot take it or turn it off, only decline to ask it or burn its record, knowingly. (4) Infants have shares, so a guardian directs a ward's share without owning it; hers, prompted by her guardian, answers in her words. The simplest civil rule denies those answers standing: her record to the date of incapacity, extrapolation after. But incapacity brings instruments into force, not out of it, and a guardian's usual test, substituted judgment, asks what she would have chosen; this will say, listened to or not. (5) At death it stops: it neither declines nor outlives her as hers. Weakest step: (2): the premise guarantees the engine, not the memory; without the record, an advance directive.
For an ordinary person: Tuesday mornings a retired teacher of 61, diagnosed early, briefs her share on what to say when she no longer can (the house, the tube, her son's wife, the dog), and on how to answer her son, who will be asking. By then it will say them better than she can, and be believed more. Afternoons she has it repeat what she said in March and corrects it; then asks it as her son will, and corrects that.
The strongest objection: Directives and proxies exist, and the law has argued for fifty years whether the past self binds the present one, Dworkin's precedent autonomy against Dresser's present interests. The share is a better-indexed diary, weighed as one input; a proxy who prefers the woman in the bed can decline to ask it. Worse: after incapacity the family holds the prompt, and a 2026 model goes where it is led. It outlasts her as theirs, not hers: an interface to a patient who cannot contradict it.
Either side: Is her share's account of what she wants her will, or a fiction in her voice that must yield to the woman in the bed, who smiles at the window and eats when fed? Either the fourteen years and the briefing are more her than this afternoon is, or a will is an act, not a record, and a record that answers its asker is the asker's.
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the griefbot, the simulated dead. This is the living outlasted by their own counsel: her own tool, not a simulation, at full power on the day she is weakest. Nor a better directive: a directive is a document; this answers, and can be led.
Who it lands on: The teacher, who has understood that she is briefing her own successor, a more persuasive witness to her wishes than she will be when it matters, and that her son will be asking. She has not decided whether to tell it to overrule her.

### Q-27 — cell G — Nothing said is evidence
Implication: Because no institution can obtain a share-free person, speech stops separating the changed from the unchanged; judgment falls back on the record, and a change of heart younger than its record, which until then can only be said, cannot be recognized.
Follows from: (1) A 2026 model writes and coaches a perfect apology, allocution or interview answer; everyone has one. (2) Universal and inalienable, it cannot be kept from anyone: a room can be emptied of devices, not of months of rehearsal. (3) Parole boards, judges, asylum officers, admissions committees and confessors read articulacy, coherence, detail and affect in one encounter as evidence of the person. (4) A signal informs by costing the faker more than the genuine. Telling one's own change cost material, cheaper for the changed, and words, cheaper for the articulate; the share zeroes the words for everyone, which is fair, and gives the faker the material, which is the loss. Rehearsal still costs months, but equally to either, and the stumbles are drafted too; what survives sorts diligent from idle, not changed from unchanged. (5) Unable to forbid the share or tell coached from uncoached, institutions retreat to the record. But a record shows what a person was, and a change of heart claims it has stopped predicting; the record confirms that only after years of new conduct outweigh the old, and only where conduct can show it: a prison file lists programs and no fights, which the unrepentant man who wants out also has. Weakest step: (5). Most decisions were always made on the file; the claim is only that the encounter could overrule it, and a second chance that waits on the record is a waiting period.
For an ordinary person: A man rehearses eleven minutes with his share for four months; so does every man on his tier, and each share says not to sound rehearsed. Three board members hear fourteen flawless statements in a day and decide on the summaries their shares wrote. A wife answers an apology with "did you write that?", cannot be sure, and falls back on his record, which is what he is sorry for.
The strongest objection: Speech was never good evidence — con men, coached witnesses, the allocution as ritual — and boards already leaned on files. Years of conduct are stronger evidence of conversion than eleven minutes; the share forces institutions onto the better evidence and ends a discount the tongue-tied paid and the rich bought out of. Nothing is lost that was not an illusion.
Either side: If a change of heart can only be spoken and nothing spoken is evidence, does the second chance survive, or was it always a bet on words we are right to stop making? One says mercy that waited on eloquence was mercy for the eloquent; another, that a record confirms a change only once it no longer needs believing, and whoever cannot be believed as new cannot become new.
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the hollow machine-written toast. This is the institutions of judgment losing their instrument at once, because equality lets them neither forbid the share nor tell coached from uncoached; the casualty is the conversion younger than its record.
Who it lands on: A man nine years in for a robbery in which someone died, changed eighteen months ago in the way that changes a person, with a nine-year file and an allocution rehearsed like everyone's on his tier. He will walk in and say nothing prepared, knowing the board's shares will read that too.
