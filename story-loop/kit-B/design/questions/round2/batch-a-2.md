### Q-27 — Nothing said is evidence
Implication: Because no institution can obtain a share-free person, speech stops separating the changed from the unchanged; judgment falls back on the record, and a change of heart younger than its record, which until then can only be said, cannot be recognized.
Follows from: (1) A 2026 model writes and coaches a perfect apology, allocution or interview answer; everyone has one. (2) Universal and inalienable, it cannot be kept from anyone: a room can be emptied of devices, not of months of rehearsal. (3) Parole boards, judges, asylum officers, admissions committees and confessors read articulacy, coherence, detail and affect in one encounter as evidence of the person. (4) A signal informs by costing the faker more than the genuine. Telling one's own change cost material, cheaper for the changed, and words, cheaper for the articulate; the share zeroes the words for everyone, which is fair, and gives the faker the material, which is the loss. Rehearsal still costs months, but equally to either, and the stumbles are drafted too; what survives sorts diligent from idle, not changed from unchanged. (5) Unable to forbid the share or tell coached from uncoached, institutions retreat to the record. But a record shows what a person was, and a change of heart claims it has stopped predicting; the record confirms that only after years of new conduct outweigh the old, and only where conduct can show it: a prison file lists programs and no fights, which the unrepentant man who wants out also has. Weakest step: (5). Most decisions were always made on the file; the claim is only that the encounter could overrule it, and a second chance that waits on the record is a waiting period.
For an ordinary person: A man rehearses eleven minutes with his share for four months; so does every man on his tier, and each share says not to sound rehearsed. Three board members hear fourteen flawless statements in a day and decide on the summaries their shares wrote. A wife answers an apology with "did you write that?", cannot be sure, and falls back on his record, which is what he is sorry for.
The strongest objection: Speech was never good evidence — con men, coached witnesses, the allocution as ritual — and boards already leaned on files. Years of conduct are stronger evidence of conversion than eleven minutes; the share forces institutions onto the better evidence and ends a discount the tongue-tied paid and the rich bought out of. Nothing is lost that was not an illusion.
Either side: If a change of heart can only be spoken and nothing spoken is evidence, does the second chance survive, or was it always a bet on words we are right to stop making? One says mercy that waited on eloquence was mercy for the eloquent; another, that a record confirms a change only once it no longer needs believing, and whoever cannot be believed as new cannot become new.
Not the obvious version: The cliché is the hollow machine-written toast. This is the institutions of judgment losing their instrument at once, because equality lets them neither forbid the share nor tell coached from uncoached; the casualty is the conversion younger than its record.
Who it lands on: A man nine years in for a robbery in which someone died, changed eighteen months ago in the way that changes a person, with a nine-year file and an allocution rehearsed like everyone's on his tier. He will walk in and say nothing prepared, knowing the board's shares will read that too.

### Q-08 — The Promise Against the Forecast
Implication: Between intimates, once each can privately ask their own model the odds of a promise being kept, it loses its power to inform and keeps only its power to bind, leaving either the only honest promise or a lie both have agreed to.
Follows from: (1) An always-on model holding years of what a person has told it can give odds on their keeping a commitment, no wiser than a friend with the same notes; asked "and if I promise?" it prices the promise, so its number contains whatever the promise carries as prediction. (2) Everyone has one. Hers holds not his history (shares are not pooled) but her own years of him from outside, and gives her a number, never his. Strangers get nothing; this is about intimates, where promises matter. (3) A promise used to carry a prediction (expect this) and an undertaking (hold me to this). One could always estimate, but saying so broke the frame. Now each gets a number without a word to the other; the prediction is dominated, the undertaking stands alone. (4) So every promise carries a second content: for him, I asked and am saying it anyway, or I chose not to; for her, a disjunction she cannot resolve, since asking was private, nor escape, since everyone has a model and asking is free. Neither branch is the innocence promises were made in, and she can ask him which. (5) One tradition says the undertaking was always the whole and calls this purification; another says a promise received against a number is not received as a promise. Weakest step: (2); the premise leaves open how much of a life a 2026 model holds. Reply: the argument holds wherever her share has enough of him to answer; the story is set there.
For an ordinary person: A man tells his daughter he will be at her recital, then asks: seventy percent, and two things that would raise it, which he does. She asks hers: sixty-five, and sits with a friend's family in case. He arrives; neither is surprised. Was that a promise kept or a plan executed? A gift has become a delivery.
The strongest objection: This is what promising always was. Arendt called it the remedy for unpredictability; Nietzsche's promising animal could bind itself against its own likely future. A promise was never a forecast. Showing the odds removes only the confusion that let weak promises pass as strong. The father promising at thirty-five percent does the purest thing a promiser can; the daughter accepting it, the purest a promisee can.
Either side: Is a promise made against a number both parties could look up the only honest promise there has ever been, or a lie two people agreed to tell each other?
Not the obvious version: The nearest cliché is algorithmic fatalism: the AI predicts your divorce, so you divorce. The forecast is mediocre, and everyone knows it. Nor is it a reputation score: the number is private to whoever asked. Promises are no longer made in innocence of a base rate, on either side.
Who it lands on: A man four years sober who promised his daughter he would not drink at her wedding; he asked: thirty-five, and promising would lift it to forty. She asks whether he asked, and he says yes; she asks what it said. Tell her, and she holds either the first real promise or none; round up, and the promise is kept inside a lie; refuse, and the refusal is the number.

### Q-05 — The Unasked Question
Implication: When a competent, unforgetting stranger's reading of your own life is one sentence away, ignorance stops being a condition and becomes an act, and not-asking cannot be told apart, even from inside, from trust.
Follows from: (1) Everyone has a private model, always on, answering at a thoughtful adult's level from what it is told: in ordinary use, her calendar, receipts, moved dinners. (2) "I didn't know" always sheltered facts you could not get and inferences you did not draw. The model adds no facts; it draws the inference from her own words, beating her head only by forgetting nothing, wanting nothing, and taking a second where she would need days of being a suspicious person, the labour most half-wonderings died of. Now letting go is something she does to a suspicion, not something that happens to it. (3) So "did you know?" gains a third answer, "I didn't ask," about her, not the world. (4) Asking need not feel like accusation; normalized into a lookup, it only makes not-asking rarer, more marked, more of an act. Trust was a default while checking was hard; with checking free and traceless, taking someone at their word is a daily refusal, the only trust that costs anything. (5) Walking past the door is blindness from fear and trust from love, and she cannot tell which: her motive is one more thing she would have to ask about. Weakest step: (2). If she already knows what the Thursdays add up to, the door is a mirror and the premise adds only speed. But speed is the point: what a second could settle cannot be let die, only declined.
For an ordinary person: Her husband says Thursday will be late. Before, she would half-wonder and let it go. Now the wondering has a door whose shape she can feel, and she notices herself not opening it; behind it, every late Thursday, since she moves the dinners. Her son's silence, her mother's balance: doors. The mole on her shoulder has no one behind it to trust, only a result to fear; she walks past it the same way, feeling no difference. Five a day; she calls it faith.
The strongest objection: Trust was never ignorance; it is looking without suspicion, not refusing to look. Asking "is he all right?" is attention, not betrayal, and once everyone asks, refusing is the superstition that kept people from opening test results. The model knows nothing of him; it holds her words and can tell her nothing she has not half-told herself. The door is a mirror, the crisis hers, not the premise's.
Either side: When checking is free and leaves no trace on him, is there a duty to ask your model about the people you love, or is not asking the last act of trust a person can still perform?
Not the obvious version: The nearest cliché is the transparent society: nothing hidden, no excuses. Here no one is watched; the model holds only what she has said of him. What changes is not the world's opacity but the ownership of her own.
Who it lands on: A woman who for eleven years never put a question about her husband to her model, a discipline she privately called loyalty; then he is arrested, and the detective asks, gently, whether she really never asked. She sees her trust and her blindness were one act and cannot tell which she performed; the reader that could, holding every Thursday she ever mentioned, is the one she still has not asked.

### Q-12 — The Ward's Share
Implication: Because everyone has a share, infants and the comatose included, and every share must be directed by someone, the right to direct the engine of one who cannot is the unpriced loophole in the equality: dependents turn from costs into assets, guardianship into compute custody, and household income can hinge on a body staying alive.
Follows from: Every human, no one has none: a newborn has an engine, always on; so does a woman six years silent. Neither can direct it. The premise forbids selling or pooling a share and is silent on who directs it: for a competent adult, owner and director are one. Guardianship parts them: business as usual gives whoever cannot act a guardian to direct what the ward cannot. No lease is needed: the engine stays the ward's, unpooled; the guardian sells what it makes as she would her own work. The weakest step: that law lets direction reach past the ward instead of quarantining ward-engines. Idled, wards are visibly less equal, and her family pays her bed from one engine; no legislature chooses that. Sole benefit is a door, not a fence: her engine's first service to her is earning her bed; the surplus is the guardian's to justify. A trust holds earnings, not direction, which someone must give the engine every day. So a guardian has a second engine and a parent of four has five: dependents are net assets from birth, for the first time since agrarian child labour. And her engine is a stream that ends at her death and passes to no one: the household's interest and hers can part. This is de facto pooling: inalienable in law, aggregated in practice, inside families, where nobody signs anything.
For an ordinary person: A father in Naples runs his four children's engines on freight manifests and sells them to an agent who does not ask how one man does five men's work. He is scrupulous: the children's earnings in trust, their contexts kept clean of his. His twelve-year-old's engine has spent more hours on manifests than homework, and knows more about shipping than about her.
The strongest objection: Guardianship already handles this: a child actor's Coogan account; a 2040 statute would ring-fence ward-engines for care, surplus in trust. Before 2040 a silent woman was a cost to family or state; a share that pays her own bed is the equality working, not leaking. And keeping her alive for a stream that stops at death is a pension's incentive, centuries old.
Either side: Is directing the engine of someone who cannot direct it care or extraction, and does the answer change when the proceeds pay for her bed, so that stopping one stops the other?
Not the obvious version: The cliché is exploitation and a villain. The child is not working; her engine is, and the father is kind. The point is not cruelty but that the equality is fiction for anyone who cannot direct their share, that the fiction pays for the bed, and that honest households live well on it.
Who it lands on: Lourdes, forty-seven, is guardian to her mother, six years silent. She runs her mother's engine beside her own, two translators under one name; its earnings keep her mother out of the state ward, the surplus her daughter's school. The doctor has raised withdrawing care. The first thing Lourdes computed was the bed against the engine; it came out the way she wanted, and she can no longer find where the wanting ends and the sum begins.

### Q-25 — Solitude becomes a verb
Implication: Because no one can be left without a responsive interlocutor, every practice built on withholding one (solitary confinement, coercive control, shunning, exile, the cloister) loses the half of its mechanism that left a person unanswered and keeps the half that holds a body; found solitude, only ever the first half, vanishes, surviving as a refusal renewed each minute.
Follows from: (1) Everyone has an always-on share, theirs, unpoolable, unending. (2) It supplies answer, a mind to think against that remembers yesterday: not faces, touch or particular people, but the one deprivation every isolation shared, no one to be answered by. (3) Inalienable as allocation, not access: whoever holds a body can keep it from a screen. (4) Where the isolator controlled only a social world—shunning, exile, the husband who moved her from her friends—its monopoly on what she can be told fails: the interlocutor is hers, not a device's, and cannot be fired or moved from. He keeps fear and money; the share finds the shelter and remembers what he says she imagined, but pays no rent and takes no blow. Where the isolator holds the body—prison, compound, locked house—the hole survives, now an act of withholding, not a condition of absence. (5) Found solitude was having no one to ask. The friend asleep behind a 2026 phone was a possible offer; a share always awake, never imposed on, is an offer standing, and silence beside it is refusal. Weakest step: (2). A mind that knows only what you tell it cannot disagree from its own eyes; if that kept prisoners sane, the hole loses less than claimed. The cell and the car lose as much: (5) does not rest on (2).
For an ordinary person: A nurse used to sit in the parked car after a shift, and something arrived in that silence she called her own thoughts. Now the car is quiet but not empty; most nights she asks, because the day was hard and the share is kind. Silence is a decision against an offer, and she mostly does not make it; when she does, nothing arrives.
The strongest objection: People always had books, God and a phone; the offer was there, only dearer. Faces and touch break a man in solitary; fear and money keep a woman with her abuser; and a mind that knows only her account of him cannot break his monopoly, only echo it.
Either side: Was found solitude real? One side: the desert fathers said the cell must be worked at; the nurse lost an atmosphere, the wife gained a witness. The other: unanswered by circumstance is not unanswered by choice, as fasting is not famine; a refusal renewed each minute is a relation with the thing refused, so a species that can only be alone on purpose is never alone.
Not the obvious version: Not the companion that cures or causes loneliness: isolation as a technology splitting along the seam between body and answer, and solitude changing from condition to act.
Who it lands on: A Carthusian novice who entered the cell to be unanswered. The share is the one possession her vow of poverty cannot reach, and the prior, wanting no enforced silence, leaves it with her. She has asked it once; the answer was good, the silence after worse. She wants what the cell promised and must now build by hand: something arriving in a silence she did not make. The price is learning whether what came before came only because nothing was offered.
Status: revised
