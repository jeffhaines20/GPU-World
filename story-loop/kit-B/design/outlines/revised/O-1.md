### O-1 — He Has It With Him
Conceit: For seventeen years a 911 dispatcher never asked his model about his son. He kept instead a notebook a year of bets on him, each with a confidence, each scored. On a trainee's last supervised night, the trainee, who at ten took the blame for the son's false alarm, asks whether he knew. The father answers with the notebook. The boy answers with the one night it scored right, because he was there.
Who: Wade Hautala, 48, night training officer at St. Louis County 911 in Duluth, March 2040. At the console he reads the model's protocols to strangers word for word; about Owen, now 19, he never asked it once. When J. Tolliver appeared on the trainee roster, Wade asked for him. He wants to know what "Cost: not mine" cost, and pays with the only proof that he knew.
Obstacle: Only the one it charged can score that line. Jesse has the model Wade refused, has watched him relay it flawlessly for a month, and will not be scored. Owen is not in the building. At seven Jesse goes solo on Wade's signature.
Turn: Wade's best call, 11 March 2038: "Skyline lookout. Home by two if nobody comes after him. He has it with him. Conf 80." Scored "Right, late." Jesse was there: the north pier, Owen confessing the alarm after reading his father's entry on it, then forty minutes out on the shelf ice while his model asked him six times to call his father and Jesse's asked for 911. Neither called: Owen said his father would have it written down. What Owen had with him was the Tolliver boy, and the confession Wade bet on in 2031 came seven years late, because his son read the bet.
Last image: 06:52. Wade's headset hangs from his chair. On the canal monitor the north pier comes up gray, the light at the end still burning, nobody on it. Behind him a line rings, and Jesse, the model in his ear and no one on the second jack, answers a stranger.
Question: Wade priced every risk he ran on his son, and his numbers got better; none of them counted the boy who paid for the alarm and then stood at the rail while Wade chose not to call. Was he entitled to learn his son this way, and to let Jesse learn now on a stranger, or was every lesson drawn on someone else's account?
What the reader assumed and no longer does: That departing from the model is self-reliance. Here each departure leaned on someone: the father on the model in his son's pocket, the son on his father's ledger, the stranger on the son's faith in it; and the only one watching was the boy the father wrote off.
Why it cannot be predicted, and why it is earned: The card promises a son auditing his father. Here the auditor is the stranger who paid, and he finds not a bigger bill but a second, unrecorded charge and a confession the bet itself produced. Earned: beat 5 recites "He has it with him"; "two years ago" in beat 2 dates the pier; the model's even-handed audit shows what only a witness could add.
Not the obvious version: Not the wronged boy demanding an apology. Jesse asks for none, the model clears everyone, the father wants the number more than forgiveness, and the payer departed too, on the father's son, and would again. Nobody learns to ask.
Closest published: Andre Dubus, "A Father's Story" (1983): a father's protection of his child is paid for by another family's son. There the father hides it and answers to God; here the other son holds the proof, has the counsel the father refused, and was his unwitting insurance. The trainer-apprentice frame nears the Dardennes' The Son (2002), where the trainer is the injured one and the boy does not know.
Beats:
1. 23:40. On the second jack Wade hears his trainee, Jesse Tolliver, 19, read a choking protocol to a stranger word for word; a toddler breathes. In Wade's bag is OWEN 2031, the first notebook to leave the house. Change: the best relay in the building is training the boy he asked for.
2. 01:15, the lull. Jesse asks what he asked his first night: did you know? (The rink alarm, 2031: Jesse, seen by the pull station, suspended for the season. Owen told him two years ago.) Wade opens the book: "Script: tell the rink Monday. Not telling. He'll own it by Sunday. Conf 80." Then: "Wrong. Tolliver boy suspended. Cost: not mine." Change: the payer holds the only proof, and it calls him not mine.
3. Jesse reads on unasked: the same bet renewed through the year on falling numbers, Easter 60, fall 40, "before he's grown" 30. Change: the bill has dates, and the bet kept running on his account.
4. Between clean calls Jesse murmurs the entry to his own model, which answers fluently: thin evidence, a coach's haste, a child's silence, a wait "defensible though not generally advised." "It says nobody." Change: the payer audits with the instrument Wade refused and finds the bill charged to no one.
5. 03:40. Jesse asks for 11 March 2038. Wade recites it without the book, down to "Right, late." Change: the father offers his best call as his defense.
6. The turn (the passage). Change: Wade learns what his son had with him.
7. 05:30. Wade asks what the season cost. "You'd write it down." Would Jesse call from the rail now? "No." Change: the payer is a departer too, and will not be an outcome.
8. 06:50. The center's model has drafted Jesse's release. Before the next line lights, Wade pulls his jack from Jesse's console and walks to the canal monitor. Change: Wade places the old bet on the stranger's boy, with a stranger paying, knowing now what that insurance is worth.
The passage:
Jesse kept his hand flat on the closed book. "Skyline," he said.

"He'd been up there twice that winter. Fourteenth of January. Second of February." Wade heard himself giving a trainee dates at a quarter to four, and stopped.

Jesse reached across and put the canal camera on his second screen, which trainees were told not to do without a reason. The north pier came up gray and grainy: the glazed walk, the rail, the light at the end, and past it the shelf ice heaped out into the dark.

"There," Jesse said. "By the light. Him and me, eleven till ten to two."

Wade kept both hands on the desk.

"He'd found your books. He'd read the one about me. He told me about the alarm, and then he went down the ladder at the end and walked out on that." Jesse touched the screen, the gray past the light. "Forty minutes. I could see his phone the whole time. It kept lighting up. He showed me after: it asked him to call you six times."

"And yours."

"Mine wanted 911." Jesse tipped his head at the room around them. "Here. Every few minutes."

"Why didn't you?"

"He said you knew where he was. He said you'd have it written down already." Jesse took his hand off the book. "I figured a father who knew where his kid was and didn't come must know it was fine."

Wade had watched that pier from this chair on a hundred March nights, and not on that one.

"'He has it with him,'" Jesse said.

"I meant the model."

"I know what you meant."

## What changed
Both reviewers were right: this was the card's own scene plus a ledger, recited through the middle, with the best material buried, and (A) learning to ask read as a lesson. Rebuilt per F1: the auditor is the Tolliver boy, now Wade's trainee, on a night shift. No child scores the books, no one leaves by water, and there is no kitchen table and no pen. "He has it with him" is the turn: the boy Wade had charged was what insured his best call, and the confession Wade bet on came because his son read the bet. Nobody learns to ask. The middle is live calls, and the ending places a new bet instead of withholding a verdict. Cut: the told turn, the furnace, the uncapped pen, "raised or practiced on," and the theft (now a false alarm). Refused: A's charge that "Conf 80" reduces judgment to forecasting; the numbers are what let those who paid audit him. It survives, stranger rather than safer.
